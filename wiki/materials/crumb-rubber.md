# Crumb / Powder Rubber (from waste tires)

## Key points
- In R-HSHDC, powder rubber replaces quartz powder **by volume** at 0 / 10 / 20 / 35%. Particle
  size is about 30–300 µm ⚠️ (read from the Fig. 2 PSD). Above 40% the mix is unworkable
  ([[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]], p. 3).
- **Effects** (pp. 4–9):

  | Property | Change with rubber |
  |---|---|
  | Matrix Km | lower: 0.787 → 0.740 at 20%, → 0.671 MPa√m at 35% |
  | First-crack stress | lower |
  | f'c | down by up to 33.2% |
  | E | down by up to 26.7% |
  | Crack width | lower |
  | Crack number | higher |
  | ε_tu | **higher, up to 5.7%** (PE18-PR20) |

- At 35% rubber, ductility falls because fibers agglomerate. → [[fiber-dispersion]]
- **Mechanisms given by the authors:**
  - The rubber particles act as weak spots that lower Km.
  - Air bubbles form around the non-polar rubber.
  - The rubber–matrix interface is weak (SEM shows a gap).
  - Rubber particles may bridge cracks.
- **Prior work cited:** 100% CR replacement raised ECC ductility by 110.2% (Adesina & Das 2021,
  as cited p. 2).


## Coarser crumb rubber in normal PVA-ECC (2026-10-20, round 4 ingest)
- [[mohammed2020-cr-ecc-elevated-temperature]] ("CR-ECC", oiled PVA, FA/C 1.20): CR 0.6–2.36 mm from waste tyres
  replaced 2.5 / 5% of river sand **by mass**. Only compression, acid and sulphate tests were done, so tensile strain
  hardening is **not shown**.

  | Property | Effect of CR | Location |
  |---|---|---|
  | 28 d f'c (RSM, 30–75 MPa range) | CR main term not significant (p = 0.48); PVA, PVA² and CR × PVA (p = 0.0356) significant; optimum 2.5% CR + 2% PVA | Table 4, p. 12; Fig. 2 |
  | Residual f'c after heating | 24 °C ≈ 75 / 58 / 46 MPa for 0 / 2.5 / 5% CR; CR mixes lose ≈ 10–17% from 24 to 100 °C (2.5% CR: ≈ 58 → 47.66 → 39.81 → ≈ 13 MPa at 24 / 100 / 500 / 1000 °C); 0% CR ≈ 16 MPa at 1000 °C vs ≈ 13 / 11 for the CR mixes; curves converge above ≈ 500 °C (≈ 21–25 MPa at 800 °C), so the CR penalty largely disappears | §3.2, p. 7; Fig. 3, p. 7 (read-off) |
  | Visible cracking at 900 °C | less with CR | §3.3, p. 8; Figs. 5–6, p. 9 |
  | Spalling | none up to 1000 °C, with or without CR | §3.3 |
  | Sulphate length change | lower with CR | Fig. 10 |
  | Acid (5% H₂SO₄) weight change, 28 d | authors claim a slight gain, but Fig. 8 shows losses of 0.2–7.3% in 12 of 13 mixes; only M4 (5% CR, 2% PVA) gained (≈ 0.15%) ⚠️ | Fig. 8, p. 10 |

  - The authors state CR melts at 170 °C and leaves vapour escape paths (uncited ⚠️, §3.3). → [[high-temperature-behaviour]]

> Synthesis: both rubber sources report lower f'c (powder rubber −33% max in [[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]]; coarser CR
> lower residual f'c up to ≈ 500 °C in [[mohammed2020-cr-ecc-elevated-temperature]], converging at higher temperature). Only Su 2023 measures tension, so the claim that
> rubber raises ε rests on one HSHDC study. Particle size (30–300 µm vs 0.6–2.36 mm) and the replaced phase (quartz
> powder by volume vs sand by mass) differ. Rubber is one form of alternative aggregate → [[recycled-fine-aggregate]].
