# UHMWPE (PE) Fiber

## Definition
A high-strength, high-modulus synthetic fiber. It is hydrophobic, so it has no chemical bond with
the matrix ([[ranade2015-hshdc-tensile-rate-effects]], p. 95; [[yang2024-hshdc-uniaxial-tension]], p. 5;
[[he2024-hdc-research-advances]], p. 3).

## Key points
- PE is superior to PVA in a high-strength matrix. PE-HSHDC reaches εp up to 6.48%, while
  PVA-HSHDC reaches 0.23–0.30% ([[yang2024-hshdc-uniaxial-tension]], Table 6). → [[pva-fiber]]
- **Rate sensitivity:** PE is viscoelastic. From 0.009 to 900 mm/s its in-situ strength rises
  about 21% and its modulus about 85%. These are the dominant rate effects in HSHDC
  ([[ranade2015-hshdc-tensile-rate-effects]], Fig. 10). → [[strain-rate-effects]]
- **Low melting point** (about 124–150 °C) is a concern for fire and blast. A study is ongoing
  ([[ranade2017-hshdc-impact-resistance]], p. 31). → [[high-temperature-behaviour]], [[gaps]]

## Further data (2026-10-16 ingest)
- Five PE products are listed: d 10–38 µm, L 6.4–18 mm, E 73–120 GPa, σ_f 2700–3800 MPa. PE needs no
  surface treatment but gives wider cracks than PVA ([[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]], Table 2, p. 347).
- In HSHDCC, PE is "mandatory". Typical L/d is 300–900 at 2 vol.%, with σ_f 2500–3800 MPa and E 80–120 GPa
  ([[zhang2022-hshdcc-review]], Table 3, p. 16). → [[fiber-surface-treatment]]
- PE-ECC can reach up to 11.98% strain at f'c 45.86 MPa ⚠️ (as cited in [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]], p. 1).
- PE-ECC loses strain hardening between 100 and 140 °C (Luo 2019, as cited) → [[high-temperature-behaviour]]
- Under impact, PE-ECC is less rate-sensitive than PVA-ECC
  ([[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]], p. 349–350). → [[strain-rate-effects]]
- Spectra PE at only 0.6 vol.% in high-strength concrete failed without multiple cracking
  ([[li1992-steady-state-multiple-cracking]], Table 2).

### Behaviour
- **PE in normal-strength matrices.** PE-ECC with 60% SCM (f'c about 41–55 MPa) strain-hardens with ε about
  2.0–3.5% at about 1.55 vol.% ([[shumuye2022-slag-fa-pe-ecc]], Sect. 3.2). Printed PE-SHCC reached 2–3% strain at
  σ_tu 4.1–5.4 MPa ([[ivaniuk2022-printable-shcc-design]], Fig. 17). PE-SHCC with SF at W/B 0.22: 1.5–2.2% at
  7–8 MPa (as cited in [[xue2023-shcc-structural-review]], Fig. 1).
- **Short PE.** 6 mm × 12 µm PE at 1.5 vol.% gave **strain softening** in a W/B 0.45 matrix
  ([[homma2009-frcc-self-healing]], p. 224). The same fiber size at the same Vf strain-hardened in Nagoya UHP-SHCC
  (W/B 0.20, f'c 95 MPa) dumbbells ([[kunieda2010-uhp-shcc-steel-reinforcement]], §3.2) and in printed SHCC
  ([[ivaniuk2022-printable-shcc-design]]). → [[fiber-geometry]]
- **Casting direction.** In the JCI round robin, PE reversed the PVA trend: vertically cast PE dogbones gave more strain
  than horizontal ones (T2-V 0.253% vs T2-H 0.018%) ([[kanakubo2006-dfrcc-tensile-test-method]], Table 7, p. 8–9). → [[fiber-dispersion]]
- **Self-healing.** Many fine PE fibers act as CaCO₃ nucleation sites and promote healing of cracks up to and beyond
  100 µm ([[homma2009-frcc-self-healing]], p. 220, Conclusion 2). → [[self-healing]]
- **Fatigue.** Under high-stress flexural fatigue, pulled-out PE fibers flatten and are scratched; ruptured fibers show
  tearing ([[sui2018-uhp-ecc-flexural-fatigue]], Figs. 11–12). → [[fatigue]]
- **Slag and bond.** SEM shows more hydration products on PE in slag mixes; the authors claim a stronger bond (not
  measured) ([[shumuye2022-slag-fa-pe-ecc]], Sect. 3.5.1). → [[ggbfs]]
- **PE in a cementless alkali-activated slag matrix** (no aggregate, W/B 0.26–0.38): ε 4.58–7.50% and σ_tu 5.06–13.06 MPa
  at f'c 36.3–54.8 MPa, 28 d ([[choi2016-pe-alkali-activated-slag-composite]], Tables 5, 7). PE was chosen because it is
  hydrophobic and stronger than PVA; the authors contrast ≤ 4.5% for PVA in alkali-activated slag (as cited, §1).
  → [[alkali-activated-binder]], [[uhdc]]
- **PE in hybrids with PVA**: PE multifilament is hard to disperse, raises air content (13.1 vs 10.4%) and lowers f'c,
  but gives more fine cracks before F-T ([[yun2013-shcc-freeze-thaw]], Table 3, Fig. 11, p. 13–14). → [[hybrid-fiber]], [[freeze-thaw]]
- PE is non-conductive; in the Korea Univ. hybrid, EMI shielding comes from the steel fibers ([[lee2021-hsdc-wall-emi-impact]], §3.1). → [[hybrid-fiber]]
