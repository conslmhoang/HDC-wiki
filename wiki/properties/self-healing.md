# Self-Healing (autogenous crack healing)

> All data are second-hand, via the review [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]].

## Key points (as cited in [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]])
- **Crack width controls healing speed.** Permeability stabilises within 3–4 d for cracks
  < 60 µm, but needs 7–10 d or more for cracks > 100 µm (p. 3). → [[crack-width]]
- **Wet–dry cycles:** relative water permeability fell to 0 after 10 cycles (Ma 2014, p. 3).
- **Freeze–thaw with pre-strain** (Zhu 2012, p. 5):

  | Pre-strain | Recovery |
  |---|---|
  | 0.5–1% | about 100% |
  | 1.5–2% | limited |
  | > 2.5% | not obvious |

- A 2% pre-strained ECC recovered its stiffness fully after 50 salt-frost cycles (Sahmaran & Li 2007, p. 5).
- Healing is faster in sulfate solution (Liu 2017, p. 5).
- Healing of low pre-damage improves later fatigue life (Qiu 2018, p. 9). → [[fatigue]]
- **Ageing reduces healing.** Self-healing and ductility both decline with age because of the
  long-term pozzolanic reaction (p. 9).

> Synthesis: tight cracks (< 60 µm) are what make ECC self-healing, and PVA-ECC achieves them
> (43–88 µm, [[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]]). PE-based HSHDC (80–200 µm) sits mostly
> above that range, so its healing ability is uncertain. → [[gaps]]
