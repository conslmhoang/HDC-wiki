## Thresholds (all as cited)
| Threshold | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| < 100 µm | ECC design target; little effect on permeability | [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]], p. 2–3 |
| < 60 µm | fast self-healing (3–4 d) | [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]], p. 3 |
| 55–80 µm | chloride ingress becomes significant (Jang 2011) | [[he2024-hdc-research-advances]], p. 10 |
| ≥ 90 µm | chloride content rises (Wu 2017) | same |

These measure different things, so they are not a strict conflict.

## Influencing factors
- **Interface:** oiling PVA lowers bridging stiffness, so cracks get wider (43 → 88 µm) while
  ε_tu rises ([[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]], p. 471). → [[fiber-surface-treatment]]
- **No chemical bond** (PE) gives wider cracks than PVA ([[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]], p. 347).
- A higher fiber modulus, higher strain rate, rubber, longer fiber, more SF or a higher Vf·Lf/df all
  narrow the cracks. → [[strain-rate-effects]], [[crumb-rubber]], [[fiber-geometry]]

> Synthesis: there are two crack-width "families".
> - **PVA-ECC: about 40–90 µm**, below the durability thresholds, so it can self-heal.
> - **PE-based HSHDC: about 80–200 µm**, mostly above the thresholds.
>
> High strength and high ductility from PE therefore come at the cost of crack-width-controlled
> durability. The ingested sources do not fill this gap. → [[gaps]], [[self-healing]]
