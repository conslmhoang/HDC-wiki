# Crack Width

**Always state** (1) average or maximum width, (2) **residual (unloaded) or under load**, (3) the
imposed strain or deflection, (4) the measuring method. Almost every value and threshold in the
literature is a residual **average** width; see "Average vs maximum" and "Loaded vs unloaded" below.

## Average vs maximum crack width
- Under monotonic and cyclic tension the **average** width of PVA-SHCC stays at about 50–65 µm up to 3% strain,
  but the **maximum** width grows from about 140 µm to **290–420 µm** ([[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]], Fig. 4b ⚠️ read).
- Maximum width rises with strain while the average stays constant; crack width is a statistical
  distribution caused by non-uniform fiber dispersion (p. 262–263). The authors say design must use the
  distribution and the probability of wide cracks, not the average (p. 269). → [[fiber-dispersion]]
- R/SHCC beams: average 22–38 µm but maximum 50–120 µm ([[paul2014-r-shcc-chloride-corrosion]], Table 3).

## Loaded vs unloaded (residual)
- Under a sustained flexural load the average width is about **2×** the unloaded width
  ([[ozbay2013-ecc-self-healing-sustained-load]], Table 3): ECC-1 115 vs 60 µm (ranges 44–165 vs 27–110);
  ECC-2 85 vs 40 µm (30–103 vs 15–70).
- Widths under load are about 15% larger than residual (Yang 2009, as cited in
  [[herbert2013-ecc-self-healing-natural-environment]], §2.1). **Now confirmed at primary level:** "crack closure of
  about 15%" on unloading after tensile pre-loading ([[yang2009-ecc-self-healing-wet-dry]], p. 385).
- Unloading closes ECC cracks by about **10% (5–10 µm)** after tension ([[lepech2009-ecc-water-permeability]], p. 749).
- Paul et al. note widths fell "significantly" on unloading ([[paul2014-r-shcc-chloride-corrosion]], p. 345).
- Sustained load at 80% σ_tu gives fewer, wider cracks than monotonic loading (Boshoff 2007, as cited in
  [[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]], p. 263).
- ⚔️ Özbay's ratio (about 2×, sustained flexural load) differs from the two primary tension results (≈ 15% closure,
  Yang 2009; ≈ 10%, Lepech 2009). Loading mode (tension vs flexure) and duration (sustained vs short-term) differ.

## Field data
- ASR-damaged retaining wall (Gifu), sprayed HPFRCC 50–70 mm: uncracked to 7 months, cracks ≤ 50 µm at
  10 months and ≤ 120 µm at 24 months; repair mortar 30 / 200 / 300 µm at 3 / 10 / 24 months
  (Rokugo 2005, as cited in [[kunieda2006-hpfrcc-japan-applications]], p. 29, Table 3, Fig. 16).
- Railway viaduct lab RC beams with 10 mm sprayed HPFRCC: 130 vs 250 µm after 17 million cycles (same source, p. 30).
- ECC coupling-beam dampers: < 300 µm after cyclic shear tests (same source, p. 26).

### Chloride: the threshold depends on the exposure ⚔️
- **Immersion / diffusion** (120 d in 3.5% NaCl): 100 µm notches gave chloride profiles not significantly
  different from uncracked ECC; 200 / 300 µm raised surface free chloride by +8.7 / +14.9% at 30 d and
  +17.4 / +29.4% at 120 d ([[bu2022-ecc-chloride-crack-width]], Sect. 3). Caveat: single smooth cast notches
  without fiber bridging, not multiple mechanical cracks (Sect. 2.2). As cited there: no D_eff difference for
  10–50 µm cracks (Şahmaran & Li 2009); total, not maximum, crack width governs (concrete papers).
- **Wet–dry capillary suction** (R/SHCC, w/c 1.0 matrix): 50–100 µm cracks let chloride reach 65–89 mm within
  1–6 h vs 4.5–8 mm uncracked, and rebar chloride and corroded depth showed **no correlation** with crack width
  or crack number ([[paul2014-r-shcc-chloride-corrosion]], Table 5, Fig. 10, p. 350).
- The tolerable crack width for a given aggressive environment "is yet to be determined"
  ([[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]], p. 264).
- Neutron radiography shows sub-100 µm cracks fill with water within minutes, so "< 100 µm has little
  effect" refers to flow permeability, not capillary uptake ([[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]]).
  → [[chloride-and-corrosion]], [[permeability]]

> Synthesis: there is no single chloride crack-width threshold. Under immersion, cracks up to about
> 100 µm behave almost like uncracked ECC (Bu 2022); under wet–dry cycling, even cracks of 22–38 µm average
> did not stop chloride reaching the steel (Paul 2014). The often-quoted "55–80 µm" (Jang 2011) is second-hand
> and sits between these primary results; it should not be used as a design value without its exposure
> conditions. All these thresholds are residual widths, while service cracks under load may be about 2× wider.

## Influencing factors
- **Interface:** oiling PVA lowers bridging stiffness, so cracks get wider (43 → 88 µm) while
  ε_tu rises ([[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]], p. 471). → [[fiber-surface-treatment]]
- **No chemical bond** (PE) gives wider cracks than PVA ([[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]], p. 347).
- A higher fiber modulus, higher strain rate, rubber, longer fiber, more SF or a higher Vf·Lf/df all
  narrow the cracks. → [[strain-rate-effects]], [[crumb-rubber]], [[fiber-geometry]]
- **Fly ash:** higher FA/C gives more and narrower cracks (43–50 vs 30–39 cracks)
  ([[ozbay2013-ecc-self-healing-sustained-load]], Table 3, p. 171); FA/C 2.2 reaches 24–30 µm
  ([[herbert2013-ecc-self-healing-natural-environment]], §2.1). → [[fly-ash]]
- **Slag** widens cracks in PE-ECC ([[shumuye2022-slag-fa-pe-ecc]], Sect. 3.4). → [[ggbfs]]
- **Freeze–thaw + carbonation** widen existing 20–28 µm cracks slightly (to 28–30 µm), and the cracks
  accelerate carbonation (+5–6 mm front) ([[chai2019-hdcc-freeze-thaw-carbonation-flexure]], Fig. 2, 4b).
  → [[freeze-thaw]], [[carbonation]]
- Water flow through a crack ∝ COD³ (Tsukamoto 1990, as cited in [[kanda2003-sprayed-ecc]], p. 279; also
  [[li2003-ecc-review]], p. 225).
- Corrosion in fine-cracked HPFRCC is micro-cell rather than macro-cell (Hiraishi 2005, as cited in
  [[kunieda2006-hpfrcc-japan-applications]], p. 24); fine cracks also complicate visual inspection (same, p. 24).
- **Matrix W/B (cementless AAS-PE):** lower W/B → more cracks and smaller computed width (88.9 µm at W/B 0.26)
  ([[choi2016-pe-alkali-activated-slag-composite]], Table 8). Slag-based PE composite (≈ 100 µm) sits between PVA-ECC
  and PE-HSHDC values. → [[alkali-activated-binder]]
- **Temperature (hot tests):** PVA-ECC bending cracks widen to hundreds of µm at 60–100 °C and a single crack forms
  from 150 °C; steel fibres in HECC limit width ≈ 5× at 60–100 °C ([[pourfalah2018-ecc-high-temperature]], §4.3,
  Fig. 10d–e, Conclusions). → [[high-temperature-behaviour]]
- **Typical development:** ECC reaches ≈ 60 µm at ≈ 1% strain, then new cracks form instead of widening
  ([[lepech2009-ecc-water-permeability]], p. 745, Fig. 1). → [[steady-state-cracking]]
- **Fatigue:** the S–N inflection of earlier ECC models coincides with the inflection of the crack-width vs
  strain curve, suggesting a critical crack width ([[sui2018-uhp-ecc-flexural-fatigue]], Fig. 20). → [[fatigue]]

> Synthesis (revised 2026-10-20): there are two crack-width "families" of **average residual** width.
> - **PVA-ECC: about 22–90 µm** (HVFA mixes 22–40 µm: Herbert, Paul, Özbay; standard mixes 40–90 µm).
> - **PE-based HSHDC / UHP-ECC: about 80–200 µm** (Sui 2018: < 160 µm).
>
> Three refinements weaken the simple message "PVA-ECC stays below the thresholds": maximum widths in PVA-SHCC
> reach 290–420 µm at 3% strain (van Zijl); widths under sustained load are about 2× residual (Özbay); and under
> wet–dry exposure no safe width was found (Paul). Conversely, under immersion the chloride threshold is
> 100–200 µm (Bu), so PE-HSHDC widths of 80–130 µm may be acceptable in submerged exposure. → [[gaps]], [[self-healing]]

- **Standard bar**: JC/T 2461-2018 allows an average crack width up to **200 µm** for HDC (as cited in
  [[chai2023-hdc-ambient-temperature]]; the same 0.5% / 200 µm pair is attributed to JSCE 2008 in
  [[chai2019-hdcc-freeze-thaw-carbonation-flexure]]), twice the 100 µm ECC design target. → [[terminology-hdc-ecc-shcc]]
- Wording of the ECC bound varies: "generally not exceeding 80 µm" ([[bu2022-ecc-chloride-crack-width]], Sect. 1),
  "less than 80 µm" (Li 2003, as cited in [[qian2007-shcc-inverse-tensile-strain]], p. 235), "< 60 µm" at 3–5%
  strain (as cited in [[herbert2013-ecc-self-healing-natural-environment]], §1), "< 100 µm" ([[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]]).
  Different definitions, not a real conflict.

## Conflicts & open questions
- ⚔️ Chloride threshold: 55–80 µm (Jang 2011, second-hand) vs 100–200 µm under immersion (Bu 2022) vs no safe
  width under wet–dry (Paul 2014). → [[gaps]]
- ⚔️ Loaded/unloaded ratio: about 2× (Özbay 2013, sustained flexure) vs ≈ 15% closure (Yang 2009, primary, tension) and
  ≈ 10% closure (Lepech 2009, primary, tension). → [[gaps]]
- The "≈ 100 µm permeability threshold" attributed to Lepech & Li is a concrete-derived design target (Wang 1997), not an
  ECC measurement ([[lepech2009-ecc-water-permeability]], p. 745); the "0.10–0.15 mm" wording (Lepech & Li 2009, as
  cited in [[paul2014-r-shcc-chloride-corrosion]]) is not found in the primary. → [[permeability]]
- Whether Lepech 2009's ECC widths (Table 5) are averages or maxima is not stated (Fig. 5 axis says "maximum").
- Healing threshold: < 60 µm (Reinhardt & Jooss, via Ma 2021) vs < 50 µm (Yang 2009, on mortar) — refinement, not a
  contradiction. → [[self-healing]]
- No primary data on the crack-width **distribution** of PE-HSHDC; no Chinese PVA-HDC paper reports tensile crack width.
