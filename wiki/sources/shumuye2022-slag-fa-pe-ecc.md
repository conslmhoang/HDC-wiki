# Slag/fly-ash PE-ECC: slag replacing 0–15% of FA (Shumuye et al., 2022)

## One-paragraph summary
Shenzhen University (with Zhengzhou University and Hawassa University) made four PE-fiber ECC mixes with a
fixed 40% cement / 60% SCM binder in which slag replaces fly ash at 0, 5, 10 and 15% of binder
(SF-0-60 … SF-15-45; W/B 0.30; Table 1). They tested 100 mm cube compression at 7, 14 and 28 d,
dogbone direct tension and three-point bending of thin plates at 7 and 28 d, and characterised the matrix
by SEM/EDS, XRD and XRF. All mixes strain-hardened with multiple cracking. Slag raised 28 d compressive
strength from 40.79 to 55.05 MPa (+34.9% at 15% slag) and the 28 d first-crack strength (2.25 → 2.57 MPa
at 15%), while ultimate tensile strength stayed about 5.2–5.7 MPa (Table 4, Section 3.1). The tensile strain
reported is about 2.0% (0% slag), 3.5% (5%), 2.9% (10%) and "similar" for 15% (Section 3.2). More slag gave
more but wider cracks; SEM shows a denser matrix and more particles on the fiber surface, which the
authors read as stronger fiber–matrix bonding. SF-10-50 is recommended overall (Section 4).

> Synthesis: a normal-strength PE-ECC with a high-SCM binder (60% SCM). It is a useful data point for
> [[ggbfs]] vs [[fly-ash]] in PE-ECC. But the paper's own ductility statements conflict (see ⚔️ below), the
> flexural "load" units are implausible, and no crack-width numbers are given.

## Objective / research question
- Effect of partial replacement of fly ash by slag (0–15% of binder) on compressive, direct tensile and
  flexural behaviour of PE-ECC (Section 1).
- Microstructural and mineralogical changes caused by slag, which the authors say are rarely studied
  (Section 1).
- Motivation: high-quality FA supply is shrinking as coal plants close; slag supply is considered stable
  (Section 1).

## Materials & mix design
Constituents (Section 2.1, Tables 2–3):
- **Cement:** Type I Portland cement; CaO 64.56%, SiO₂ 17.94%.
- **Fly ash:** Class F; SiO₂ 45.20%, Al₂O₃ 26.10%, CaO 11.84% ⚠️ (high CaO for Class F), LOI 4.32%.
- **Slag:** CaO 33.60%, SiO₂ 36.50%, MgO 7.50%. Fineness/grade not stated.
- **Sand:** type and size not stated ⚠️.
- **HRWR:** type not stated.
- **PE fiber:** L 18 mm, d 20 µm, E 100 GPa, elongation 3%, σ_f 3000 MPa, ρ 0.97 g/cm³ (Table 3). Product
  not named.

## Test methods
- **Compression:** 100 mm cubes, 0.5 MPa/s, MTS 2000 kN, modified GB 50081 (cited as GB/T 50081-2002), n = 3,
  at 7, 14, 28 d (Section 2.3).
- **Direct tension:** dogbones "80 mm (length) × 30 mm (width) × 13 mm (depth)" (probably the gauge zone,
  Fig. 1 ⚠️); 0.4 mm/min; LVDTs; n = 3; 7 and 28 d (Section 2.4).
- **Flexure:** three-point bending, plate 320 × 60 × 13 mm, 0.4 mm/min, n = 3; span not stated; 7 and 28 d
  (Section 2.5, Fig. 2).
- **Crack analysis:** photographs of the dogbone central zone analysed with ImageJ (Section 3.4, Fig. 9).
- **Microstructure:** Hitachi SU8020 SEM + EDS (7 d), XRD (7, 28 d), XRF (28 d) (Section 2.6).
- **Curing & test age:** 7, 14 and 28 d.

Locations: f'c Section 3.1 and Fig. 4; σ_fc, σ_tu and flexural load Table 4; strain Section 3.2 / Fig. 5;
cracks Section 3.4 / Fig. 9. The tensile strain definition (at peak or at failure) is stated only as "average
ultimate strain" (Section 3.2).

Main findings (with location):
- **Compression:** 7 d gains of 27.0 / 57.0 / 77.3% for 5 / 10 / 15% slag vs SF-0-60; 28 d gains 3.5 / 28.5 /
  34.9% (Section 3.1).
- **Tension:** strain hardening and multiple cracking in all mixes at 7 and 28 d (Fig. 5). σ_tu/σ_fc at 28 d
  = 2.38 / 4.23 / 2.27 / 2.02 (authors print +137 / +323 / +127 / +101%, Section 3.2; checked here). Strain
  hardening "reduced with increasing slag content and longer curing time", except SF-5-55.
- **Flexure:** SF-10-50 highest at 28 d (553.48), SF-15-45 slightly lower (543.12); curing time had no
  obvious effect on the slag-free mix (Section 3.3, Table 4). Units printed as kN ⚠️.
- **Cracking:** crack number and crack width both rose with slag; SF-15-45 had the largest widths and
  "poor crack-control ability" (Sections 3.2, 3.4).
- **EDS Ca/Si (7 d):** 4.27 / 3.80 / 2.60 / 2.46 for 0 / 5 / 10 / 15% slag; by 28 d XRF compositions were
  similar (Section 3.5.1, Table 5).
- **XRD:** quartz, portlandite, calcite; minor gypsum and ettringite (Fig. 14).
- **Recommendation:** SF-10-50 best overall in a five-parameter radar assessment (Fig. 10, Section 4).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Slag reacts with CH to form C-S-H / C-A-S-H, densifying the matrix and the ITZ, raising f'c and first-crack
  strength (Sections 3.1, 3.5).
- Stronger fiber–matrix bond in slag mixes (more particles adhered to PE in SEM) restricts slip and can
  cause fiber rupture, lowering tensile strain (Sections 3.2, 4). → [[fiber-matrix-interface]]
- Higher matrix fracture toughness with slag gives lower ultimate strength and less ductility; FA gives a
  porous matrix, weaker friction bond and better ductility (Sections 3.4, 3.5.1). Fracture toughness was
  not measured ⚠️. → [[matrix-fracture-toughness]]
- Spherical FA improves flow and fiber distribution (Section 3.5.1). → [[fiber-dispersion]]

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- **Stated:** only normal exposure; future work on environment, curing temperature, cracks, fiber
  distribution and a wider slag range (Section 4).
- **Observed:** ⚔️ internal conflict on ductility: Section 3.2 says slag reduced tensile strain and
  "SF-0-60 showed the highest tensile strength capacity", but its own numbers give SF-0-60 ≈2.0% vs 3.5%
  for SF-5-55 and ≈2.9% for SF-10-50.
- **Observed:** flexural "ultimate load" of 403–562 kN for a 60 × 13 mm plate is physically implausible;
  probably N ⚠️.
- **Observed:** crack widths and crack numbers not reported numerically despite Section 3.4 promising details.
- **Observed:** sand type/size, slag fineness, curing regime after 24 h, fiber addition step, flexural span
  not stated. Table 2 is titled "(X-ray diffraction)" for oxide composition (probably XRF).
- **Observed:** Intro says ECC crack width is "less than 60 mm" (unit error for µm).
- **Observed:** n = 3; average curves only; no standard deviations.

## Relation to other work
- **Agrees with:** slag raises f'c and lowers ductility through a stronger interface, as with FA lowering bond
  in Xi'an HSHDC ([[yang2024-hshdc-uniaxial-tension]], p. 11) and in [[fly-ash]]; Zhu et al. 2012 (slag in
  HVFA-ECC, as cited, ref. 11).
- **Contradicts:** ⚔️ own data vs own text (see above). The EDS Ca/Si trend contradicts Tahmouresi et al.
  2021 (as cited, ref. 46), which the authors acknowledge (Section 3.5.1).
- **Extends:** first wiki primary source on PE-ECC in a **normal-strength** (≈41–55 MPa) high-SCM matrix;
  PE 18 mm × 20 µm also used by GDUT R-HSHDC ([[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]]) at much higher strength.
