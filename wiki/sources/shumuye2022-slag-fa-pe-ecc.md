# Slag/fly-ash PE-ECC: slag replacing 0–15% of FA (Shumuye et al., 2022)

> Re-checked against the publisher PDF on 2026-10-21 (page numbers added; Fig. 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10 read from the PDF).

## One-paragraph summary
Shenzhen University (with Zhengzhou University and Hawassa University) made four PE-fiber ECC mixes with a
fixed 40% cement / 60% SCM binder in which slag replaces fly ash at 0, 5, 10 and 15% of binder
(SF-0-60 … SF-15-45; W/B 0.30; Table 1, p. 4). They tested 100 mm cube compression at 7, 14 and 28 d,
dogbone direct tension and three-point bending of thin plates at 7 and 28 d, and characterised the matrix
by SEM/EDS, XRD and XRF. All mixes strain-hardened with multiple cracking. Slag raised 28 d compressive
strength from 40.79 to 55.05 MPa (+34.9% at 15% slag; Fig. 4, p. 7) and the 28 d first-crack strength (2.25 → 2.57 MPa
at 15%), while ultimate tensile strength stayed about 5.2–5.7 MPa (Table 4, p. 8). The tensile strain capacity
reported is about 2.0% (0% slag), 3.5% (5%), 2.9% (10%) (p. 8); Fig. 5b (p. 8) shows these are 28-d strains at peak stress,
and SF-15-45 peaks near ≈ 2.2% (read from Fig. 5b). More slag gave more but wider cracks (≈ 100 → 130 µm, read from the
radar chart Fig. 10, p. 12); SEM shows a denser matrix and more particles on the fiber surface, which the
authors read as stronger fiber–matrix bonding. SF-10-50 is recommended overall (§4, p. 17).

> Synthesis: a normal-strength PE-ECC with a high-SCM binder (60% SCM). It is a useful data point for
> [[ggbfs]] vs [[fly-ash]] in PE-ECC. But the paper's own ductility statements conflict (see ⚔️ below), the
> flexural "load" units are implausible, and crack widths are given only on a radar chart.

## Objective / research question
- Effect of partial replacement of fly ash by slag (0–15% of binder) on compressive, direct tensile and
  flexural behaviour of PE-ECC (§1, p. 3).
- Microstructural and mineralogical changes caused by slag, which the authors say are rarely studied
  (§1, p. 3).
- Motivation: high-quality FA supply is shrinking as coal plants close; slag supply is considered stable
  (§1, p. 3).

## Materials & mix design
Constituents (§2.1, p. 3–4; Tables 2–3, p. 4):
- **Cement:** Type I Portland cement; CaO 64.56%, SiO₂ 17.94%.
- **Fly ash:** Class F; SiO₂ 45.20%, Al₂O₃ 26.10%, Fe₂O₃ 6.93%, CaO 11.84% ⚠️ (high CaO for Class F), LOI 4.32%.
- **Slag:** CaO 33.60%, SiO₂ 36.50%, Al₂O₃ 14.30%, MgO 7.50%. Fineness/grade not stated.
- **Sand:** type and size not stated ⚠️ (only the Table 1 quantity).
- **HRWR:** type not stated.
- **PE fiber:** L 18 mm, d 20 µm, E 100 GPa, elongation 3%, σ_f 3000 MPa, ρ 0.97 g/cm³ (Table 3). Product
  not named.

## Test methods
- **Compression:** 100 mm cubes, 0.5 MPa/s, MTS 2000 kN, modified GB 50081 (ref 42 = GB/T 50081-2002), n = 3,
  at 7, 14, 28 d (§2.3, p. 4).
- **Direct tension:** dogbone (Fig. 1, p. 5): total length 330 mm (85 + 40 + 80 + 40 + 85), ends 60 mm wide, test section
  30 mm wide × 80 mm long × 13 mm thick. The text's "80 mm (length) × 30 mm (width) × 13 mm (depth)" (p. 4) is therefore the
  gauge zone. 0.4 mm/min (300 kN MTS machine); LVDTs; n = 3; 7 and 28 d (§2.4, p. 4–5).
- **Flexure:** three-point bending, plate 320 × 60 × 13 mm, **span 240 mm** (40 mm overhang each side, Fig. 2a, p. 5), 0.4 mm/min, n = 3;
  7 and 28 d (§2.5, p. 5).
- **Crack analysis:** photographs of the dogbone central zone analysed with ImageJ (§3.4, p. 10–11; Fig. 9, p. 11–12).
- **Microstructure:** Hitachi SU8020 SEM + EDS (7 d), XRD (7, 28 d), XRF (28 d) (§2.6, p. 6; §3.5, p. 12–17).
- **Curing & test age:** 7, 14 and 28 d.

Strain basis: the text says "average ultimate strain" (p. 7); in Fig. 5b the annotated "tensile strain capacity" arrow ends at ≈ 3.45%,

The averaged curves continue to ≈ 4.4–5.0% on the descending branch (Fig. 5b). Crack widths are read from the radar chart (axis 0–0.2 mm), so they are approximate (± ≈ 10 µm).

7-d strain at peak (read from Fig. 5a, p. 8, low precision): SF-0-60 ≈ 1.85%, SF-5-55 ≈ 0.75% (flat plateau to ≈ 1.8%), SF-10-50 ≈ 1.25%, SF-15-45 ≈ 1.5%.

Main findings (with location):
- **Compression (Fig. 4, p. 7):** 7 d 29.03 / 36.88 / 45.6 / 51.48 MPa; 28 d 40.79 / 42.23 / 52.44 / 55.05 MPa. Gains vs SF-0-60:
  7 d +27.0 / +57.0 / +77.3%, 28 d +3.5 / +28.5 / +34.9% (p. 6–7; checked against the bar labels).
- **Tension:** strain hardening and multiple cracking in all mixes at 7 and 28 d (Fig. 5). σ_tu/σ_fc at 28 d
  = 2.38 / 4.23 / 2.27 / 2.02 (authors print +137 / +323 / +127 / +101%, p. 8; Fig. 6b, p. 9); at 7 d +197 / +215 / +250 / +307% (Fig. 6a).
  Strain hardening "reduced with increasing slag content and longer curing time", except SF-5-55 (p. 8).
- **Flexure (Table 4, p. 8; Fig. 8, p. 10):** SF-10-50 highest at 28 d (553.48), SF-15-45 slightly lower (543.12); at 7 d SF-15-45 highest (561.50);
  curing time had no obvious effect on the slag-free mix (§3.3, p. 10). Units printed as kN in Table 4, Fig. 8 and Fig. 10 ⚠️.
  > Interpretation: with span 240 mm and a 60 × 13 mm section, σ = 3PL/(2bh²) gives ≈ 14.3–19.9 MPa if the loads are in **N**
  > (e.g. 553.48 N → ≈ 19.7 MPa), a plausible ECC flexural strength; in kN it would be ≈ 14,000–20,000 MPa. Computed here, not in the paper.
  The annotated "deflection capacity" is ≈ 12 mm at 7 d and ≈ 24.5 mm at 28 d (Fig. 8, read off; mix not identified).
- **Cracking:** crack number and crack width both rose with slag; SF-15-45 had the largest widths and
  "poor crack-control ability" (p. 10–11), yet the same section calls SF-15-45's multiple cracking "the most pronounced" (p. 11).
- **EDS Ca/Si (7 d):** 4.27 / 3.80 / 2.60 / 2.46 for 0 / 5 / 10 / 15% slag (p. 15). XRF at 28 d (Table 5, p. 16): Ca 28.63 / 26.31 / 28.37 / 28.77 wt.%,
  Si 15.38 / 16.13 / 15.32 / 15.52 wt.% → Ca/Si ≈ 1.86 / 1.63 / 1.85 / 1.85 (computed), i.e. similar across mixes.
- **XRD:** quartz, portlandite, calcite; minor gypsum and ettringite; calcite and portlandite peaks grow with age (Fig. 14, p. 16; p. 17).
- **Recommendation:** SF-5-55 and SF-10-50 best overall; SF-10-50 recommended (Fig. 10, p. 12; §4, p. 17).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Slag reacts with CH to form C-S-H / C-A-S-H, densifying the matrix and the ITZ, raising f'c and first-crack
  strength (§3.1, p. 6–7; §3.5, p. 12–15).
- Stronger fiber–matrix bond in slag mixes (more particles adhered to PE in SEM) restricts slip and can
  cause fiber rupture, lowering tensile strain (p. 8; §4, p. 17–18). → [[fiber-matrix-interface]]
- Higher matrix fracture toughness with slag gives lower ultimate strength and less ductility; FA gives a
  porous matrix, weaker friction bond and better ductility (§3.4, p. 11; §3.5.1, p. 12–13). Fracture toughness was
  not measured ⚠️. → [[matrix-fracture-toughness]]
- Spherical FA improves flow and fiber distribution (§3.5.1, p. 13). → [[fiber-dispersion]]

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- **Stated:** only normal exposure; future work on environment, curing temperature, cracks, fiber
  distribution and a wider slag range (§4, p. 18).
- **Observed:** ⚔️ internal conflict on ductility: p. 7 says slag reduced tensile strain, and p. 11 says
  "SF-0-60 showed the highest tensile strength capacity", but its own numbers give SF-0-60 ≈ 2.0% vs 3.5%
  for SF-5-55 and ≈ 2.9% for SF-10-50 (p. 8; Fig. 5b).
- **Observed:** flexural "ultimate load" of 403–562 kN for a 60 × 13 mm plate on a 240 mm span is physically implausible;
  probably N ⚠️ (see interpretation above).
- **Observed:** crack widths only on a radar chart (Fig. 10); crack numbers not reported, despite p. 10 promising details in §3.4.
- **Observed:** sand type/size, slag fineness, curing regime after 24 h, fiber addition step not stated.
  Table 2 is titled "(X-ray diffraction)" for oxide composition (probably XRF). Mixing cites "Huang et al. (2013) [3]" but ref 3 is Huang et al. 2020.
- **Observed:** Intro says ECC crack width is "less than 60 mm" (p. 1; unit error for µm).
- **Observed:** n = 3; average curves only; no standard deviations.

## Relation to other work
- **Agrees with:** slag raises f'c and lowers ductility through a stronger interface, as with FA lowering bond
  in Xi'an HSHDC ([[yang2024-hshdc-uniaxial-tension]], p. 11) and in [[fly-ash]]; Zhu et al. 2012 (slag in
  HVFA-ECC, as cited, ref. 11).
- **Contradicts:** ⚔️ own data vs own text (see above). The EDS Ca/Si trend contradicts Tahmouresi et al.
  2021 (as cited, ref. 46), which the authors acknowledge (§3.5.1, p. 15).
- **Extends:** first wiki primary source on PE-ECC in a **normal-strength** (≈41–55 MPa) high-SCM matrix;
  PE 18 mm × 20 µm also used by GDUT R-HSHDC ([[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]]) at much higher strength.
