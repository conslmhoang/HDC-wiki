# Surface-modified PVA fibers in ECC (Sun et al., 2019)

> Re-checked against the publisher PDF on 2026-10-21 (page numbers added; Fig. 1, 5, 6–10 read from the PDF).

## One-paragraph summary
Wuhan University of Technology tested three **fiber surface pre-treatments** for PVA in a 40 MPa-class
ECC (P.O 42.5 cement + first-grade fly ash + 0.15 mm quartz sand; W/B 0.30 or 0.35; 2 vol.% PVA):
1. soaking in a diluted **vinyl acetate–ethylene (VAE) emulsion** with ultrasonication, then drying;
2. coating with a boric anhydride + cement slurry;
3. soaking in boric anhydride + styrene–butadiene ("butylbenzene") emulsion.
VAE treatment worked best. It leaves a weakly acidic polymer film that locally retards hydration and
weakens the interfacial transition zone, so fibers slip and pull out instead of rupturing (§3.2, p. 15–17; §3.3, p. 17).
With Kuraray REC-15, 7-d tensile strain rose from **3.21% (untreated, S4) to 4.05% (VAE, S2)**, +26.2%,
at almost unchanged strength (Table 10, p. 13). The error bars in Fig. 6 (p. 13) are large (S2 ≈ 2.8–5.3%), so the
gain lies within the scatter. With a Chinese domestic PVA, VAE (or the boric/SB emulsion; the text and table
disagree ⚠️) raised strain from 0.46% to 0.97% (Table 10). Boric anhydride treatments lowered strength
(28-d f'c 34.2 vs 40.6 MPa) and made the fibers pull out too easily (S3: 2.02%, softening). Strain fell
sharply from 7 to 28 d: 3.21 → 1.39% (untreated) and 4.05 → 2.18% (VAE) (Table 11, p. 16). Slurry rheology was
first optimised with HPMC and PCE (Tables 6–7, p. 8, 11).

## Objective / research question
- Optimise the fresh slurry (PCE superplasticizer + HPMC viscosity agent) for fluidity and stability (§3.1, p. 7–11).
- Find a fiber surface pre-treatment that weakens the PVA/matrix interface and raises ECC ductility (§1, p. 3–4; §3.2).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry:
  - Compression/flexure: cement-mortar method (ISO method, ref 30; WDW-10 machine) (§2.3.2, p. 6). Prism size not stated
    (40 × 40 × 160 mm implied by the ISO method ⚠️).
  - Tension: JSCE-type dumbbell (Fig. 1a, p. 7): total length 330 mm; 60 mm wide grip ends (85 mm long); 40 mm tapers (R = 25 mm);
    30 mm wide, 80 mm long test section; thickness 13 or 30 mm (both given in Fig. 1a; which was used is not stated). LVDT gauge 80 mm (Fig. 1b).
- Loading / rate: tension at 0.4 mm/min displacement control, Instron 5882; 25 °C, 75% RH (§2.3.3, p. 6–7).
- Measurement: two displacement sensors on aluminium clamps, averaged; 1 Hz logging, Keithley 2700 (p. 7). SEM (FEI Quanta 450 FEG) of fiber surfaces (§2.3.4, p. 7).
  Rheology: Brookfield R/S-SST rotational rheometer, vane V80-40, shear stress range 6–200 Pa; constant 200 s⁻¹ at 0 / 30 / 120 min (60 s each);
  ramp 0–200 s⁻¹ in 180 s at 1 / 31 / 121 min; up-down loop for thixotropy (§2.3.1, p. 6).
- Curing & test age: demould at 24–48 h, "standard curing" (conditions not given); tension at 7 d (all) and 28 d (S2, S4 via C2, C1 batches, p. 16);
  strength at 7 d (Fig. 5, p. 13) and 28 d (Table 8, p. 11). Specimen count per group not reported.

Strain basis: the paper calls it "ultimate tensile strain" and does not define it. The representative curves (Figs. 7–10,
p. 14–16) show that it is the strain at the **end of the test, including the descending branch**, not at peak stress:
S3 peaks at ≈ 2.85 MPa near ≈ 1.0% strain and ends near ≈ 2.2% (Table: 2.02%); S4 7-d peaks near ≈ 2.9% and ends near ≈ 3.35%
(read from Figs. 8–9). Values are therefore upper bounds on the strain capacity at peak stress.

Scatter (error bars in Fig. 6, p. 13, read off): strain S1 ≈ 2.3–5.25%, S2 ≈ 2.8–5.3%, S4 ≈ 2.6–3.8%, S3 ≈ 1.35–2.7%, C2 (28 d) ≈ 1.65–2.7%, C1 (28 d) ≈ 0.95–1.8%.
Fig. 6 also plots the 28-d C1 bars at first-crack ≈ 3.4 MPa and ultimate ≈ 3.55 MPa, slightly above Table 11's S4-28d 3.2 / 3.3 MPa ⚠️ (read from Fig. 6).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- VAE treatment of REC-15: +26.2% strain at 7 d (4.05 vs 3.21%), +56.8% at 28 d (2.18 vs 1.39%); first-crack and tensile strength unchanged (Table 11, p. 16; §3.2, p. 15–17).
- **Age loss of ductility:** 7 → 28 d strain fell 56.7% (untreated) and 46.2% (VAE); first-crack strength rose from about 2.6 to about 3.2–3.4 MPa (Table 11; p. 16).
- Domestic untreated PVA (31 µm, 1400 MPa) is nearly brittle (0.46%); treatment doubles it to 0.97% (+110.8%) (Table 10, p. 13; Fig. 7, p. 14).
- Lower W/B (0.30 vs 0.35) gave higher strain and strength at 7 d (3.79 vs 3.21%; 3.52 vs 3.3 MPa) but poorer fluidity, so 0.35 was chosen (p. 14).
  The difference lies within the Fig. 6 error bars.
- 7-d compression (Fig. 5, p. 13): S1 33.0 MPa (text) > S2 ≈ 30.3 > S4 ≈ 26.3 > S3 ≈ 23.7 MPa (read from Fig. 5); flexural all ≈ 9.3–10.9 MPa.
- Boric anhydride + cement slurry: f'c −15.8% and flexural −31.3% vs C1 (Table 8, p. 11, deviation column 84.3 / 68.7%). Text (p. 12) says f'c "dropped by 25.7%" ⚠️ (does not match Table 8).
- Boric + SB emulsion (S3): strain −37.1% vs S4, softening; SEM shows long, intact pulled-out fibers (weak bond) (Table 10; Fig. 9, p. 15; Fig. 12, p. 17).
- Adding 2% PVA lowered 28-d f'c by about 4 MPa (44.4 → 40.6) but raised flexural strength 5.7 → 18.2 MPa (Table 8, p. 11).
- Rheology: 1.8% PCE gave lower viscosity than 0.9%; more or higher-viscosity HPMC raises initial viscosity; L2 had lowest thixotropy (9910.953 → 7673.119 → 5063.676 Pa·s⁻¹ at 1 / 31 / 121 min) (Tables 6–7, p. 8, 11; Figs. 2–4, p. 8–10).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Untreated PVA –C–OH groups form hydrogen bonds with matrix –OH → strong bond → fiber rupture at early cracking (p. 14).
- VAE film is weakly acidic (pH 4.0–6.5): retards hydration next to the fiber and weakens the ITZ; fibers slip and pull out (p. 15–17; Fig. 11b, p. 17 shows less adhering hydrate and axial scratching).
- Untreated S4 fibers carry adhered hydrates and show slight "necking" at the break → rupture after small slip (Fig. 11a, p. 17).
- Boric anhydride retards cement hydration strongly; loose particles detach in mixing and lower matrix alkalinity → loss of strength, and bond becomes too weak (p. 12).
- Strain loss with age: hydration densifies the ITZ, constraining slip and promoting rupture; authors cite (uncited) "other academics" that ECC strain peaks at about 10 d (p. 16).
- Air entrained by fibers lowers f'c slightly (p. 11–12).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: the effect of boric anhydride dosage needs further study (Conclusion 5, p. 18).
- Observed:
  - No full mix proportions (FA content, sand ratio missing) — results cannot be reproduced.
  - Internal inconsistencies: G1/G2 W/B (0.3 text p. 14 vs 0.35 Table 10) and G2 treatment (boric/SB text vs VAE table); HPMC 0.05% vs 5; "25.7%" strength drop; S3 named where S4/S2 seem meant (p. 16, Fig. 10 paragraph); Fig. 6 C1 bars vs Table 11 S4-28d.
  - No single-fiber pullout tests: interface weakening is inferred, not measured. Coating mass/thickness not measured.
  - Specimen count not reported; strain scatter (Fig. 6) is about ± 1–1.5% for S1/S2, larger than the VAE effect; no crack width or crack count.
  - "Ultimate tensile strain" includes the softening branch (Figs. 8–9), so it overstates strain capacity at peak, especially for S3.
  - 28-d tensile data come from C1/C2 batches (p. 16); curing conditions ("standard") not specified.
  - Durability of the VAE film not studied.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]] — weakening the PVA interface (oil there, VAE here) shifts failure from rupture to pull-out and raises strain; too weak a bond (S3 here) loses ductility, consistent with an optimum bond ([[fiber-surface-treatment]]).
- Agrees with: ductility loss with age of PVA-ECC (Lepech & Li 2006, as cited in [[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]], via [[pva-fiber]]); here the loss is much faster (−47 to −57% in 7→28 d).
- Contradicts: none directly; ⚔️ note that the 28-d strain of the untreated REC-15 ECC (1.39%) is far below other REC-15 / oiled-PVA ECC at 28 d (about 4–4.5% M45, [[yang2008-ecc-fiber-bridging-law]]); different matrix (unknown FA content).
- Extends: a non-oil, post-purchase coating (VAE) as an interface-tailoring route; also on domestic Chinese PVA.
