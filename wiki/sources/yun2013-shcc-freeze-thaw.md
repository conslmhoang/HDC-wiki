# Hybrid PVA + PE SHCC after 300 freeze–thaw cycles (Yun, 2013)

## One-paragraph summary
A single-author study from Chungnam National University (Korea) exposed four hybrid PVA + PE SHCC mixes
(W/B 0.45 or 0.55; PVA 1.30% + PE 0.20% or PVA 0.75% + PE 0.75%, total 1.5 vol.%) to rapid freeze–thaw
by ASTM C666 **Procedure B** (freezing in air, thawing in water; +4 to −18 °C, 3.5 h per cycle), starting
at 14 d, up to 300 cycles (§2.3). Compressive, third-point flexural and **direct tensile (JSCE dumbbell)**
tests were made at 0, 100, 200 and 300 cycles (Table 3). Compressive strength and modulus of rupture changed
little; direct tensile strength showed no distinct trend; but the **tensile strain capacity fell slightly,
multiple cracking became sparser, and flexural prisms failed with a single crack after 300 cycles**
(§3.2–3.4, Figs. 6, 9, 11; Conclusions 2–4). Loss of ductility was larger for the higher W/B and the
higher (hydrophilic) PVA share (Conclusion 3). SEM showed microcracks in the fiber–matrix ITZ after F-T
(Fig. 13).

> Synthesis: this is the wiki's **first primary study with uniaxial tension after freeze–thaw**. It
> resolves the "Yun 2013 vs Yun 2011" citation question: they are two different papers by the same group.
> Yun 2011 (Cold Regions Sci. Technol. 67(1–2):49–57) is this paper's ref. [24]. The data quality is
> modest (2 dumbbells per point, large scatter, internal inconsistencies, ageing confounded with cycles).

## Objective / research question
- How do rapid F-T cycles change the compressive, flexural and direct tensile behaviour of hybrid PVA + PE
  SHCC with different W/B and fiber blends (§1, p. 12)?
- Motivation: SHCC is increasingly used as an exterior repair material (e.g. sprayed SHCC for bridge-pier
  seismic retrofit in Korea, Fig. 1), but its properties after F-T have been little studied (§1).

## Materials & mix design
Constituents (§2.1, Tables 1–2):
- **Cement:** ASTM C150 Type I; Blaine 364 m²/kg, SG 3.15.
- **Fly ash:** ASTM C618 Class F (Boryung power plant); Blaine 341 m²/kg, SG 2.35.
- **Silica fume:** Elkem Microsilica grade 940 (amount not reported; listed under "agents in binder", Table 1).
- **Sand:** silica sand, SG 2.61, grain size 105–120 µm (text prints "lm" for µm).
- **Polymer:** acrylic polymer (Forton BV), polymer/cement 5.0 wt.%.
- **Admixtures:** methyl cellulose (viscosity agent), shrinkage-reducing agent, polynaphthalene-sulfonate SP,
  antifoaming agent.
- **PVA fiber:** L 12 mm, d 40 µm, σ_f 1600 MPa, E 40 GPa (oiling and maker not stated).
- **PE fiber:** UHMW-PE, L 12 mm, d 12 µm (strength and modulus not reported).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry: per mix, six Ø100×200 mm cylinders (ASTM C39), four 100×100×400 mm prisms (ASTM C78,
  third-point loading), nine dumbbell specimens (JSCE 2008 recommendations, Fig. 2; dimensions only in figure) (§2.3).
- Loading / rate: rates not reported.
- Measurement (LVDT, DIC, ...): not described in text; tensile curves are strain-based (Figs. 7–10).
- Exposure: ASTM C666 Procedure B; +4 → −18 → +4 °C in 3.5 h; 100, 200, 300 cycles (§2.3, p. 13).
- Curing & test age: 1 d under wet burlap + plastic at 23 ± 2 °C, then **air-cured 23 ± 2 °C, 65% RH** to
  14 d; F-T started at 14 d; reference specimens kept in a high-humidity room (§2.2). Virgin properties
  "at the age of 14 days" (p. 13), but Table 3 footnote b gives curing ages **39, 68, 121, 151 d** for 0, 100,
  200, 300 cycles ⚠️ (inconsistent).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- f'c at "28 d" about 49 (PVA1.30+PE0.20) and 44 MPa (PVA0.75+PE0.75) at W/B 0.45; f'c falls about 19% as W/B
  rises 0.45 → 0.55 (§3.1, p. 13). More PE lowers f'c; blamed on poor dispersion of PE multifilament and
  extra air/porosity (p. 13–14).
- F-T within 300 cycles has little effect on compressive behaviour; static modulus decreases slightly
  (Fig. 4b; Conclusion 1). Compressive strength ratio after 300 cycles is > 1 for three of four mixes (Fig. 4a),
  which the author attributes to fiber "porosity" giving space for freezing water (p. 15).
- Flexure: after 300 cycles the ascending branch is steeper and deflection at peak smaller (Fig. 5); MOR does not
  change appreciably (Table 3; Conclusion 2); prisms show **multiple cracks before F-T but fail with a single
  crack after 300 cycles** (Fig. 6, §3.2).
- Tension: all dumbbells strain-harden before and after F-T (Fig. 8, §3.3); "strain capacities ranging from 0.5%
  to nearly 1.0%" (Fig. 7, p. 16); tensile strength shows little change, tensile strain capacity decreases slightly
  (Figs. 8–9; Conclusion 3). The reduction is more prominent for **higher W/B and more PVA** (Conclusion 3).
- Fewer, less widespread cracks after 300 cycles; before F-T, crack number rises with lower W/B and more PE
  (Fig. 11, §3.4).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- The fiber–matrix ITZ (said to be 40–70 µm thick and porous, as cited, p. 18) is still porous at 14 d; F-T
  causes moisture movement and hydraulic pressure there, giving ITZ microcracks (SEM, Fig. 13b) and matrix
  microcracking, which reduces multiple cracking (§3.5).
- Hydrophilic PVA (vs hydrophobic PE) and higher W/B make SHCC more F-T sensitive (Conclusion 3; mechanism not
  measured).
- PE fibers raise air content and local porosity and are hard to disperse, which lowers f'c (p. 13–14).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Observed: only 2 dumbbells per tensile point and large scatter (Fig. 9); the "trend" in strain capacity is not
  visible in Fig. 9b, where 300-cycle values (≈ 0.35–0.85%) overlap the 0-cycle values (≈ 0.05–0.9%) ⚠️.
- Observed: ageing confounded with cycling (cured 39 → 151 d per footnote b); strength gains after F-T may be
  hydration, not F-T resistance.
- Observed: high air content (10–13%, Table 3) acts like air entrainment; not discussed by the author.
- Observed internal inconsistencies ⚠️: (1) §3.3 says higher W/B gives higher tensile strength (Fig. 7), but
  Table 3 at 0 cycles gives 3.77 (W/B 0.45) > 2.89 MPa (W/B 0.55); (2) Fig. 9b strain capacities (≤ ≈ 1%) vs
  Fig. 10 text (SH to ≈ 2.4% for the virgin 45-PVA1.30+PE0.20); (3) §3.2 refers to "higher W/B (=0.50)" though
  mixes use 0.55; (4) §3.5 says F-T has a "small effect" on SHCC with hydrophilic PVA and higher W/B, contrary
  to Conclusion 3 (apparently a wording error); (5) Fig. 7 caption names only 55-PVA1.30+PE0.20 but shows both W/B.
- No crack-width measurement; no RDEM or mass loss reported; no plain-concrete control in tension (a "Concrete"
  series appears only in Fig. 4).
- Air curing at 65% RH (not water curing) before F-T.

- Agrees with: [[wang2023-ecc-frost-damage]], [[zhong2019-ecc-freeze-thaw]] (SHCC/ECC strength survives 300 cycles);
  [[chai2019-hdcc-freeze-thaw-carbonation-flexure]] and [[wang2023-ecc-frost-damage]] (fiber–matrix interface as
  the weak link after F-T; here shown as ITZ microcracks by SEM).

  paper does (dumbbells, Figs. 8–10). ⚔️ (refinement) Li et al. 2003 (as cited in [[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]])
  reports ε_tu about 3% retained after 300 cycles; here, hybrid SHCC with ε at peak ≤ ≈ 1% loses multiple cracking.
- Extends: confirms the [[he2024-hdc-research-advances]] citation (hybrid PVA + PE loses more ε than σ) and shows
  the [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]] citation "Yun 2011" is a separate earlier paper (this paper's ref. [24]).
  Cites Sahmaran 2012 (FA/B 55% and 70% ECC: less stiffness after 300 cycles), opposite to the steeper
  ascending branch seen here (p. 15–16).
