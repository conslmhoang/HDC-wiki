# Crack formation and chloride-induced corrosion in R/SHCC (Paul & van Zijl, 2014)

## One-paragraph summary
Stellenbosch study of steel-bar-reinforced **SHCC** beams (100 × 100 × 500 mm, Y10 bars, cover 15/25/35 mm,
1 or 2 bars) made with fine sand (FS, ≤ 0.30 mm) or a cheaper natural coarse sand (CS, ≤ 1.7 mm), plus
R/mortar controls of the same matrix without fibres. Beams were pre-cracked in three-point bending
(3.5 mm deflection for R/SHCC, 1.5 mm for R/mortar), unloaded, and exposed on the cracked face to 3.5% NaCl
in 4-day dry / 3-day wet cycles for up to 620 d (R/SHCC) or 458 d (R/mortar). R/SHCC formed 5–18 cracks with
average widths **22–38 µm** and maximum **50–120 µm**; R/mortar formed 5 cracks, average **139 µm**, maximum
**250 µm** (Table 3). Yet **cracks of 50–100 µm let chloride penetrate 65–89 mm (nearly the full crack
height) within 1–6 h** of capillary suction, vs 4.5–8 mm in uncracked SHCC (Table 5), and the authors
state that cracks **below 50 µm** also give rapid chloride ingress (Abstract; p. 349, citing Paul & van
Zijl 2013a). Chloride at the rebar was 0.17–0.59% by binder mass with no correlation to crack width,
number of cracks or cover. Corrosion rates were similar in R/SHCC and R/mortar (about 0.003–0.02 mm/yr),
higher for 15 mm cover; a threshold cover of 15–25 mm is suggested. After 560–610 d pitting depth was
0.2–0.3 mm (avg) and 0.3–0.6 mm (max), with up to 13.2% loss of rebar yield force; corrosion was
**localised** in R/SHCC but spread out in R/mortar (Table 4, Fig. 13).

## Objective / research question
- Relate flexural crack patterns (width, number, spacing), cover depth, reinforcement level and sand
  type to chloride ingress and rebar corrosion in R/SHCC (p. 341).
- Test whether a cheaper coarse natural sand SHCC performs adequately (p. 341).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry: compression 100 mm cubes and Ø100 × 200 mm cylinders; direct tension dumbbells,
  30 × 16 mm section, 80 mm gauge; beams 100 × 100 × 500 mm (p. 341).
- Loading / rate: three-point bending pre-cracking to 3.5 mm (R/SHCC) / 1.5 mm (R/mortar) deflection;
  unreinforced SHCC beams to 1.0 mm at 28 d for AgNO₃ tests (p. 342). Loading rate not reported.
- Measurement: crack widths by high-resolution camera + Leica MZ 7.5 microscope + line-width template,
  over the central 200 mm (p. 345); half-cell potential (Cu/CuSO₄); corrosion rate by coulostatic pulse
  (4 mA, 5 ms; B = 26 mV; Eqs. 1–6); CT scan; pitting depth; rebar tensile tests (Zwick Z250); XRF
  chloride profiles in 3 mm layers to 45 mm (Ø16 mm drill); 0.1 N AgNO₃ spray (p. 342–343).
- Exposure: epoxy-sealed sides; cracked face in contact with 3.5 wt% NaCl, 4 d drying / 3 d wetting,
  21 ± 2 °C, 60 ± 5% RH; up to 620 d (R/SHCC), 458 d (R/mortar) (p. 342, p. 349).
- Curing & test age: demoulded at 2 d; water 21 ± 2 °C for 7 d; then lab air 21 ± 2 °C, 60 ± 5% RH.
  Cubes/cylinders at 28 d; dumbbells and reinforced beams at 14 d (p. 341–342).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Fine cracks do not stop chloride**: 50–100 µm cracks carried chloride 65–89 mm deep within 1–6 h,
  about 10–15× deeper than in uncracked SHCC (Table 5, Fig. 16, p. 349). Cracks < 50 µm also gave quick
  penetration; max 14.5 mm in the uncracked region after 90 d wet–dry (Paul & van Zijl 2013a, as cited, p. 349).
- **Chloride reached and passed the rebar** in all R/SHCC and R/mortar beams: 0.30–0.59% (C15), 0.22–0.58%
  (C25), 0.17–0.40% (C35) by binder mass at the rebar; **no correlation** with total or average crack width,
  number of cracks or cover (Fig. 17, p. 349–350). XRF = total chloride.
- **Half-cell potential** < −350 mV CSE in most specimens, some within the first day; ASTM C876 limits
  may not apply to SHCC (Fig. 6, p. 346).
- **Corrosion rate** about 0.003–0.02 mm/yr, similar in R/SHCC and R/mortar despite fewer cracks in mortar;
  higher at 15 mm cover; slightly lower with 2 bars (Fig. 8, p. 346; values read from figure ⚠️).
- **Corroded depth** (uniform, Eq. 7, from 480 d with d_c0 = 0) about 1.7–3.3 µm by 600 d, highest in
  FS-C15B1 (Fig. 9). No correlation with total crack width (Fig. 10). Threshold cover 15–25 mm (Fig. 11, p. 346).
- **Damage pattern**: distributed over long length in R/mortar, **localised pits** in R/SHCC (Fig. 13, p. 348);
  uniform-loss model underestimates local section loss and calculated F_y differs strongly from measured (Fig. 15).
- Max ΔF_y 13.2% (CS-SHCC C25B1); C15 bars had most visible pitting but not the largest strength loss
  (Table 4, p. 348–349). Avg pitting depth correlates with ΔF_y (Fig. 14).
- At 17 and 52 d, only discolouration at crack positions; pitting appeared by 560–610 d (p. 347).
- **Coarse sand** lowered ε_max (2.93 → 1.52%) with similar σ_tu; attributed to early crack localisation at
  large sand particles (p. 344). Corrosion showed no clear sand effect (p. 346).
- Unreinforced beams: 17.06 kN (FS) vs 14.73 kN (CS) at 1 mm deflection (p. 344–345).
- Crack widths "significantly reduced" after unloading (p. 345).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Cracks are fast capillary paths; penetration widens along the crack height with time (p. 349).
- Postulated: fibre-enhanced tensile capacity restrains corrosion-induced expansion, subduing corrosion
  rate; a strong steel–matrix interface lengthens initiation (p. 341, citing others).
- Smaller crack spacing lowers corrosion rate in RC (Schiessl & Raupach 1997); SHCC gives fine spacing
  without increasing bar surface area (p. 341).
- Local high Cl peaks inside the profile attributed to crack geometry varying with depth (p. 349).
- High perturbation (200–1500 mV) in SHCC vs concrete attributed to different resistivity / permeability (p. 343).
- Role of chloride binding in the high-FA matrix remains to be established (p. 350).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: accelerated exposure not calibrated to real exposure (p. 342); corrosion-rate measurement only
  began after about 320 d (mortar) and 480 d (SHCC), so d_c0 ignored and R/SHCC vs R/mortar comparison is
  unfair (p. 346–347); original rebar mass not recorded (p. 344); small data set (p. 350); XRF gives total,
  not free, chloride; Ø16 mm drill exceeds crack spacing (p. 342, 349).
- Observed: R/SHCC (3.5 mm) and R/mortar (1.5 mm) pre-cracked to different deflections; R/mortar exposed
  458 d vs 620 d for R/SHCC.
- Observed: very weak matrix (w/c 1.0, f_cu 23–24 MPa) and low σ_tu (2.7 MPa) — not representative of
  M45-type ECC or HSHDC.
- Observed: text says most cracks were 50–100 µm (p. 345), but Table 3 averages are 22–38 µm and the Fig. 5
  histograms peak in the 0–49 µm bins ⚠️. Whether Table 3 widths are under load or after unloading is not
  stated ⚠️.
- Observed: the Abstract's "< 50 µm cracks allow chloride to the rebar within hours" relies on a cited
  companion study (2013a); the hours-scale data here (Table 5) are for 50–100 µm cracks ⚠️.
- Observed: Fig. 16 labels penetration "65 cm … 89 cm", Table 5 says mm (typo in figure).
- Observed: Table 2 lists "FS-Mortar" twice; second row probably CS-Mortar.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]] (cracks well below 100 µm fill with water within minutes,
  neutron radiography; tolerable crack width unknown).
- Contradicts: ⚔️ chloride crack-width thresholds in [[crack-width]] / [[durability]]: "55–80 µm (Jang 2011)"
  and "≥ 90 µm (Wu 2017)" as cited in [[he2024-hdc-research-advances]], and "about 100 µm also for chloride"
  in [[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]]. Here cracks of 50–100 µm (and reportedly < 50 µm) give chloride
  ingress to rebar depth within hours under capillary suction. Different transport mode (capillary
  suction vs diffusion/migration) and different matrix; not a direct numeric conflict but it undermines a
  "safe" threshold. ⚔️ Also contradicts the general expectation (e.g. [[li2003-ecc-review]], p. 225;
  [[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]], p. 265 citing Miyazato 2005) that R/SHCC corrodes more slowly than R/mortar:
  rates here were similar.
- Extends: first primary wiki source on reinforcement corrosion in a strain-hardening composite; first
  with measured crack-width distributions under chloride; coarse-sand SHCC.
