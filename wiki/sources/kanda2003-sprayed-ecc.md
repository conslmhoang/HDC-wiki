# Direct Sprayed ECC (Kanda et al., 2003)

## One-paragraph summary
Kajima / Kuraray develop the first **wet-process sprayed ECC** showing clear pseudo-strain hardening (PSH)
(p. 274–275, Conclusion 2). The matrix is pre-designed with the Kanda & Li micromechanics model: J′b/J_tip
rises with w/c, ≥ 44% would give the "matured PSH" margin of about 2.7, but **0.32** is chosen for durability,
keeping J′b/J_tip about 2.0 (Fig. 2, p. 270–271). Four mixes (OPC/LPC, 15/25% air, omni vs ordinary concrete mixer)
with 2.1 vol.% PVA (12 mm, 40 µm) all strain-harden; sprayed specimens exceed 2% ultimate strain and outperform
cast ("placed") specimens in tension, which the authors attribute to 2-D fibre orientation in thin sprayed layers
(Fig. 8, 10, p. 275–276). Cement type, air content and mixer type have little effect. In a tapered-pin
**re-bar corrosion simulation**, sprayed ECC and a commercial PCM crack at similar pin displacement (about
3.2–3.6 mm), but ECC keeps carrying load, forms ≥ 3 cracks and keeps first-crack COD < 0.3 mm (PCM 0.5–1 mm)
(Table 8, Fig. 13–15). → [[repair-and-field-applications]]

## Objective / research question
- Achieve wet-process direct-spray processing of ECC (previously only dry-mix glass-fibre composites existed) (p. 269).
- Verify that sprayed ECC matches cast ECC in tension and flexure, and examine effects of cement type,
  air content, mixer type, casting method and spray thickness (p. 272–273).
- Quantify anti-cracking / anti-spalling ability against re-bar corrosion expansion vs PCM (p. 273).

## Test methods
- **Processing:** omni-mixer (70 L) or concrete mixer with two horizontal scraping axes (100 L); squeeze mortar
  pump (6 m³/h), 40 mm × 10 m hose, 8 mm nozzle, 3 L/min; about 10 mm per pass, repeatable at about 10 min
  intervals to about 80 mm overhead (p. 271, 274). Concrete-mixer route needed 15 min mortar mixing and staged
  water (80% + 20% after 4 min) to avoid fibre balls (p. 274).
- **Fresh tests:** temperature, table flow (JIS R 5201), specific gravity (JIS A 1116), air (JIS A 1128) after
  mixing and after spraying (p. 273). → [[fresh-properties]]
- **Tension:** coupon gauge length 80 mm, width 30 mm, thickness 13 mm (Fig. 4a); sprayed vs placed; ≥ 3 specimens
  (p. 273). Loading rate not reported. Ultimate point = start of continuous load decrease. → [[dogbone-uniaxial-tension]]
- **Flexure:** 40 × 40 × 160 mm, centre-point loading, 100 mm span; sprayed, placed, and cut-out (top/middle/bottom
  50 mm of a 500 × 500 × 150 mm block sprayed in one go); ≥ 3 specimens (p. 273).
- **Anti-spalling (re-bar corrosion simulation):** 100 × 100 × 50 mm prism with a hole; stainless tapered pin
  (JIS B 1352, 200 mm, taper 1/50) pushed at 2 mm/min (hole diameter grows 0.04 mm/min), 500 kN machine; COD by
  optical microscope and crack gauge; 3 specimens per material; mix B vs PCM (SBR-modified commercial premix)
  (Fig. 5–6, p. 273–274).
- **Curing & test age:** demould at 2 d, cured at 20 °C, tension/flexure at **28 d** (p. 273). Anti-spalling,
  companion tension and Ø100 × 200 mm compression specimens: 20 °C water, tested at **50–52 d** (p. 274).

Fresh properties (Table 5): table flow 174–183 mm after mixing, 162–174 mm after spraying; air 12.5–25.0% after
mixing falls to 6.6–8.4% after spraying; specific gravity 1.66–1.87 → 1.98–2.01. Fresh temperature 20.5–24.1 °C
after mixing.

Main findings:
- All sprayed specimens exceed 2% ultimate strain (Fig. 8a, p. 275); placed specimens have lower strain and
  stress capacity (Fig. 8b, 10).
- Effects of air content, cement type (OPC vs LPC) and mixer type are "rather insignificant"; all mixes exceed
  multiple-crack saturation (p. 275–276). Hardened specific gravity A 1.98, B 1.97 (p. 276).
- Flexure: MOR roughly 12–17 MPa across specimen types (Fig. 11b, read approximately ⚠️); sprayed does not
  outperform placed in flexure; cut-out specimens have clearly lower ultimate mid-span displacement (Fig. 11c, p. 277).
- Anti-spalling (Table 8): first cracking at pin displacement 3.24 mm (ECC) vs 3.61 mm (PCM); load 5.65 vs 6.03 kN;
  COD just after first cracking 0.036 vs 0.067 mm. At 10 mm pin displacement first-crack COD < 0.3 mm (ECC) vs
  0.5–1 mm (PCM) (Fig. 14). At 15 mm, ECC keeps all cracks but one < about 0.2 mm, PCM about 1 mm (Fig. 15, p. 279).
  ECC forms > 3 cracks (up to 7), PCM 1–2.
- Pin displacement 0.5–1 mm ≈ the 0.01–0.02 mm re-bar expansion that cracks normal concrete cover (FEM literature,
  as cited), so both repair materials delay corrosion cracking; only ECC avoids post-crack load loss (p. 279).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- **2-D fibre orientation** in thin (about 10 mm) sprayed layers improves tensile performance of 13 mm coupons;
  thicker sprays (40 mm flexure, 150 mm blocks) lose this and may contain more flaws (p. 276–277). → [[fiber-dispersion]]
- **Micromechanics pre-design:** J′b insensitive to w/c while J_tip rises as w/c falls (Km 0.13 → 0.33 MPa√m),
  so J′b/J_tip increases with w/c (Fig. 2, Table 2). → [[strain-hardening]], [[matrix-fracture-toughness]]
- Air squeezed out during pumping/spraying, so air content after mixing need only be roughly controlled (p. 275).
- LPC limits fresh temperature (hot weather); by 28 d the hydration-rate difference vanishes (p. 272, 276).
- ECC's fibre bridging halves the first-crack COD and distributes damage; water flow through cracks ∝ COD³
  (Tsukamoto 1990, as cited), so smaller COD should slow corrosion (expectation, not tested) (p. 279).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: COD benefit on corrosion rate must be clarified experimentally; larger-scale tests needed (p. 279, 281).
- Observed: tensile loading rate, sand type and grading not reported; tensile results only as bar charts (no
  tabulated means or scatter); no crack widths in the tension tests; no durability tests; E unit misprint in Table 7.
- Observed: inconsistency between "32% w/c" (p. 271) and W/B 0.32 with 30% fly ash (Table 4); the
  micromechanics analysis (Table 2) assumes w/c levels, not W/B.
- Observed: fibre properties differ between Table 1 (1620 MPa, 43 GPa) and text (1600 MPa, 40 GPa).

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: J′b/J_tip ≈ 2.7 for saturated PSH (Kanda & Li 2002, as cited, p. 270), the same energy margin later
  published in [[kanda2006-psh-design-criteria]] for fibre-rupture-type composites. Supports the
  "sprayed ECC" processing route listed in [[li2003-ecc-review]] (p. 216, 226).
- Contradicts: ⚔️ none direct. Placed-specimen ε (about 1.2–1.6%) is far below the about 3–5% typical of cast
  PVA-ECC ([[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]]; [[li2003-ecc-review]]); different matrix (high air content, W/B 0.32).
- Extends: first primary field-processing data in the wiki (spraying, pumping, concrete-mixer production) and the
  first primary anti-spalling / corrosion-cracking comparison against a repair mortar.
