# NSC beams strengthened with no-slump HSHDC jackets (Yuan et al., 2020)

> Re-checked against the publisher PDF (16 pp.) on 2026-10-21; locations give PDF page numbers.

## One-paragraph summary
The Korea University (Yoon) group strengthens normal-strength concrete (NSC, f'c 46.2 MPa) with a
**no-slump high-strength, high-ductility concrete (NSHSDC)**: cement + silica fume + silica filler +
fine silica sand, W/B 0.172, and a **hybrid of 1.0 vol.% straight steel fiber and 0.5 vol.% PE fiber**
(28-d f'c 123.4 MPa, direct tensile strength 9.7 MPa; Table 5, p. 3). Slant shear tests (100 × 100 × 300 mm
prisms, interface at 30–75°, as-cast vs chiselled substrate) show that chiselling raises slant shear
strength by about 27%, and that high-roughness interfaces meet the ACI 546 bond range (14–21 MPa at 28 d)
except at 75° (§3.1.2, p. 10, Fig. 7). Six RC beams (125 × 250 × 2222 mm, 2 D19 bottom bars, no stirrups
except the reference B-SR) are tested in 3-point bending. Jacketing with NSHSDC raises peak load by
about 8% (top + bottom), 29% (three sides) and 40% (four sides) relative to the unstrengthened beam
B-NN (Table 8, p. 11–12; Fig. 12, p. 14). Bottom-only and top + bottom jackets do not prevent brittle shear failure;
the three-side jacket fails by concrete crushing with poor post-peak ductility; the four-side jacket
fails in flexure like the stirrup-reinforced beam, with the largest energy absorption (7.11 kN·m).
No jacket debonded from the NSC. → [[rc-member-strengthening]]

## Objective / research question
- Evaluate the interface bond (slant shear) between NSC substrate and NSHSDC overlay for two surface
  roughnesses and four interface angles (§1, p. 2; §2.2.1, p. 3). The abstract (p. 1) says "five angles", but Table 6 (p. 4) and the text list four (30°, 45°, 60°, 75°).
- Evaluate the individual (bottom) and combined (top + bottom, three sides, four sides) contributions
  of NSHSDC jacketing to the flexural capacity of NSC beams; the authors state no earlier work had
  separated the longitudinal side contributions (§1, p. 2).
- Motivation: UHPC jackets raise strength but reduce member ductility (over-reinforced behaviour) and
  need heat curing; NSHSDC is proposed as a room-cured, shape-holding alternative (§1, p. 1–2).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry:
  - Compression: ASTM C39 cylinders (size not reported); flexure: ASTM C1609 four-point (size not reported).
  - Tension: NSC by splitting (ASTM C496); NSHSDC by direct tension on dog-bone specimens per JSCE
    recommendations (geometry not reported here; refs 12–13) (§2.1, p. 2). → [[dogbone-uniaxial-tension]]
  - Slant shear: ASTM C882, 100 × 100 × 300 mm prisms, interface at 30°, 45°, 60°, 75° to the vertical;
    low roughness = as cast (NSC cleaned after 24 h); high roughness = chiselled with an electric breaker (Table 6, p. 4; Fig. 1, p. 4). 3 specimens per series (§3.1.1, p. 6).
  - Beams: 125 × 250 mm, 2222 mm long, 2 D19 bottom bars; B-SR adds Ø8 smooth U-stirrups at 101 mm
    (ACI 318-14 minimum) (§2.2.2, p. 4). Jackets: substrate chiselled to 40 mm depth at the bottom and 20 mm on sides
    and top, then NSHSDC cast in a mould (§2.2.2, p. 5; Fig. 2–3, p. 5–6). Configurations: B-NN, B-SR, B-BJ (bottom), B-2J (top + bottom),
    B-3J (two sides + bottom), B-4J (four sides).
- Loading / rate: slant shear on a 2500 kN UTM; beams in 3-point bending on a 1000 kN MTS 815 UTM, displacement
  control 0.25 mm/min (p. 5, p. 8).
- Measurement: load cell; 2 LVDTs (slant shear); mid-span LVDTs and rebar strain gauges (beams, Fig. 4, p. 6).
- Curing & test age: all specimens room-cured at 20 ± 1 °C, 60 ± 5% RH; 28 d for material and slant shear
  tests (p. 4–5, Table 5, Table 7); beam specimens cured "until the test day" (p. 5), test age not stated ⚠️.

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- Chiselling raises slant shear strength by about 27% over as-cast interfaces (§3.1.1, p. 8; Fig. 6, p. 9).
- Interface shear strength rises as the interface angle falls; 30° specimens fail by total crushing of the NSC (Table 7, p. 7; Fig. 5, p. 8). 30HS reached 0.99 of the monolithic NSC prism strength.
- High-roughness interfaces meet ACI 546 (14–21 MPa at 28 d) except at 75°; low-roughness ones meet it only at 30–45° (Fig. 7, p. 10; Conclusion 1, p. 14). 45LS (20.6 MPa) and 60HS (15.2 MPa) lie inside the 14–21 MPa band; 30LS, 30HS and 45HS exceed it (Fig. 7).
- Design codes (ACI 318-19, AASHTO-LRFD, CSA, MC2010) under-predict τ: mean τ/τ_pred 22.5 (ACI), 1.5 (AASHTO), 3.6 (CSA), 1.7 (fib) (Table 7, p. 7). ⚠️ The conclusion (p. 14) gives 1.27–2.17, which does not match Table 7; §3.1.2 (p. 10) also says code predictions were "totally higher than the test values", which contradicts ratios > 1 in Table 7.
- Peak-load gains vs B-NN: +8% (B-2J), +29% (B-3J), +40% (B-4J); vs B-SR: +6.7% (B-3J), +15.9% (B-4J) (§3.2.3, p. 13–14; Fig. 12, p. 14). B-BJ: 0.98 × B-NN; B-SR: 1.21 × B-NN (Fig. 12).
- No jacket delaminated from the NSC substrate (§3.2.1, p. 11; Conclusion 2, p. 14). Fiber pull-out "sound" was heard in B-BJ (§3.2.1, p. 11).
- First cracking: B-NN at 11.81 kN (≈ 15.6% of peak), B-SR at 12.88 kN (≈ 14.1% of peak), B-3J at 13.67 kN (≈ 1.2 × B-NN) (§3.2.1, p. 10–11).
- B-3J crushed in compression without rebar yielding, with "a considerable loss of ductility"; B-4J reached rebar strain similar to B-SR (§3.2.2, p. 13). Fig. 11 inset (p. 13), ultimate rebar strain ε (× 10⁻⁶) / load P_ε (kN): B-NN 0.77 / 75.93; B-SR 47,538 / 83.24; B-BJ 0.72 / 74.04; B-2J 0.74 / 82.19; B-3J 11.93 / 97.35; B-4J 40,771 / 100.99.

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Roughness increases mechanical interlock between NSC and NSHSDC, raising slant shear strength (§3.1.1, p. 8).
- Bottom and top + bottom jackets cannot work at full capacity because the unstrengthened NSC web cracks in shear at low load (§3.2.2–3.2.3, p. 13–14).
- Adding more jacketed sides moves the beam toward over-reinforced behaviour (stiffer, compression-controlled) (§3.2.3, p. 14, citing ref 2).
- Four-side confinement plus the top jacket allows flexural failure with wide crack distribution (§3.2.1, p. 11).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- One beam per configuration; no repeat specimens (observed).
- The paper reports no tensile stress–strain curve, strain capacity, crack width or crack count for NSHSDC; ductility is only quoted from earlier papers (observed).
- Reported rebar strains are inconsistent in magnitude ⚠️ (§3.2.2, p. 13): "0.77 × 10⁻⁶ mm/mm" at shear failure and 11.93 × 10⁻⁶ for B-3J (implausibly small); B-4J is 0.04771 mm/mm in the text but 40,771 × 10⁻⁶ (= 0.0408) in the Fig. 11 inset; B-SR first-yield strain is given as 0.024425 (p. 10) and ultimate 47,538 × 10⁻⁶ (Fig. 11). B-3J crushing load is 97.35 kN in §3.2.2 vs P_p 97.71 kN in Table 8.
- Beam test age and NSHSDC jacket curing age not stated (observed).
- NSC flexural strength (26.5 MPa) is implausibly high for a 46 MPa concrete ⚠️ (Table 5, p. 3).
- Table 8 prints initial stiffness in "kN·mm" (probably kN/mm) (observed).
- The beams lack stirrups, so most configurations fail in shear; this limits conclusions on flexural strengthening (observed).
