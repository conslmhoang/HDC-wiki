# Freeze–thaw + carbonation interaction and flexure of Eco-HDCC (Chai et al., 2019)

## One-paragraph summary
The Southeast University group (Guo Liping; first author Chai Lijuan, with Chen Bo of the Nanjing Hydraulic
Research Institute) exposed a low-cost **"ecological" PVA-HDCC** (domestic PVA, river sand, FA/C 1.5 by mass,
f'c 43.4 MPa) for **bridge-deck link slabs** to up to **15 "interaction cycles"**. One cycle = 4 d water
immersion + 10 rapid freeze–thaw cycles (+4 ↔ −18 °C) + 2 d oven drying at 60 °C + 9.7 h accelerated
carbonation (20% CO₂); one cycle is meant to equal one year in northern China (§2.3). Prisms were preloaded
to stress levels 0, 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7 before exposure. After 15 cycles, four-point **ultimate flexural strength
fell by 19–33%** (14.31 → 9.63–11.55 MPa) and **ultimate deflection by 24–50%** (2.70 → 1.36–2.05 mm), the
loss growing with preload (Table 4). Carbonation front reached 22 mm (unloaded) to 28 mm (preload 0.5/0.7)
(Fig. 4). Fine cracks (20–30 µm) **widened rather than self-healed** under the coupled exposure (§3.1). All
curves stayed deflection-hardening (four stages, Figs. 6–7). The authors recommend the curve for **15 cycles
and preload 0.5** as the design basis for link slabs (§3.4, Conclusions).

> Synthesis: same group and the same constituent family as [[chai2023-hdc-ambient-temperature]] (P·II 42.5R,
> river sand FM 1.68 / max 1.18 mm, domestic PVA 12 mm / 39 µm / ≥ 1250 MPa / 30 GPa, 26 kg/m³ fiber), but a
> **different binder split** (FA/C 1.5 here vs 0.88 in Chai 2023's HDC-1). The 2019 paper gives PVA density
> 1300 kg/m³ (Table 1), so 26 kg/m³ = 2.0 vol.% here; this supports (but does not prove) the ≈ 2 vol.%
> assumption on the Chai 2023 page. It is the wiki's **first primary coupled-exposure (freeze–thaw +
> carbonation) study of an HDC-family material**, and the first primary freeze–thaw data.

## Objective / research question
- How do interacting freeze–thaw cycles and carbonation (seasonal sequence in northern China: F–T December to
  February, carbonation March to November) change the flexural behaviour of Eco-HDCC (§1, §2.3)?
- How do pre-existing cracks (preloading stress level) affect carbonation and flexure (§1)?
- Which flexural curve should be used for link-slab design with a 15-year service life (§2.3, §3.4)?
- Stated motivation: HDCC durability under coupled factors "has been reported rarely" (p. 127).

## Materials & mix design
Constituents (§2.1, Table 1), all from Nanjing local suppliers:
- **Cement:** P·II 42.5R Portland cement.
- **Fly ash:** Class F.
- **Sand:** river sand; density 1570 kg/m³ ⚠️ (likely bulk density), fineness modulus 1.68, max 1.18 mm.
- **SP:** polycarboxylate, powder.
- **Fiber:** "normal" domestic PVA (not Kuraray K-II; chosen to cut cost to one third of "traditional HDCC",
  Zhang et al. 2014 as cited, p. 127): L 12 mm, d 39 µm, ρ 1300 kg/m³, elongation 5–8%, σ_f ≥ 1250 MPa,
  E 30 GPa (Table 1). Oil coating / surface treatment: not reported.

Units of binder quantities: Table 2 is headed "(mass ratio)", but the values (sum 2011) read as **kg/m³** ⚠️.
Ratios computed here by mass: FA/C = 1.50 (FA = 60% of binder); sand/binder = 0.30.

- Mixing (§2.2): dry solids 120 s → water, 240 s → rest 30 s → fibers added slowly, 120 s.
- Curing (§2.2): 24 h at 21 ± 5 °C in the mould, then 28 d at 23 ± 2 °C and 95 ± 2% RH.
- f'c: 43.4 MPa (100 mm cubes, n = 3; age not stated, presumably 28 d ⚠️) (§2.1).

## Test methods
- **Specimen geometry:** 100 mm cubes (compression); 100 × 100 × 400 mm prisms (flexure, carbonation, MIP).
  Five faces sealed with paraffin; only the 100 × 400 mm face in the pure-moment zone is exposed (§2.3(3)).
- **Exposure ("interaction cycle")** (§2.3(1)): 4 d water immersion + 10 rapid F–T cycles in water
  (+4 → −18 → +4 °C, 4 h per cycle; GB/T 50082-2009) + 2 d oven at 60 °C + 9.7 h carbonation (20 °C, 70% RH,
  20% CO₂). 10 lab F–T cycles ≈ 118 field cycles and 9.7 h ≈ 9 months of field carbonation (Li 2015;
  Zeng 2013, as cited). Cycles tested: 0, 1, 3, 5, 10, 15 (15-year design life of expansion joints,
  JTG D60-2015). One cycle takes about 8 d; 15 cycles = 120 d after the 28 d curing (p. 132).
- **Preloading** (§2.3(2)): stress levels 0, 0.3 (elastic limit), 0.5 (first-cracking point), 0.7 (multiple-cracking
  stage), read from the flexural stress–deflection curve; the reference stress for the ratio is not stated ⚠️.
- **Flexure:** four-point bending per CECS 13-2009, 0.2 mm/min, two LVDTs for mid-span deflection. Span not
  stated ⚠️. Number of specimens per condition not stated ⚠️.
- **Flexural toughness:** area under the load–deflection curve up to **peak** load (§3.5).
- **Carbonation front:** powder drilled at 1 mm intervals from the pure-moment zone, sieved at 0.075 mm, XRD
  for Ca(OH)₂ / CaCO₃, then DSC-TG (Ca(OH)₂ 400–550 °C, CaCO₃ 550–950 °C); front = transition point (§2.3(3), §3.2).
- **Pore structure:** MIP on un-preloaded specimens, slices every 10 mm from the exposed face, 5 mm particles
  dried at 50 °C (§2.3(4), Table 3).
- **Crack width:** observed on the surface (Fig. 2); instrument not stated ⚠️.
- **Tension:** not tested here.
- **Curing & test age:** 28 d standard curing, then exposure; the 0-cycle reference is at about 28 d, the 15-cycle
  specimens at about 148 d (computed: 28 + 120 d). Age and exposure are therefore confounded.

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Flexural strength:** falls with cycles; −19 to −33% at 15 cycles; nearly flat between 3 and 5 cycles (Table 4, p. 132).
- **Deflection is more sensitive than strength:** −24 to −50% at 15 cycles (Table 4, p. 133, 135).
- **Preload:** 0 and 0.3 differ little; 0.5 and 0.7 lose much more, especially at 10–15 cycles (Table 4, §3.4(2)).
- **Cracks:** only preload 0.7 cracked on loading (≈ 28 µm); preload 0.5 cracked after 5 cycles (20 µm). Cracks
  widened with cycles (to 28–30 µm) despite being below the < 50 µm self-healing range cited (§3.1, Fig. 2).
- **Carbonation front** grows fast for 1–5 cycles and slower for 10–15; jumps after 5 cycles for preload ≥ 0.5 (Fig. 4, §3.2).
- **Pores** (0–10 mm depth): harmless pores (< 20 nm) disappear and harmful (50–200 nm) and > 200 nm pores grow
  with cycles; damage decreases with depth (Fig. 5, §3.3). Critical aperture 0-cycle about 12 nm vs about 108 nm
  after 5–15 cycles (Fig. 5c, read ⚠️).
- **Curve shape:** all specimens keep four stages (linear, non-linear, deflection hardening, softening); the
  hardening branch shortens with cycles and preload (Figs. 6–7).
- **Flexural toughness** (to peak): about 92 kN·mm at 0 cycles; at 15 cycles about 59 / 61 / 50 / 33 kN·mm for
  preload 0 / 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 (Fig. 8, read ⚠️). One cycle slightly raises toughness for preload 0 and 0.3.
- **Design recommendation:** use the 15-cycle, preload-0.5 flexural curve for link-slab design (§3.4(1), Conclusions).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Freeze–thaw loosens the matrix and lowers the fiber/matrix bond; repeated ice-expansion stress scratches the
  (softer) PVA fibers with stripes, lowering bridging capacity (p. 131–133; fiber stripes from Chai & Xu 2018, as cited).
- Carbonation densifies the matrix and raises the interfacial bond, which by Li & Leung 1992 lowers ductility (p. 127).
- Early cycles: continued hydration, fly-ash pozzolanic reaction and carbonation partly offset damage (explains the
  3–5 cycle plateau and the toughness rise after 1 cycle) (p. 132, 135).
- Cracks give paths for CO₂ (deeper front) and water; surface scaling widens measured cracks (§3.1, §3.2).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Observed: **no tensile test**; strain hardening is inferred only from deflection hardening, which does not prove
  tensile SH (see [[flexural-strength]], [[strain-hardening]]). Tension under the same exposure is in the companion
  paper (Chai et al. 2018, CBM 186, 773–781, as cited) — not ingested.
- Observed: exposure age and curing age are confounded (0-cycle reference ≈ 28 d; 15 cycles ≈ 148 d) — no aged control.
- Observed: span, specimen count, scatter and the reference stress for the "stress level" are not reported.
- Observed: the lab-to-field equivalence (10 cycles ≈ 1 year) relies on cited theses; the text says "northern China"
  and then "northwest region" (p. 128) ⚠️.
- Observed: Table 2 labelled "mass ratio" but numerically kg/m³ ⚠️.
- Observed: MIP only on un-preloaded specimens; crack-width measurement method not given.
- Stated: order of exposure matters (F–T then carbonation is more severe; He et al. 2016, as cited); only one order tested.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: reviews that F–T resistance of ECC exceeds normal concrete (Şahmaran 2012; Nam 2016, as cited p. 126;
  see [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]]). The specimens keep deflection hardening after 150 F–T cycles plus carbonation.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ (refinement) the self-healing expectation that cracks < 50 µm heal (Yıldırım 2018; Zhu 2012/2016, as
  cited p. 129) — under coupled F–T + carbonation 20–30 µm cracks **widened** (§3.1). See [[self-healing]].
- Contradicts: ⚔️ (as cited) single F–T: ultimate deflection of 15 mm HDCC plates **rose** over 0–150 cycles (Chai & Fei
  2018); here, under coupled exposure, deflection fell monotonically (Table 4). Different exposure and specimen size.
- Extends: [[chai2023-hdc-ambient-temperature]] (same group, same domestic PVA and sand; a different FA/C).
- Extends: [[li2021-hdc-sulfate-dry-wet]] (another coupled-exposure HDC study; that one compression only, this one flexure only).
- Terminology: this paper attributes the 0.5% / 200 µm HDCC definition to **JSCE 2008**; Chai et al. 2023 give the
  same numbers from **JC/T 2461-2018**. See [[terminology-hdc-ecc-shcc]].
