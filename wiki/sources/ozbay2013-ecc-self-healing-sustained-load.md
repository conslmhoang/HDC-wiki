# Self-healing of ECC under sustained flexural load (Özbay et al., 2013)

## One-paragraph summary
Turkish–Canadian–Michigan study of whether ECC still self-heals when its cracks are held open.
Two PVA-ECC mixes, ECC-1 (FA/C 1.2, about 55% FA) and ECC-2 (FA/C 2.2, about 70% FA), W/CM 0.27,
were cast as 360 × 50 × 75 mm prisms, moist-cured 7 d then air-cured, and pre-cracked in four-point
bending to 2.5 mm mid-span deflection at 28 d (Table 1, §2.2). Specimens were then kept 30 or 90 d in
water (23 °C, still tap water) or air (50% RH), with or without a **sustained load of 60% of the
ultimate flexural load** (Fig. 3–4). Under load, average crack widths were about **twice** the
unloaded values: ECC-1 about 115 vs 60 µm, ECC-2 about 85 vs 40 µm (Table 3). In water without
sustained load, pre-cracked ECC almost fully recovered flexural strength and deflection capacity within
30 d, and stiffness reached about 87% (ECC-1) and 95% (ECC-2) of uncracked values at 90 d (Table 4,
Fig. 6). **Sustained load reduced recovery** (e.g. 90-d water stiffness 76% and 83%), more for ECC-1
whose loaded cracks exceeded 100 µm. Air-stored specimens showed no healing. Cracks 10–40 µm healed
fully, cracks > 60 µm only partly (Fig. 7). Products were CaCO₃ at 30 d and C–S–H-like gel at 90 d
(SEM/EDX, Fig. 8). The authors warn that unloaded tests may **overestimate** self-healing.

## Objective / research question
- Clarify the effect of sustained flexural loading (which widens cracks by creep) on the self-healing
  of pre-cracked ECC, in water and air, compared with unloaded and uncracked references (p. 168).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry: prisms 360 × 50 × 75 mm (flexure); 50 mm cubes (compression, ≥ 5 per mix).
- Loading / rate: four-point bending, span 304 mm, centre span 101 mm, 0.005 mm/s, closed-loop; pre-load
  to 2.5 mm mid-span deflection at 28 d (below the ≈ 5–6 mm capacity, no localisation, Fig. 1).
  Sustained load at third points by threaded rods and torque wrench calibrated to a load cell; 60% of
  ultimate flexural load; "almost no stress relaxation" over 90 d (p. 170).
- Measurement: two mid-span LVDTs; flexural stiffness = secant between 1 and 5 MPa; deflection capacity =
  deflection at MOR (residual pre-load deflection excluded, p. 174); cracks counted and measured along two
  lines with a 125× video microscope (unloaded) and portable microscope (loaded and unloaded); SEM/EDX
  on 1 cm slices; TGA of matching pastes for CH at 28 d.
- Curing & test age: demould 24 h; 7 d at 95 ± 5% RH, 23 °C in plastic bags; then air 50 ± 5% RH to
  28 d; pre-cracking at 28 d; exposure 30 or 90 d (water: immersed in still tap water at 23 ± 2 °C, no
  renewal; air: 23 ± 2 °C, 50 ± 5% RH). Each Table 4 value: average of 4–6 specimens; COV stiffness < 5%,
  strength 4.8–7.1%, deflection 6.3–11.4%.

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Water heals, air does not.** Air-stored pre-cracked prisms showed almost no stiffness gain and
  little deflection hardening on reload; water-stored ones regained deflection hardening (Fig. 5–6, p. 173–174).
- **Stiffness recovery** at 90 d water, no sustained load: about 86–87% (ECC-1) and > 95% (ECC-2) of the
  uncracked value (p. 174; Table 4 ratios 15.3/17.6 and 14.5/15.2). With sustained load: about 76% and
  83% (computed here from Table 4; Fig. 6a).
- **Strength**: pre-loading cut MOR by about 17% (ECC-1) and 10% (ECC-2) (Table 3, p. 174); water without
  sustained load restored it almost fully within 30 d; with sustained load the deficit fell to about 10%
  (30 d) and 5% (90 d) for ECC-1, 5% and 3% for ECC-2 (p. 174). Strength reflects healing least, because
  the final fracture plane had not opened.
- **Deflection capacity**: almost fully recovered in water within 30 d without sustained load; sustained
  load reduced recovery, more for ECC-1 (relative deflection about 78% vs 90% for ECC-2 at 90 d, read
  from Fig. 6c ≈) (p. 175).
- **Loaded crack width ≈ 2 × unloaded** (Table 3), consistent with Yang 2007 and Zhou 2010 (as cited).
- **Higher FA → tighter, more numerous cracks** (40 vs 60 µm unloaded), and better healing (p. 171, 175).
- Crack count fell sharply in water at 90 d (ECC-2 no-load: 40–47 → 2–7) (Table 4).
- **Healing vs crack width**: cracks 10–40 µm healed fully; > 60 µm partially; < 30 µm totally closed;
  products grow from both faces toward the middle (Fig. 7, p. 175). ECC-1 30-d water: avg width 60 → 40 µm.
- **Products**: CaCO₃ by EDX at 30 d; at 90 d also a C–S–H-like product bridging cracks < 50 µm (Fig. 8,
  p. 176–177); EDX limits acknowledged.
- **CH at 28 d** (TGA): ECC-1 4.91% (normalised to cement 10.80%), ECC-2 3.55% (11.36%) — little CH yet
  consumed by FA, so Ca²⁺ is available for calcite (Table 5, p. 175–176).
- Sustained load alone did not affect uncracked specimens; water-exposed uncracked prisms had lower
  deflection capacity than air-exposed ones (Table 4, p. 174).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Healing = CaCO₃ precipitation (Ca²⁺ leached + dissolved CO₂) early, plus continued hydration and
  pozzolanic reaction later; ongoing hydration works only if the crack faces are close (p. 175–177).
- ECC healing recovers mechanics better than concrete because cracks are tight and CaCO₃ is "reinforced"
  by micro-fibres (p. 176).
- Higher FA: lower chemical bond and matrix toughness, higher frictional bond → more, tighter cracks;
  plus a larger reservoir of unreacted binder (p. 171, 175).
- Sustained load keeps cracks wide (creep), so the products cannot bridge them (p. 174, 178).
- Self-healing in ECC is attributed to tight cracks, high binder content and low W/B (Conclusions).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: EDX interaction volume (~1 µm³) blurs phase identification (p. 177).
- Stated: unloaded tests may overestimate healing; future studies must include sustained load (p. 178).
- Observed: only one load level (60%) and one damage level (2.5 mm); continuous water, no wet–dry cycles;
  no direct tension or permeability test.
- Observed: text says Table 4 shows ECC-1 crack width > 100 µm under sustained load, but Table 4 gives only
  crack counts; the value comes from Table 3 (loaded avg about 115 µm).
- Observed: text gives the ECC-1 sustained-load strength deficit as falling "from 20%", though Table 3
  implies 17%; ECC-2 "next 60 days" vs 90-d exposure.
- Observed: PVA L = 8 mm is shorter than the usual 12 mm ⚠️.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[homma2009-frcc-self-healing]] (cracks < about 100 µm seal; CaCO₃ is the main product) and
  [[herbert2013-ecc-self-healing-natural-environment]] (water/moisture needed; high-FA ECC heals well;
  > 100% stiffness partly from bulk hydration not seen here, max ≈ 95%).
- Agrees with: review threshold "< 60 µm heals fast" and healing faster for small cracks (as cited in
  [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]], p. 3); "self-healing threshold below 100 µm, preferably < 50 µm" (as cited, p. 167).
- Extends / ⚔️ refines: the wiki assumption that ECC cracks (< 100 µm, measured unloaded) are always
  "self-healing": **in service under load** they are about 2× wider (ECC-1 about 115 µm), above the healing
  limit. This affects the crack-width thresholds in [[crack-width]], which are all unloaded/residual values.

- Same mixes (FA/C 1.2 and 2.2) as the M45 family ([[yang2008-ecc-fiber-bridging-law]]) and Herbert & Li 2013 (FA/C 2.2).
