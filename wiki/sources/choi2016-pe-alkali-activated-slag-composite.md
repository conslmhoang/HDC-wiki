# PE-fibre alkali-activated slag composite with 7.5% tensile strain (Choi et al., 2016)

## One-paragraph summary
**Experimental** study from Chonnam National University (Gwangju, Korea) with Ranade (Buffalo), Li (Michigan) and Daejeon
University. Four **cementless** composites were made from GGBS activated by powdered calcium hydroxide (8.38% of GGBS mass)
and sodium sulfate (3.35%), with **no aggregate**, 1.75 vol.% PE fibre (18 mm × 12 µm, 2700 MPa) and W/B 0.26 / 0.30 /
0.34 / 0.38; HRWRA and VMA were dosed to control matrix viscosity for fibre dispersion (§2.1, Tables 1–3). At 28 d, all mixes
strain-hardened with multiple cracking. The best mix M26 (W/B 0.26) reached f'c 54.8 MPa, first-cracking strength 4.87 MPa,
**tensile strength 13.06 MPa and tensile strain capacity 7.50%** (JSCE dogbone, 80 mm gauge) (Tables 5, 7). Raising W/B
lowered all properties monotonically (M38: 36.3 / 3.37 / 5.06 MPa, 4.58%). The tensile-to-compressive strength ratio averaged
19.8% (23.8% for M26), about double that of normal concrete, while the first-crack-to-compressive ratio (9.2%) was concrete-like
(§3.3). Average crack width (computed from strain and crack count) was 89–110 µm, mean 101 µm; crack spacing 1.20–2.25 mm
(Table 8).

> Synthesis: the strongest evidence in the wiki so far that **high ductility and high tensile strength do not need a
> high-strength, cement-rich matrix**: 13 MPa and 7.5% at f'c ≈ 55 MPa, without cement, silica fume, aggregate or heat curing.
> It parallels Tongji [[uhdc]] (PE in a ≈ 37 MPa matrix, 9.45%) and supports the "trade-off is not fundamental" row in [[gaps]].
> The material is not called HDC/ECC; it qualifies under the wiki scope rule (tensile strain hardening with multiple cracking).

## Objective / research question
- Improve fibre-reinforced alkali-activated slag composites by switching from PVA (earlier work: ≤ 4.5%, Lee et al. 2012, as
  cited) to PE fibre and by optimising the mix, and characterise the "ultra-high" tensile ductility (§1, last paragraph).
- Effect of W/B (0.26–0.38) on density, compressive strength and tensile behaviour (§2.1).

Units of binder quantities: mass ratio to binder (Table 1); fibre by volume. Whether "binder" includes the activator powders is
implied by §2.1 ("GGBS and the alkali activator ... were used as binding materials") but not stated explicitly ⚠️.
- GGBS: Blaine 4320 cm²/g, density 2.92 g/cm³; CaO 44.6%, SiO₂ 31.5%, Al₂O₃ 13%, MgO 4.9%, SO₃ 3.4% (XRF, Table 2).
- PE fibre: d 12 µm, L 18 mm (L/d 1500, computed), σ_f 2700 MPa, E 88 GPa, density 0.97 g/cm³ (Table 3).
- Aggregate deliberately excluded because it raises matrix toughness (§2.1). Viscosity tuned following Li & Li (2013) (§2.1).
- Mixing: Hobart mixer; dry powders 3 min, water 3 min, HRWRA/VMA, then fibres; ≈ 10 min total (§2.2).

## Test methods
- **Specimen geometry:** JSCE dogbone, 30 × 13 mm section in the gauge length, gauge length 80 mm (measured per specimen);
  4 specimens per mix (§2.4, Fig. 1). Compression: three 50 mm cubes per mix (ASTM C109-07).
- **Loading / rate:** electronic universal testing machine, displacement control, 0.1 mm/min (§2.4).
- **Measurement:** two LVDTs on both sides; cracks counted manually on unloaded specimens; average crack width = gauge
  elongation / number of cracks (all deformation assumed to be crack opening); spacing = gauge / number (§3.3).
- **Density:** Archimedes, saturated-surface-dry cubes at 28 d (Eq. 1).
- **Curing & test age:** moulds covered with plastic, 2 d in air at 23 ± 3 °C, then water at 23 ± 3 °C "for 28 days"; tests at
  28 d (§2.2–2.3) ⚠️ (whether age is counted from casting or from demoulding is unclear). No heat curing.
→ [[dogbone-uniaxial-tension]]

## Key results

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M26 | 54.8 ± 1.5 (50 mm cube) | 4.87 ± 0.46 | 13.06 ± 2.40 | 7.50 ± 0.45 | 88.9 (avg, computed) | 67.3 cracks, spacing 1.20 mm; density 2.03 g/cm³ |
| M30 | 49.0 ± 3.2 | 4.61 ± 0.26 | 11.46 ± 1.05 | 6.33 ± 0.38 | 106.9 | 47.7 cracks, 1.70 mm; 1.99 g/cm³ |
| M34 | 42.3 ± 2.4 | 3.92 ± 0.37 | 7.64 ± 1.08 | 5.35 ± 0.41 | 109.8 | 40.3 cracks, 2.03 mm; 1.95 g/cm³ |
| M38 | 36.3 ± 1.0 | 3.37 ± 0.34 | 5.06 ± 0.94 | 4.58 ± 1.31 | 99.3 | 36.6 cracks, 2.25 mm; 1.89 g/cm³ |

All at 28 d (Tables 4, 5, 7, 8). Strain capacity appears to be at peak stress (Fig. 2 curves peak near the reported strains,
read ⚠️); the text also says "ultimate tensile strain" (§2.4).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Strain hardening in all four mixes**, with multiple micro-cracks at < 2.25 mm spacing (§3.3, Figs. 2, 4; Table 8).
- **W/B effect:** relative to M26, tensile strength −12.3 / −41.5 / −61.3% and strain capacity −15.6 / −28.7 / −38.9% for
  M30 / M34 / M38; f'c falls 54.8 → 36.3 MPa; first-crack strength 4.87 → 3.37 MPa (Tables 5, 7, §3.3). Same sensitivity as
  PVA alkali-activated composites (Lee 2012, as cited).
- **Strength ratios:** first-crack / f'c = 8.88 / 9.41 / 9.26 / 9.27% (mean 9.20%, "similar to normal concrete"); σ_tu / f'c =
  23.8 / 23.4 / 18.1 / 13.9% (mean 19.8%) (§3.3). Tensile strength is a fibre-bridging property, not a matrix property.
- **"Stress performance index"** computed as σ_tu / first-crack strength: 2.68 / 2.49 / 1.95 / 1.50 (mean 2.16); the number of
  cracks falls with this index (§3.3, Table 8). ⚠️ This is the macroscopic ratio, not the micromechanical σ0/σ_fc of Kanda & Li.
- **Crack width:** mean 101 µm over the four mixes (88.9–109.8 µm); M26 has the most cracks, smallest width and spacing,
  attributed to higher interfacial frictional bond in the strongest matrix (§3.3, Table 8). These are average widths at the
  end of loading computed from elongation, not measured residual widths.
- **Comparison with other composites** (§3.3, Fig. 3, Table 6):
  - vs PE-ECC (first crack 3 MPa, σ_tu 5.5 MPa, ε ≈ 6%; Li et al. 2001 and Maalej & Li 1994, as cited): M26 σ_tu > 2×, σ_tu/σ_fc
    2.68 vs 1.8.
  - vs PVA-ECC (4.5 MPa, ≈ 4%; Li et al. 2001, as cited): 2.9× strength, 1.9× strain.
  - vs Michigan HSHD concrete (f'c 166 MPa, first crack 8.3 MPa, σ_tu 14.5 MPa, ε 3.4%; Ranade et al. 2013, as cited): lower σ_tu
    but σ_tu/f'c 23.8% vs 8.7%.
  - Tensile "toughness" (to end of hardening): 0.0025 (Li 2001), 0.0019 (Lee 2012), 0.0038 (Ranade 2013), 0.0067 MPa·mm/mm
    (M26) → 2.7× and 1.8× (Table 6) ⚠️ (units/magnitude: the area under a 13 MPa × 7.5% curve is ≈ 0.6 MPa).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Excluding aggregate lowers matrix toughness, making PSH easier; VMA/HRWRA control rheology for uniform PE dispersion (§2.1).
- Lower W/B → stronger matrix → higher interfacial frictional bond → more cracks, tighter crack width, higher bridging strength
  (§3.3, citing Yang et al. 2007).
- PSH requires σ_fc < σ0 and J_tip < J′b (Li & Leung 1992; Kanda & Li 2006, as cited); a larger stress index means more
  saturated cracking (§3.3). → [[strain-hardening]]
- The composite needs no silica fume, filler, very low W/B or special curing, unlike UHPC/HSHD concrete (§3.3).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: mechanical, physical, chemical and rheological properties still need evaluation before construction use (§3.3, last paragraph).
- Only one binder/activator combination and one fibre type/content; no single-fibre pull-out, matrix toughness or bridging
  tests, so the "stress performance index" is not the micromechanical one.
- Conclusion 1 states σ_tu "5.06 MPa to 7.5 MPa", contradicting Table 7 (5.06–13.06 MPa) — typo.
- Text calls the 0.0025 toughness row "PE-ECC" while Table 6 attributes it to Li et al. 2001 (PVA-ECC); toughness magnitudes
  look ≈ 100× too small for the stated unit.
- Crack widths are computed (elongation / count), so they mix elastic and crack opening and are loaded widths at peak.
- High scatter in M26 σ_tu (± 2.40 MPa) and M38 strain (± 1.31%); n = 4.
- No shrinkage, durability or long-term data; alkali-activated slag shrinkage is a known concern ([general knowledge — needs source]).
- 13 mm-thick JSCE dogbones favour 2-D orientation of 18 mm fibres; values are likely upper bounds for thick members (see
  [[kanakubo2006-dfrcc-tensile-test-method]], [[kunieda2010-uhp-shcc-steel-reinforcement]]).

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[uhdc]] / [[ye2021-uhdc-flexural-size-effect]] — PE in a moderate-strength (≈ 37–55 MPa) matrix gives very high
  strain (9.45% / 7.50%). Agrees with the "trade-off not fundamental" synthesis in [[tensile-strain-capacity]] and [[gaps]].
- Agrees with: [[ranade2017-hshdc-impact-resistance]] on the quoted Michigan HSHDC values (166 MPa, 14.5 MPa, 3.4%) — both quote
  Ranade et al. 2013; still ⚔️ against 4.2% in [[ranade2015-hshdc-tensile-rate-effects]] (existing conflict, not new).
- Contradicts: ⚔️ W/B direction — here lower W/B raises **both** strength and strain capacity (0.38 → 0.26: ε 4.58 → 7.50%),
  as in [[sun2019-modified-pva-fiber-ecc]], but opposite to [[zhao2022-ecc-tension-damage-model]] and
  [[chai2023-hdc-ambient-temperature]] (lower W/B less ductile). Different binder (cementless slag, no aggregate), fibre (PE) and
  W/B range. → [[water-binder-ratio]]
- Contradicts: ⚔️ [[shumuye2022-slag-fa-pe-ecc]] reports that slag widens cracks in PE-ECC; here an all-slag (alkali-activated)
  matrix gives 89–110 µm average computed widths — comparable to PE-HSHDC (80–200 µm) rather than PVA-ECC; not a direct conflict
  (different binder systems), but slag + PE gives ≈ 100 µm cracks in both.
- Extends: [[ggbfs]] (first cementless, alkali-activated GGBS composite in the wiki); [[pe-fiber]] (12 µm × 18 mm, L/d 1500);
  [[hshdc]] (moderate-strength alternative with similar σ_tu).
