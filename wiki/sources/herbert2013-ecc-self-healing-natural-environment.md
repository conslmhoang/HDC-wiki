# ECC self-healing outdoors over one year (Herbert & Li, 2013)

## One-paragraph summary
University of Michigan study in which 60 PVA-ECC coupons (high-volume fly ash, FA/C 2.2) were
pre-strained in direct tension to **0.5% or 1.0%** at 7 d and then left **outdoors in Ann Arbor for
up to 12 months** (Sept 2011–Sept 2012, −11.7 to +32.8 °C, rain and snow) (§2.2–2.3). The mix was chosen
for tight cracks: average residual crack widths **24 µm at 0.5% and 30 µm at 1.0%** strain (§2.1).
Healing was tracked weekly by longitudinal resonant frequency (RF, ASTM C215) normalised to uncracked
controls under the same exposure, and by tensile reloading at 3, 6, 9 and 12 months (Sets 1–4); Set 1
was also re-damaged at each reloading. Normalised RF recovered to **90–96% after the first week** and
then fluctuated between **95 and 105%** (Fig. 4), possibly from washout and re-formation of Ca(OH)₂ in
rain. Stiffness and first-cracking-strength recovery rose with exposure time: stiffness 62% at 3 months
(0.5% pre-strain), often > 100% after 6 months; first-cracking strength about 80% at 3 months and **116%
at 12 months** for both strain levels (§3.2.1, Fig. 6). Under repeated damage, RF still recovered to
88% (0.5%) and 84% (1.0%) after the third reloading (Fig. 5), but mechanical recovery varied with the
season: it tracked **average temperature** very consistently and precipitation reasonably (Fig. 8).

## Objective / research question
- Determine to what extent ECC self-heals in the natural environment, over one year, and whether
  self-healing persists under repeated damage (Abstract; §1). Foundation for field exploitation of
  self-healing.

## Materials & mix design
Type I OPC; Class F fly ash; silica sand, average particle size 110 µm; polycarboxylate HRWRA;
Kuraray-type PVA fibre (oiled 1.2% by weight): L 12 mm, d 39 µm, σ_f 1600 MPa, ρ 1300 kg/m³,
E 42.8 GPa, elongation 6% (§2.1).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry: coupons cast 300 × 76 × 12.5 mm, cut to 200 mm length; aluminium plates glued to
  ends (§2.2). 60 specimens: 4 sets × (5 control + 5 at 0.5% + 5 at 1.0%).
- Loading / rate: uniaxial tension, 25 kN frame, displacement control 0.5 mm/min; pre-load at 7 d;
  reloads to the same strain (§2.2, §2.4.2).
- Measurement: two LVDTs (elongation; gauge length not reported); RF by ASTM C215 longitudinal mode,
  average of 3 readings, weekly; secant stiffness between 0.5 and 2.0 MPa; first-cracking strength per
  JSCE HPFRCC recommendation (2008) (§2.4, §3.2).
- Curing & test age: 24 h in mould under plastic, then air-cured in the lab until pre-loading at 7 d;
  then outdoor exposure 3 / 6 / 9 / 12 months.
- Recovery indices: RF ratio (Eq. 1); normalised RF = RF_preloaded / RF_control under same exposure
  (Eq. 2); stiffness recovery = reload / preload (Eq. 3); first-cracking strength recovery (Eq. 4).
→ [[dogbone-uniaxial-tension]] (coupon variant)

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- Self-healing of ECC occurs outdoors despite 0.5–1.0% pre-strain and Michigan winter (Conclusion 1).
- RF recovery is fastest in the first week, as in the lab (§3.1.1).
- Mechanical recovery grows with exposure time; > 100% values include bulk hydration, but the reloaded
  first-cracking strength never exceeds that of an uncracked control with the same exposure (§3.2.1).
- With repeated damage the recovery depends on the weather in the preceding 3 months: strongly on mean
  temperature, less clearly on precipitation (Fig. 8). The 6- and 9-month reloads (autumn–winter)
  gave the lowest recovery.
- Stiffness of unhealed control specimens reloaded immediately is low: debonded fibres are slack (§3.2.1).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Intrinsic healing relies on continued hydration, pozzolanic reaction and carbonation producing C–S–H
  and CaCO₃ in cracks < 100 µm (§1); both CaCO₃ and hydration of unreacted cement matter in ECC
  (Kan et al. 2010, as cited).
- RF fluctuation: Ca(OH)₂ formed in cracks dissolves in rain and re-forms; the slow pozzolanic reaction
  of the high fly-ash content may not convert it to stable C–S–H fast enough (§3.1.1).
- Stiffness > 100%: healing of cracks plus continued hydration of the bulk (springs-in-series argument, §3.2.1).
- Large scatter: each specimen has a different crack-width distribution; wider cracks heal less (§3.2.1).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: more research needed to reach 100% recovery after several damage events (§3.2.2).
- Observed: one climate and one mix; no crack-width measurement after healing; no permeability test;
  no product characterisation (SEM/XRD).
- Observed: air curing to 7 d means much unhydrated cement and fly ash at cracking, which favours healing;
  results may overstate healing of mature ECC.
- Observed: figure values (Fig. 4–8) not available in the XML; only text values used here.
- Observed: absolute stresses, strains at failure and f'c not reported.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: reviewed claim that ECC self-heals and that healing depends on crack width (as cited in
  [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]], p. 3). Confirms in a field setting the wet–dry healing reported
  second-hand (Ma 2014; Yang 2009 lab, as cited).
- Agrees with: [[homma2009-frcc-self-healing]]: fastest healing in the first days of water exposure;
  < 100 µm cracks heal.
- Refines: ⚔️ (minor) the review statement that ageing reduces healing ([[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]],
  p. 9): here, recovery *increased* with exposure time up to 12 months — but cracking was at 7 d, so this
  is not a test of healing in aged ECC. Not a strict conflict.
- Note: crack widths 24–30 µm are well below PVA-ECC values in [[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]]
  (43–88 µm), because of the very high fly-ash content (FA/C 2.2).
- Extends: first natural-environment and repeated-damage healing data in the wiki.
