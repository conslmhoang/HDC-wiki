# HSHDCC review: design, production, properties (Zhang et al., 2022)

## One-paragraph summary
Hunan University (Caijun Shi, Zemei Wu) with UBC (Banthia) review "HSHDCC", which they define as a
strain-hardening cementitious composite with **f'c > 100 MPa and tensile strain capacity > 3%**
(p. 2). The review covers:
- design theory: ECC micromechanics (strength and energy criteria, PSH indices) plus dense-packing
  for strength (Section 2);
- raw materials: binder, fine aggregate and flaws, fiber, fiber–matrix interface (Section 3,
  Tables 1–4);
- workability (mini-slump 145–200 mm typical, Table 5);
- mechanical properties: compression, flexure, tension, test methods, size effect (Section 5,
  Tables 6–7, Figs. 9–22);
- a simple cradle-to-gate CO₂, energy and cost estimate for 15 HSHDCC mixes against normal ECC and
  UHPC (Section 6, Tables 8–10).

Headline claims: PE fiber is "mandatory" (p. 16, conclusion 2); PVA and mono-steel fiber are
unsuitable in high-strength matrices (p. 4); best reported values are f'c over 210 MPa with ε_tu
over 5% (Huang 2021, as cited), σ_tu 19.2 MPa (Yu 2018, as cited) and ε_tu 11.0% (Li 2020, as
cited) (p. 7). The authors state there is **no field deployment** of HSHDCC yet (p. 2). All
quantitative data are second-hand.

## Objective / research question
- Review mixture design, workability control, mechanical properties, and environmental and cost
  impacts of HSHDCC with f'c > 100 MPa and ε_tu > 3% (p. 2).
- The authors position it against earlier reviews (p. 2):
  - Yoo & Banthia 2021: "high-performance SHCC", f'c > 55 MPa, ε_tu > 4%;
  - Ding et al. 2022: "HS-ECC", f'c > 80 MPa, ε_tu > 2%;
  - Dai et al. 2021: strain-hardening UHPC, ε_tu > 1%.
- They state that workability has been "scarcely discussed" (p. 2).

## Test methods
This is a review; no own tests. Methods summarised from the literature:
- Specimen geometry:
  - Tension (Table 7, p. 11): JSCE dogbone, 80 mm gauge, 12.7 mm thick, ≥ 5 specimens, 0.5 mm/min
    (most common; [10,12,59]); Ø75 mm cylinders at 3 mm/min [77]; 40 mm-thick dogbone [79];
    thin (20 × 30 mm gauge section) and thick (60 × 100 mm) dogbones [99].
  - Flexure (Table 6, p. 10): 3-point (ASTM C1609, GB/T 17671) and 4-point (ASTM C1609, CECS 13:2009)
    on 40 × 40 × 160, 100 × 100 × 400/500, 15 × 75 × 300, 15 × 100 × 400 and
    10.16 × 10.16 × 35.56 mm (sic, likely 101.6 mm ⚠️) prisms.
  - Compression: Ø50 × 100 mm cylinders and 50.8 mm cubes (Fig. 11).
  - Interface: single-fiber pullout, embedment 0.6–9 mm, 1–50 µm/s (Table 4).
- Loading / rate: tension 0.02–3 mm/min across studies (p. 10); flexure 50–1000 µm/min (Table 6).
- Measurement (LVDT, DIC, ...): not reported (review).
- Curing & test age: varies; ambient water curing to 90 °C water for 9 d [13]. Ranade 2011 values at
  14 d; Huang 2021 at 12 d; Yu 2019 at 28 d (p. 2).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Definition:** HSHDCC = SHCC with f'c > 100 MPa and ε_tu > 3%; thresholds derived from the
  data compiled in Section 5 (p. 2).
- **Comparison table** (Table 1, p. 4), as given by the authors:

  | | Normal FRC | ECC | UHPC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Design principle | conventional | micromechanics + fracture mechanics | close packing |
  | Fiber | various, < 2%, d ≈ 0.5 mm | PE/PVA, 2%, d < 0.05 mm | steel 2–5%, d ≈ 0.2 mm |
  | W/B | ≈ 0.5 | ≈ 0.34 | ≤ 0.24 |
  | f'c | 20–60 MPa | 40–70 MPa | ≥ 150 MPa |
  | Tensile failure | strain-softening | strain-hardening, multiple steady-state cracking | strain-hardening at low strain, one major crack |
  | ε_tu | < 0.1% | 3–7% | < 1.0% |
  | Fracture energy | < 10 J/m² | 25–30 kJ/m² | 20–40 kJ/m² |
  | Crack width | unconstrained | < 100 µm | crack propagates continuously up to about 1.0% strain, then constraint decreases (paraphrased) |

- Normal ECC f'c is below 70 MPa (p. 1); ECC residual crack width < 100 µm (p. 1).
- Cement should have C₃S + C₂S > 65% (p. 3). Fine silica sand 0.18–1.18 mm at s/b 0.3–0.7 was
  typical in early HSHDCC (p. 4, Fig. 4).
- **Fiber choice** (p. 4): PE (L/d 300–900, 2 vol.%) preferred. Steel: aspect ratio too low for
  enough σ0 and J′b. PVA: hydrophilic, bonds too strongly, ruptures prematurely.
- **PSH targets** with PE fiber: PSH_strength > 1.2 and PSH_energy > 3 (p. 2, after Li et al. and
  Kanda & Li).
- **Interface** (Table 4, p. 8): PE τ0 0.42–2.68 MPa (uncoated). τ0 roughly tracks f'c because of
  denser ITZ, e.g. 1.38 → 1.62 MPa as f'c 128 → 148 MPa (p. 6). Coatings raise bond: CNF +22% [16],
  GO +71.2% [17], PDA +90% [18] (p. 5–6). Chromic acid, plasma and ozone treatments raise bond but
  lower fiber strength (p. 5).
- PE has almost zero chemical bond with the matrix and shows slip-hardening after debonding (p. 5).
- **Workability** (Table 5, p. 9): mini-slump 145–200 mm typical; LC³ raises it; covalently
  grafted SF gave a matrix mini-slump up to 335 mm [37]; coarse sand (1.18–4.75 mm) 160–180 mm vs
  fine sand (< 0.3 mm) 145 mm (p. 6).
- **Compression** (p. 7–9): strategies are packing, hydration/pozzolanic reaction, smaller max
  aggregate (124 → 132 MPa for 4.75 → 1.18 mm [59]), heat curing (210 MPa, 90 °C water 9 d [13]),
  and steel fiber (+51% at 2% [61]). Compressive strain capacity 2.8–4.1‰ [47,80,93]; ductile
  post-peak with tiny cracks around the main crack (Fig. 11).
- **Flexure** (p. 10): flexural strength 15.5–33.5 MPa [12,94]; deflection up to 1/10 of span
  (slabs, [37]) and 1/50 (prisms, [12,80]). Flexural deflection-hardening does not prove tensile
  strain-hardening (p. 9–10).
- **Tension** (p. 10–14): first-crack stress is matrix-controlled; σ_tu and ε_tu are
  fiber-bridging-controlled. Strong bond can backfire: 17% RHA [48] and 2% nano-SiO₂ [14] gave the
  highest σ_tu but the lowest ε_tu, with about twice as many ruptured fibers (Fig. 16). Blending PE
  lengths improves σ_tu and ε_tu [63]. Flaw materials (river sand, GPA, CBA) raise ε_tu but lower
  σ_tu (p. 12).
- **Size effect in tension** [99]: thick dogbones give more stable curves; thick-to-thin
  conversion coefficient 0.69 for σ_tu and 0.94 for compressive stress (p. 11) ⚔️ (see To verify).
- **Environment and cost** (Table 10, p. 17): HSHDCC 523–1169 kg CO₂/m³, 5.97–10.26 GJ/m³,
  641–1036 $/m³. Normal ECC [74]: 910 kg/m³, 8.84 GJ/m³, 632 $/m³ (f'c "≈57" estimated, not
  measured). UHPC [135]: 759 kg/m³, 5.38 GJ/m³, 348 $/m³ at 145 MPa. Per MPa, HSHDCC carbon
  intensity (4.29–10.10 kg/m³/MPa) is below normal ECC (15.96). PE fiber: 4080 kg CO₂/t,
  27 000 $/t; PVA 3430 kg/t, 15 000 $/t; steel 170 kg/t, 654 $/t (Table 8). Lowest impact: [61]
  (GGBS/C 1.07, 1.5% PE); highest: [62] (no SCM other than SF).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Strength comes from UHPC-style dense packing, low W/B, pozzolanic SCMs and (optionally) heat
  curing; ductility from ECC micromechanics (σc < σ0; J_tip ≤ J′b, J_tip ≈ Km²/Em) (Eqs. 1–4, p. 2).
- Raising f'c raises J_tip faster than J′b, which can kill ductility; fiber coating raises J′b
  without raising J_tip (p. 6, p. 16).
- Low lateral modulus and water absorption of PE lower composite f'c relative to the matrix
  (p. 2, p. 9).
- Low s/b reduces Km, but too low s/b raises cement content and microcracking and weakens bond (p. 12).
- Coarse river sand acts as a "flaw" through a weak aggregate–paste bond, giving saturated
  multiple cracking (p. 4).
- Thick dogbones: 3-D fiber orientation and more initial defects lower matrix toughness, giving
  better strain-hardening (p. 11).
- Hybrid PE + steel: steel restrains crack width and raises compression; PE gives bridging.
  Too much steel worsens dispersion and orientation (p. 13–14).
- Short PE fibers bridge microcracks first; long fibers carry load once macrocracks form (p. 14).
- Multiple fine cracks plus large unhydrated cement content give high self-healing potential (p. 15).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: no field deployment (p. 2); transport excluded from LCA, industrial wastes assumed
  zero-impact, normal ECC f'c estimated (Tables 8, 10 notes); test methods not uniform (p. 17).
- Observed:
  - All data second-hand; no new experiments. Fig. 9 values are only readable approximately.
  - The definition (> 100 MPa, > 3%) is the authors' own and some compiled mixes fall outside it
    (e.g. [16] ≈ 2.3% in Fig. 9; [61] f'c 100 MPa; [37] σ_tu only 4.9–6.6 MPa).
  - Several internal inconsistencies (see To verify): highest σ_tu 19.2 vs 18.2 MPa;
    conversion coefficient wording; Fig. 10 text vs figure; Table 4 citation of [14]/[15];
    Table 3 [77] geometry; Table 2 [50] sums above 100%.
  - Durability is not reviewed with data; only self-healing is argued qualitatively.
  - Figs. 11 and 14 have mislabeled axes ("Strain capacity (µm)", and "Compressive stress" on a
    tensile plot) ⚠️.
  - Michigan (Ranade) lineage treated as the origin of HSHDCC; Chinese "HDC/HSHDC" groups (Xi'an,
    GDUT) are not reviewed.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with:
  - [[ranade2017-hshdc-impact-resistance]] and [[ranade2015-hshdc-tensile-rate-effects]]: Michigan
    HSHDC, about 160–166 MPa, PE, 90 °C curing (Ranade 2011/2013, as cited).
  - [[yang2024-hshdc-uniaxial-tension]]: PE far better than PVA in high-strength matrices (p. 4).
  - [[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]]: room-temperature HSHDCC is feasible; most compiled mixes are
    not heat-cured.
  - [[he2024-hdc-research-advances]]: UHS-UHDCC term (Lei 2019, Lei 2021, Wu 2021, as cited here).
- Contradicts: ⚔️
  - Michigan HSHDC ε_tu: a third value, 3.5% at 14 d (Ranade 2011, as cited), alongside 3.4%
    ([[ranade2017-hshdc-impact-resistance]]) and 4.2% ([[ranade2015-hshdc-tensile-rate-effects]]).
  - Michigan HSHDC fiber diameter: Table 3 lists 38 µm (L/d 334) for Ranade 2011 and 28 µm
    (L/d 454) for Ranade 2013; the wiki records 28 µm only.
  - Michigan HSHDC binder: Table 2 gives cement 72 / SF 28 for [10], Table 5 gives 80 / 20 for
    [11,80].
  - Normal ECC f'c: "< 70 MPa" (p. 1) and 40–70 MPa (Table 1) vs "normal-strength HDC 30–80 MPa"
    in [[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]].
  - Heat curing: the review lists high-temperature curing as a strength strategy and its effect on
    ductility as unclear (p. 17); [[heat-curing]] says heat is not required.
- Extends:
  - Gives an explicit quantitative HSHDCC threshold (f'c > 100 MPa, ε_tu > 3%) and the thresholds
    of three other reviews → [[terminology-hdc-ecc-shcc]].
  - Adds hybrid PE + steel, fiber coatings (CNF, GO, PDA), sea sand / river sand, LC³, RHA, CKD, RFP,
    and an LCA/cost comparison not yet in the wiki.
