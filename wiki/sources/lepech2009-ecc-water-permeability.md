# Water permeability of cracked ECC (Lepech & Li, 2009)

## One-paragraph summary
Lepech and Li designed **ECC M45** (PVA, FA/C 1.2) to keep crack widths below a **100 µm
permeability threshold** taken from Wang et al. 1997, then measured water permeability of ECC
tension plates (300 × 75 × 12 mm) pre-strained to **1.5, 2 and 3%** and of steel-mesh reinforced
mortar plates strained to 1.5% (§2–3). Loaded ECC crack widths were **40–70 µm** and closed about **10%
(5–10 µm)** on unloading (p. 749). Permeability was tested unloaded from 28 d. Cracked ECC gave
**k = 1.95 × 10⁻¹⁰, 3.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ and 7.74 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s** at 1.5, 2 and 3% strain (crack widths 50, 52,
63 ± 10 µm; 47, 59, 87 cracks) vs **8.18 × 10⁻¹² m/s** uncracked. Normalised per crack, k was
**4.15–8.90 × 10⁻¹² m/s**, i.e. close to uncracked (Table 5). Reinforced mortar rose from
4.58 × 10⁻¹¹ m/s (uncracked) to **4.46 × 10⁻⁴ m/s at ≈ 500 µm** cracks (Table 5). k of cracked ECC fell by
about two orders of magnitude during the test because of **self-healing (CaCO₃)** that appeared only
under **flowing** water, not in 14 d of static saturation (Fig. 7, p. 751–752). So the Table 5 ECC values
are asymptotic, partly healed values.

## Objective / research question
- Establish the micromechanical basis for tailoring ECC with crack width below 100 µm, verify tight
  crack widths in tension, and establish a crack width vs permeability relation for multiple-cracking
  materials compared with steel-reinforced mortar (p. 745).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry: tension plates 300 × 75 × 12 mm, gauge 180 mm (p. 748); notched matrix prisms
  75 × 38 × 300 mm, notch 2 mm wide × 30 mm deep, for Km (p. 747); single-fiber pullout from M45 matrix
  (Redon et al. method) at 14 d.
- Loading / rate: uniaxial tension to 1.5% (first round) or 0–3% (second round), following Li et al.
  2002; rate not reported. Reinforced mortar strained to 1.5% (p. 748).
- Measurement (LVDT, DIC, ...): crack widths at 30 locations by optical microscope, unloaded; falling-
  head (low k) and constant-head (high k) permeameters adapted from Wang et al. 1997 and Cernica 1982
  (Eqs. 5–6, Fig. 4; a = 2.84 × 10⁻⁵ m², L = 0.012 m, A = 8.93 × 10⁻³ m², Table 4); ESEM/EDX on
  healing products.
- Curing & test age: tension plates air-cured 14 d, tensioned, then 14 d in water; permeability
  testing started at **28 d** and ran to ≈ 55–70 d (Fig. 7). Pullout tests at 14 d (wet curing);
  matrix prisms air-cured 14 d.

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Micromechanics**: J′b/J_tip ≈ **1.08** for M45, treated as a lower bound (p. 749). Design aim:
  δ0 (crack opening at peak bridging stress σ0) < 100 µm (Eqs. 2–4, Fig. 2).
- Loaded ECC crack widths **40–70 µm** (p. 749); Table 3 gives 40–80 µm with spacing 2–5 mm for ECC
  vs 125–500 µm and 5–30 mm for reinforced mortar.
- **Unloading closes ECC cracks by about 10% of the loaded width (5–10 µm)**; reported widths are
  unloaded (p. 749).
- ECC at 1.5% strain: k nearly **five orders** lower than similarly strained mortar with ACI/AASHTO-size
  cracks (300–350 µm) (p. 750). Conclusion: up to **six orders** lower than reinforced mortar at 1.5%.
- Raw k of cracked ECC is about one order above uncracked, but per-crack k matches a single 60 µm crack
  in concrete (1.00 × 10⁻¹¹ m/s, Wang et al. 1997) (p. 750).
- k rises roughly linearly on a log scale with strain from 0 to 3%; normalised k stays near
  10⁻¹¹–10⁻¹² m/s (Fig. 6).
- Reinforced mortar k rose by nearly **seven orders** from uncracked to ≈ 500 µm (p. 749). Fig. 5 plots k
  against **maximum** crack width.
- **Self-healing during the test**: k of cracked ECC fell from ≈ 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ m/s at the start to the
  asymptotic Table 5 values within ≈ 20–30 d (Fig. 7 ⚠️ read). White residue formed in cracks and on
  surface pores (Fig. 8); ESEM: mainly calcium carbonate (Fig. 9).
- **No healing in 14 d of static water saturation**; healing appeared after 3 d of flow in the
  permeameter, and in partially submerged specimens only near the water surface (p. 752). Authors
  conclude a supply of carbonate / bicarbonate (flowing water or dissolved CO₂) is needed.

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Flow through a crack follows parallel-plate laminar flow ∝ (crack width)³, reduced by a tortuosity
  coefficient n (Tsukamoto 1990, Eq. 1); fibers reduce both n and crack width (p. 745).
- Steady-state flat cracking keeps width ≤ δ0; a new crack forms before any crack exceeds δ0 (energy
  criterion J′b ≥ J_tip ≈ Km²/Em, strength criterion σ0 > σ_fc, Eqs. 2–3). → [[steady-state-cracking]]
- Crack width is a material property of ECC, uncoupled from reinforcement ratio (p. 745).
- Self-healing is favoured by high binder content, low W/B and unhydrated binder, and needs a
  carbonate supply (p. 751–752).
- Permeability per unit area misleads for single-crack specimens; normalise by crack number (p. 749).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: J′b/J_tip is a lower bound because the pullout model ignores two-way pullout and spalling (p. 749).
- Stated: k measured unloaded because tests are long (p. 748).
- Observed: Table 5 ECC values are after partial self-healing during the test (Fig. 7), so they are not
  "as-cracked" values ⚠️; initial values were ≈ 2 orders higher.
- Observed: Fig. 7 asymptote for 3% (≈ 10⁻⁹ m/s ⚠️ read) looks higher than Table 5 (7.74 × 10⁻¹⁰).
- Observed: Table 5 "crack width" is not defined as average or maximum; Fig. 5 axis says "maximum crack
  width" and p. 750 says the 1.5% cracks opened to a maximum of ≈ 60 µm, while Table 5 gives 50 ± 10 ⚠️.
- Observed: per-crack normalised k of cracked ECC (4.15–8.90 × 10⁻¹²) is below or equal to uncracked ECC
  (8.18 × 10⁻¹²) — the normalisation is not a physical property.
- Observed: label mismatch — Table 3 gives R/M-a 350–500 µm (ρ 0.019) and R/M-c 125–200 µm (ρ 0.028),
  but Table 5 lists R/M-a at 150 µm and R/M-c at 500 µm; R/M-b appears twice ⚠️.
- Observed: text gives "approximately 1.00 × 10⁵ m/s" at 300 µm (p. 749), clearly a sign error
  (Table 5: 2.58 × 10⁻⁵ m/s) ⚠️.
- Observed: f'c, tensile loading rate and Km value not reported; one mix only; pressure-driven
  permeation only (not capillary suction or diffusion), which the authors say mainly applies to deep
  underwater or water-retaining structures (p. 744).

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]] Table 2 (k values 1.95 / 3 / 7.74 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s, crack
  numbers, uncracked 8.18 × 10⁻¹² and 4.58 × 10⁻¹¹ m/s reproduced correctly).
- Contradicts: ⚔️ [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]] (and [[permeability]]) give reinforced mortar up to
  "10⁻⁵ m/s" at 500 µm; primary value is **4.46 × 10⁻⁴ m/s** (Table 5); 10⁻⁵ is the 300 µm value.
- Refines: the "≈ 100 µm permeability threshold" ([[vanzijl2010-shcc-durability]], citing Lepech & Li
  2005) is in this paper a design target taken from **Wang et al. 1997** on concrete, not a threshold
  measured on ECC; ECC itself was only tested at 50–63 µm.
- Refines: loaded vs residual crack width — ≈ 10% closure here (p. 749), ≈ 15% in
  [[yang2009-ecc-self-healing-wet-dry]], ≈ 2× under sustained flexure in
  [[ozbay2013-ecc-self-healing-sustained-load]].
- Extends: [[yang2009-ecc-self-healing-wet-dry]] (same mix and permeameters; healing requires water
  plus carbonate supply).
- Agrees with: [[homma2009-frcc-self-healing]] (CaCO₃; most deposition in first days; k ∝ w³).
