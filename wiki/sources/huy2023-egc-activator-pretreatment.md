# Activator pretreatment and 18.6% strain in fly-ash EGC (Nguyễn et al., 2023)

Slug note: the first author's family name is **Nguyễn** (Huy Hoàng Nguyễn); the slug uses the given name "Huy", which
is the human's choice to match the raw file name and to tell it apart from other Nguyễn 2023 papers.

## One-paragraph summary
**Experimental** study from Chonnam National University (Gwangju, Korea) with Chosun University. Two fly-ash-based
"extremely ductile engineered geopolymer composites" (ED-EGCs) had the same mix (FA 0.7, sodium hydroxide SH 0.1, sodium
metasilicate pentahydrate SMP 0.2, water 0.345, 1.75 vol.% PE 18 mm × 12 µm). Only the **activator pretreatment** differed.
In warming pretreatment (WP), the activator solution was cooled for 5 min and used at 50.0 ± 0.3 °C, fully liquid. In cooling
pretreatment (CP), it was cooled for 24 h to 23 ± 0.2 °C and SMP partly re-formed as solids (§2.1, Table 1, Figs. 1–2). Curing
was 5 d at ambient temperature, then 2 d at 80 °C, then ambient to 28 d (§2.2). **ED-EGC-CP reached a tensile strain capacity
of 18.62 ± 1.59%** (at tensile strength, JSCE dogbone, 80 mm gauge) with fts 5.54 MPa and f'c 20.7 MPa. ED-EGC-WP had
10.97%, 8.72 MPa and 31.1 MPa (Table 4, Fig. 8). Densities were about 1.7 and 1.5 g/cm³ (lightweight, Fig. 8). In the
micromechanics, matrix Km was very low (0.11 / 0.06 MPa√m) and J_tip was 1.25 / 0.62 J/m² (Table 6). J′b was 64.8 / 57.5 J/m²
at 0.2 vol.% fibre, scaled ×8.75 to 1.75%, giving PSH_E 452 / 817 (Table 7). Both mixes passed the Kanda & Li thresholds
(PSH_S ≥ 1.3, PSH_E ≥ 2.7). The authors attribute CP's ductility to a porous, incompletely geopolymerised N-A-S-H matrix
with low Km, while fibre bridging is kept (§3.2–3.3).

> Synthesis: this is the highest tensile strain capacity in the wiki (18.6%, vs 9.45% Tongji [[uhdc]] and 7.50% in
> [[choi2016-pe-alkali-activated-slag-composite]]). It is obtained with a deliberately **weak** matrix (f'c ≈ 21 MPa,
> Km 0.06 MPa√m), so it is the far "ductility" end of the strength–ductility map, not a counter-example to the trade-off.

## Objective / research question
- Develop an "extremely ductile" EGC whose tensile strain capacity equals or exceeds the elongation of steel rebar, using a
  new pretreatment of the SMP + SH activator (§1, last two paragraphs).
- Explain the tensile behaviour by micromechanics (single-crack and matrix-toughness tests) and by SEM/BSE/EDS (§1).

## Materials & mix design
| Mix ID | Cement | Fly ash | Slag/other | Sand (type, max size) | W/B | SP | Fiber type | L (mm) | d (µm) | Vf (vol.%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ED-EGC-WP | 0 | 0.7 | activator in binder: SH 0.1 + SMP 0.2; warming pretreatment (5 min, 50 °C); defoamer 0.001 | none | 0.345 (water / binder, binder = FA + SH + SMP) | P-SP 0.003 | PE (UHMWPE, Dyneema) | 18 | 12 | 1.75 |
| ED-EGC-CP | 0 | 0.7 | SH 0.1 + SMP 0.2; cooling pretreatment (24 h, 23 °C); defoamer 0.001 | none | 0.345 | P-SP 0.037 | PE | 18 | 12 | 1.75 |

Units of binder quantities: weight ratios of binder (Table 1). The binder total is FA + SH + SMP = 1.0, so the activator
solids count in the binder. The Table 1 layout gives only one water value, 0.345, which appears to apply to both mixes ⚠️.
- FA (XRF, Table 2): SiO₂ 74.59%, Al₂O₃ 14.38%, CaO 3.02%, Fe₂O₃ 2.75%, Na₂O 1.77%, K₂O 0.82%, MgO 0.75%, SO₃ 0.69%,
  TiO₂ 0.70% (a low-calcium, very silica-rich FA). FA class and fineness are not reported.
- PE fibre (Table 3): d 12 µm, L 18 mm, σ_f 2700 MPa, density 0.97 g/cm³, E 88 GPa. Fibre surface treatment is not reported.
- Defoamer: DEHYDRAN 1922, a mineral-based, silicone-free, non-ionic product with bulk density 430 g/L (§2.1).
- Mix design "inspired by" UD-EGC (Nguyễn et al. 2021, ref. [24], as cited). ED-EGC-WP is "almost identical" to UD-EGC20,
  which used a 30 min cooling time (§3.1).
- Mixing: 18 L planetary mixer. FA + activator 2 min at 90 rpm, then 3 min at 180 rpm; P-SP and defoamer added; fibres
  added slowly, then 10 min at 270 rpm (§2.2).

## Test methods
- **Specimen geometry:**
  - JSCE dogbone, 330 mm long, 60 mm wide at the ends, 30 × 13 mm section and 80 mm gauge length; 4 per mix (§2.2–2.3, Fig. 3).
  - Compression: three 50 mm cubes (ASTM C109). Density from masses in air and in water (§2.3).
  - **Single-crack test**: a notched dogbone at **0.2 vol.%** PE, 3 per mix. The net section is 20 × 9 mm after 5 mm side
    notches and 2 mm face notches (Fig. 4b), plus a sawn "artificial deep notch" < 2 mm. Two clip gauges measure crack
    opening (§2.4, Figs. 4–5).
  - **Matrix toughness**: fibre-free notched beam 90 × 90 × 400 mm, span 360 mm, 45 mm cast notch (α = 0.5), 3-point bending
    (per ASTM E399), 3 per mix; Km from peak load (Eq. 3) (§2.5, Fig. 6).
- **Loading / rate:** tension on a 20 kN UTM at 0.1 mm/min (JSCE). Compression on a 300 kN UTM. The single-crack loading rate
  is not reported.
- **Measurement:**
  - Tension: two LVDTs over the 80 mm gauge. Cracks counted with a magnifier.
  - **Crack width = gauge deformation at fts / number of cracks** (computed, loaded). Spacing = gauge / number (§2.3).
  - Em from the initial slope of the tensile stress–strain curves (§3.2).
  - Toughness Ut = area under the curve up to fts and εsc (§3.1).
  - SEM/BSE with EDS (15 kV) on 1 cm³ dry-cut samples from tested dogbones, palladium-coated (§2.6).
- **Curing & test age:** 5 d at 23 ± 3 °C in moulds (delayed demoulding due to high P-SP in CP), 2 d at **80 °C** in a
  thermal chamber, then 23 ± 3 °C to **28 d** (§2.2). Humidity is not reported.
→ [[dogbone-uniaxial-tension]] · [[single-crack-test]] · [[notched-beam-three-point-bending]]

## Key results
| Mix ID | f'c (MPa) | First-crack stress (MPa) | Tensile strength (MPa) | Tensile strain capacity (%) | Crack width (µm) | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ED-EGC-WP | 31.1 (50 mm cube, Fig. 8) | 4.67 ± 0.36 | 8.72 ± 0.76 | 10.97 ± 1.31 (at fts) | 171.8 ± 23.3 (avg, computed) | Nc 50.0; Sc 1.57 mm; ρ ≈ 1.7 g/cm³; Ut 0.73 MPa·m/m; fts/fc 28.0% |
| ED-EGC-CP | 20.7 | 3.40 ± 0.16 | 5.54 ± 0.10 | **18.62 ± 1.59** (at fts) | 199.3 ± 7.4 | Nc 72.8; Sc 1.07 mm; ρ ≈ 1.5 g/cm³; Ut 0.83 MPa·m/m; fts/fc 26.8% |

All at 28 d (Tables 4–5, Fig. 8). The strain is "corresponding to the tensile strength" (§3.1), i.e. at peak stress. In
Fig. 9(b) the CP curves continue to about 18–24% before failure (read ⚠️).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Pretreatment trade-off:** WP versus CP gives ρ +12.3%, f'c +50.2%, first-crack +37.6% and fts +57.4%, but εsc −41.1%,
  Ut −13.7%, crack count −31.3% and crack width −13.8%, with crack spacing +46.7% (§3.1, Tables 4–5, Fig. 8).
- **Strain capacity record (claimed):** CP's 18.62% is 5.4×, 3.9× and 1.4× the FA-EGC values of Ohno & Li 2014 (3.40% in
  Fig. 11), Shaikh/Nematollahi 2018 HGP-1% PE (4.75%) and Nguyễn 2021 UD-EGC15 (13.70%) (as cited). It is also above
  the minimum rebar elongations in Fig. 11 (ASTM A615 9%, ASTM A706 14%, ISO 6935-2 class A/B/C 14%, class D 17%)
  (§3.1, Fig. 11).
- **Lightweight, structural-grade:** both densities are < 1.92 g/cm³ (the ACI 213R lightweight limit, as cited), and both
  f'c values are > 17.2 MPa (the ACI 318 minimum for structural concrete, as cited) (§3.1).
- **fts/f'c 26.8–28.0%**, about 2.7× the "below 10%" of normal concrete (§3.1, Table 4).
- **Crack widths are large:** about 0.2 mm (CP) to 0.17 mm (WP) average loaded widths. The authors say this is "over two times"
  the < 100 µm typical of ECC (§3.1, Table 5).
- **Micromechanics** (Tables 6–7, §3.2):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ED-EGC-WP | 0.29 ± 0.01 | 0.11 ± 0.00 | 10.08 ± 0.58 | 1.25 ± 0.08 | 1.45 ± 0.05 | 0.18 ± 0.04 | 64.79 ± 12.39 | 452.4 | 1.87 (computed here) |
| ED-EGC-CP | 0.17 ± 0.00 | 0.06 ± 0.00 | 6.81 ± 1.51 | 0.62 ± 0.04 | 1.28 ± 0.09 | 0.19 ± 0.03 | 57.52 ± 19.20 | 817.2 | 1.63 (computed here) |

  - **PSH indices used:** PSH_S = σ0/σ_fc, with **σ0 taken equal to the composite fts** ("steady-state flat crack
    propagation", refs. [59,60]) and σ_fc = fcs. PSH_E = J′b/J_tip with J_tip = Km²/Em. **Thresholds: PSH_S ≥ 1.3 and
    PSH_E ≥ 2.7**, attributed to Kanda & Li 2006 ([[kanda2006-psh-design-criteria]]). The criteria come from Li & Leung 1992
    (Eqs. 1–2) (§3.2).
  - PSH_S values are not tabulated. The text says WP is 14.7% higher than CP; fts/fcs gives 1.87 and 1.63 (+14.6%), consistent.
  - PSH_E = (J′b/J_tip at 0.2%) × 8.75 (1.75/0.2). This assumes bridging stress scales with Vf at the same δ0, per Yang et
    al. 2008 ([[yang2008-ecc-fiber-bridging-law]]). Check: 64.79/1.25 × 8.75 = 453.5 and 57.52/0.62 × 8.75 = 811.8,
    consistent with the table within rounding (computed here).
  - WP's PSH_E is 44.6% lower than CP's. The authors conclude PSH_E correlates with εsc better than PSH_S does. CP's PSH_E
    is "≈ 303 times" the 2.7 threshold, i.e. 817.2 / 2.7 = 303 (§3.2, Conclusion 2).
  - σ0 hardly changes (1.45 vs 1.28 MPa, −12%), while J_tip halves (Km 0.11 → 0.06 MPa√m). Cooling pretreatment "lessens
    the matrix fracture toughness but sustains fiber-bridging capacity" (Conclusion 2).
- **Mineralogy (CP only):** SEM shows fibre pull-out and rupture modes, with reaction products agglomerated on fibre
  surfaces and partially reacted FA particles (Fig. 12). BSE shows **voids at the fibre/matrix interface**, so strong bonding
  could not be confirmed (Fig. 13, p. 13). EDS points on a CaO–SiO₂–Al₂O₃ ternary plot fall in the **N-A-S-H** gel field
  (Fig. 14, §3.3).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- **WP (liquid activator):** SMP fully dissolved above about 40 °C (Rasuli 2022, as cited). The liquid activator drives full
  dissolution and geopolymerisation, giving a denser, stronger matrix (§2.1, §3.2).
- **CP (24 h cooling):** SMP particles re-form as solids (Fig. 2). Their diffusion at 80 °C is limited "due to the absence of
  a solvent", so geopolymerisation is incomplete. The result is a porous matrix with low Km, low f'c and high εsc (§3.2).
- **Low strength helps ductility:** the low Km satisfies the energy criterion by a wide margin. PE "connects an incomplete
  geopolymer matrix together", giving more cracks and a larger δ0 (§3.2–3.3). → [[matrix-fracture-toughness]]
- **N-A-S-H gel** favours porosity, which may further lower Km (§3.3, citing Aiken 2021).
- Pull-out of low-angle fibres (moderate bond, retained fibre strength) favours crack opening and ductility. High-angle
  fibres rupture from stress concentration (§3.3, citing Li 2019 and Yang 2008). → [[fiber-bridging]]
- **FA lowers Km** and PE (hydrophobic, little chemical bond, high aspect ratio) suits highly ductile EGCs. Elevated-temperature
  curing improves EGC ductility (§1, as cited). → [[heat-curing]], [[fly-ash]], [[pe-fiber]]

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- **Stated:**
  - The moderate f'c limits use under severe loads. Slag, silica fume or metakaolin should be added to raise strength while
    keeping ductility (Conclusions).
  - The P-SP dosage must be optimised to shorten setting before demoulding. CP needed 5 d in the mould (§2.2, Conclusions).
- **Observed:**
  - Only two mixes, one fibre and one Vf. The pretreatment temperature and time change together.
  - n = 4 for tension, 3 for the micromechanics tests.
  - **σ0 in PSH_S is the composite fts, not the single-crack σ0.** The measured σ0 at 0.2% scaled ×8.75 gives about 12.7 /
    11.2 MPa (computed here), well above fts 8.72 / 5.54 MPa. So the "proportional scaling" used for PSH_E overestimates
    the bridging at 1.75% by about 1.5–2× (computed here). PSH_E values of 452–817 are therefore inflated, and they are
    in any case far above the scale of earlier ECC data (1–200 in [[micromechanical-parameters]]).
  - Km 0.06–0.11 MPa√m is measured on a 90 mm beam with a cast notch, using a metals standard (ASTM E399). These values
    are about 5–10× lower than ECC matrices (about 0.5) in the wiki, and size and notch effects on such weak, porous matrices are
    not discussed.
  - Em (6.8–10.1 GPa) is taken from the composite tensile curve, not from the matrix.
  - Crack widths are computed (deformation at fts / count). They include elastic strain and are loaded widths, not residual
    widths. Average widths of 172–199 µm are far above the 100 µm durability guideline often quoted for ECC.
  - The abstract says density is "lower than 1.7 g/cm³" and f'c is "over 21 MPa", but Fig. 8 shows 1.7 / 1.5 g/cm³ and
    31.1 / 20.7 MPa (CP is below 21 MPa).
  - §3.2 says "SHM particles"; this is probably a typo for SMP.
  - The intro (§1) quotes Ohno & Li 2014 as 4.3%, but Fig. 11 plots 3.40% for the same work. The 5.4× ratio uses 3.40, which
    may be the 3.4 MPa tensile strength mis-plotted as strain ⚠️.
  - Fig. 11 labels UD-EGC as "Nguyễn et al. (2022)", while the text and ref. [24] say 2021.
  - The rebar comparison mixes composite strain at peak stress with steel elongation at fracture; the two are not equivalent
    measures.
  - No durability, shrinkage, fresh-property, size-effect or long-term data. The 80 °C curing limits use cast in place.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[choi2016-pe-alkali-activated-slag-composite]] (same Chonnam group; cementless binder + 1.75% PE
  18 mm × 12 µm, no aggregate, JSCE dogbone) — very high ε is achievable without cement. Agrees with
  [[su2023-rubber-modified-hshdc]] and [[strain-hardening]] that lowering Km (here via a porous matrix) raises the PSH margin and ε.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ [[alkali-activated-binder]] lists Ohno & Li 2014 FA-geopolymer ε as 4.3% (via Choi 2016). This paper's
  intro also says 4.3%, but Fig. 11 plots 3.40%. Within-paper inconsistency; the 4.3% figure is corroborated twice.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ (refinement) In [[choi2016-pe-alkali-activated-slag-composite]] the stronger, lower-W/B matrix gave **both**
  higher strength and higher strain. Here the weaker matrix (CP) gives higher strain and lower strength. The lever is
  different: a porosity/Km change with fixed W/B, not a W/B change.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ [[crack-width]] / ECC norms: average widths of 172–199 µm versus the < 100 µm usually reported for ECC. The
  authors acknowledge this.
- Extends: [[alkali-activated-binder]] (first fly-ash geopolymer composite with primary data in the wiki); [[micromechanical-parameters]]
  (lowest Km and J_tip in the wiki; first single-crack data for a geopolymer); [[tensile-strain-capacity]] (new upper bound
  of 18.62%); [[heat-curing]] (80 °C, 2 d, for geopolymerisation).
