# SHCC Under High Strain Rates (Mechtcherine et al., 2011)

## One-paragraph summary
TU Dresden / Arizona State / IPF Dresden test a PVA-SHCC (2.2 vol.% PVA, high fly ash, SAP) in
uniaxial tension on dumbbells at seven strain rates from 10⁻⁵ to 50 s⁻¹ (6 specimens per rate).
Two regimes appear (Fig. 10, "modes A and B"):
- **Mode A (≤ 10⁻² s⁻¹, below about 1 s⁻¹):** tensile strength rises 4.54 → 5.51 MPa, strain capacity falls
  1.49 → 0.82%, work to fracture falls 8.04 → 5.25 J; fewer cracks, fibre **rupture** dominates
  (pull-out length < 0.5 mm) (Table 2, p. 55–58).
- **Mode B (10–50 s⁻¹):** strength 7.81 → 11.93–12.08 MPa and strain capacity 0.68 → 1.79%, work to
  fracture 9.86 → 20.03 J; **no visible multiple cracking**, fibre **pull-out** dominates (average
  free length about 2.5 mm) with pronounced plastic deformation of the PVA fibres (Table 2, p. 58–59).
Single PVA fibre strength rises only moderately (1456 → 1674 MPa, 8.3·10⁻⁴ → 1.5 s⁻¹, Table 3), but
the single-fibre pull-out peak force roughly doubles from 10⁻⁵ to 10 s⁻¹ (Fig. 13). The authors explain
mode A by a rate-sensitive fibre/matrix bond. → [[strain-rate-effects]]

## Objective / research question
- Obtain detailed knowledge of SHCC tensile behaviour at high loading rates (up to 50 s⁻¹), where
  hardly any data existed above 1 s⁻¹ (p. 51–52).
- Identify failure mechanisms at low and high rates by fracture-surface microscopy.
- Quantify rate effects on PVA fibre tensile properties and on PVA/matrix pull-out (p. 52).

## Test methods
- **Specimen geometry:** dumbbell, narrow section 40 × 24 mm, ends 40 × 40 mm, gauge length 100 mm (Fig. 1, p. 52).
  → [[dogbone-uniaxial-tension]]
- **Low-speed tension:** Instron servo-hydraulic, actuator displacement 0.001–1 mm/s → 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻² s⁻¹;
  ends glued to steel rings (fixed–fixed); 2 LVDTs; 6 specimens per rate (p. 52).
- **High-speed tension:** high-rate MTS with slack adaptor, lightweight stainless-steel grips;
  10, 25, 50 s⁻¹ (1.0, 2.5, 5.0 m/s); Kistler 9041A piezo force link (90 kN, 33 kHz); sampling 250 kHz;
  6 specimens per rate; system ringing at 10 s⁻¹ (p. 53–54, Fig. 2–3). High-speed camera shown in Fig. 2.
- **Strain capacity** = strain at peak stress (localization); work to fracture = total area under the
  load–displacement curve (p. 53, 56). DIF reference = 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ (p. 57).
- **Single fibre tension:** FAVIGRAPH, gauge 2 mm, 0.1 / 2 / 180 mm/min → 8.3·10⁻⁴, 1.7·10⁻², 1.5 s⁻¹;
  about 50 fibres per rate (p. 54).
- **Single fibre pull-out:** embedment 1 mm; quasi-static 0.01 µm/s (about 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹); high-speed
  10⁴ µm/s (nominal 10 s⁻¹) on a piezo device (max 80 µm, 50 N, 2 MHz); 10 samples per regime
  (p. 54–55). → [[single-fiber-pullout-test]]
- **Microscopy:** OM, ESEM, AFM of fracture surfaces and pulled-out fibres (p. 55).
- **Curing & test age:** 2 d in moulds at 25 °C / 65% RH, then sealed in plastic foil at room temperature
  until testing; **test age of dumbbells not stated**. Pull-out specimens: 24 h, then 28 d at about 90% RH (p. 52, 54).

Main findings:
- Strength DIF about 1.2 at 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ and about 2.5 at 50 s⁻¹ (stated, Fig. 9, p. 58); moderate,
  log-linear rise up to about 1–10 s⁻¹, then much steeper. Note: Table 2 gives 11.93/4.54 = 2.63 (computed here).
- Mode A: crack density falls with rate; failed fibres protrude < 0.5 mm; one failed fibre necked about 0.3 mm
  from the matrix face (p. 58–59, Fig. 11a, c).
- Mode B: fibre pull-out with about 2.5 mm average free length, a few ruptured fibres; severe fibre surface
  deformation (p. 59, Fig. 11b, d, 12).
- Single-fibre pull-out peak force about ×2 at high speed, reached at a smaller displacement (Fig. 13–14, p. 60);
  high-speed fibres partly coated with matrix → shift from adhesive to partly cohesive failure in the interphase (Fig. 15).
- Fibre strain at failure is rate-insensitive (27.4–27.5%, Table 3).
- Spallation Hopkinson-bar tests at 140–180 s⁻¹ gave a strength DIF of 6.7 (Millon et al. 2010, as cited, p. 58).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- **Mode A:** bond strength rises with rate faster than fibre strength, so more fibres rupture instead of
  pulling out → fewer cracks, lower strain capacity (p. 56–57, 60; supported by Boshoff et al. 2009, as cited).
  → [[fiber-matrix-interface]]
- **Mode B:** increased pull-out length and plastic deformation of PVA fibres are "the main mechanisms"
  for the higher strain capacity and energy above 10 s⁻¹ (Conclusion 2, p. 61–62). Why the mode switches
  back to pull-out is explicitly left open (p. 60–61).
- Possible explanation (hypothesis): during high-speed pull-out the deeper embedded fibre segment is loaded
  at a much lower local strain rate because debonding takes time (p. 60).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: actual local pull-out rate unknown; piezo device stops at 40 µm, so the full high-speed pull-out
  curve was not recorded (p. 60); link between fibre-test displacement rates and crack-plane pull-out rates
  unresolved (p. 61); strength plateau between 25 and 50 s⁻¹ unexplained (p. 57); ringing at 10 s⁻¹.
- Observed: strain at high rates is computed from actuator LVDT displacement, not on-specimen gauges; no
  crack counts or widths quantified; one mix only; no compression; elastic modulus not given above 10⁻² s⁻¹.
- Observed: test age of dumbbells not stated. Table 2 lists 0.0001 m/s for both 10⁻³ and 10⁻² s⁻¹ (the
  latter would be 0.001 m/s per the 100 mm gauge) ⚠️. The stated low-pass filter cutoff "3Hz" (p. 53) looks
  implausible for a < 10 ms test ⚠️.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: Yang & Li 2005 and Douglas & Billington 2005 (as cited): strength up, strain capacity down at
  quasi-static to 0.2 s⁻¹. Boshoff et al. 2009 (as cited): higher pull-out rate shifts PVA to rupture.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ Maalej et al. 2005 (hybrid steel/PE SHCC, as cited): no obvious change in strain capacity
  to 0.2 s⁻¹. ⚔️ Michigan HSHDC with PE ([[ranade2015-hshdc-tensile-rate-effects]]): interface nearly
  rate-insensitive (τ0 +14%) and fibre properties dominate (PE modulus +85%), whereas here the PVA interface
  dominates (pull-out force about ×2, fibre strength +15% computed here). Different fibre chemistry, not a true
  conflict.
- Extends: provides the primary "Mechtcherine" result cited in [[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]] (ε falls
  with rate) and shows it holds only to 10⁻² s⁻¹; above 10 s⁻¹ ε rises again.
