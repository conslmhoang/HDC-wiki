# Rubber-modified HSHDC: rubber ratio and fiber length (Su et al., 2023)

## One-paragraph summary
Guangdong University of Technology (Lin, Guo group) study of a PE-fiber HSHDC (cement + ultra-fine
GGBFS + silica fume, quartz powder aggregate, W/B ≈ 0.155 by mass, 2 vol.% UHMWPE, water-cured
at room temperature, no heat curing) in which quartz powder is replaced by **powder rubber (PR)**
at 0/10/20/35 vol.%, combined with PE fiber lengths of 6, 12 and 18 mm (11 composite mixes,
PE18-PR35 dropped). Tests: axial compression (Ø50×100 mm cylinders), JSCE dumbbell direct tension
with LVDT + DIC crack analysis, single-crack tension (σ–δ curve), and notched-beam matrix fracture
toughness Km; Kanda–Li PSH indices computed. Key results (28 d water curing):
- PR lowers f'c (up to −33.2% at 35 vol.%) and E; f'c stays > 100 MPa up to 20 vol.% PR (p. 4–5, Fig. 6).
- PR lowers Km from 0.787 to 0.671 MPa·m^1/2 (−14.7% at 35 vol.%) (p. 5, Fig. 7).
- 6 mm fiber: single-crack brittle failure (ε ≈ 0.3–0.5%) although PSH indices formally satisfied.
- 12/18 mm fiber: multiple cracking; best mix PE18-PR20: σ_tu 19.4 MPa, ε_cu 5.7%, 31 cracks, avg crack width 88 µm (Tables 3–4).
- Recommended: 12 or 18 mm PE + 20 vol.% PR (Conclusion 5, p. 13).

## Objective / research question
1. Can powder rubber, replacing fine aggregate, lower the high matrix toughness of HSHDC and raise
   tensile ductility while keeping f'c high (≥ 100 MPa)? (p. 2)
2. Which PE fiber length (6/12/18 mm) suits rubber-modified HSHDC at different rubber ratios? (p. 2)
3. Explain the trends with micromechanical parameters (Km, σ0, δ0, J′b, J_tip) and PSH indices. (p. 2)

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry (Fig. 4, p. 5):
  - Axial tension: JSCE dumbbell, gauge section 80 mm long × 30 mm wide × 13 mm thick; grip ends 60 mm wide (dimensions read from Fig. 4a).
  - Single-crack tension: same dumbbell with a circumferential groove (notch) at mid-length (Fig. 4b).
  - Matrix fracture toughness: notched beam 40 × 40 × 160 mm, notch 12 mm, span 100 mm; three-point bending (Eqs. 1–3).
  - Compression: cylinders Ø50 × 100 mm, ASTM C469; ends ground and capped with high-strength gypsum.
- Loading / rate: displacement control 0.5 mm/min for all tests (tension per JSCE; compression per ASTM C469).
- Measurement:
  - Tension: 2 LVDTs over 80 mm gauge; DIC (7952×5304 px camera, 70 mm field, 0.2 Hz, DPP ≈ 0.03 mm/px, accuracy 0.1–0.3 px → cracks identified when displacement jump > 9 µm).
  - Single-crack: measurement within 20 mm around the notch.
  - Compression: 2 axial + 2 hoop strain gauges, 2 LVDTs, 1 Hz.
  - SEM/EDS on rubber–matrix interface (PR20 mix, Fig. 8).
- Curing & test age: covered with plastic film, 24 h at room temperature, demoulded, then water
  immersion for 28 d before testing (temperature not reported; no heat curing) (p. 3).
- Number of specimens per mix: not reported (standard deviations given in Tables 3–4).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- Fiber length has no significant effect on f'c, except relatively high f'c of PE18-PR0 (p. 4, Fig. 6a).
- f'c falls with PR; largest drop at 35 vol.%: PE6-PR35 −33.2% vs PE6-PR0 (p. 4). At 20 vol.% PR f'c
  still > 100 MPa (p. 5; Conclusion 1). Authors note f'c at 20 vol.% exceeded that at 10 vol.%
  (p. 4) — true for 6 and 18 mm groups in Fig. 6a but not obviously for 12 mm (⚠️ see To verify).
- E: PE12-PR35 = 31 GPa, −26.7% vs PE12-PR0 (p. 4–5, Fig. 6b).
- Km: 0.787 (PR0) → 0.772 (−1.9%) → 0.740 (−6.0%) → 0.671 MPa·m^1/2 (−14.7%) for 0/10/20/35 vol.% PR (p. 5, Fig. 7).
- SEM/EDS: visible gap and microcracks at PR–matrix interface (PR20 mix) → weak bond (p. 5, Fig. 8).
- Failure mode: 6 mm group fails at first crack; 12 and 18 mm show multiple cracking; 18 mm > 12 mm
  in crack number; crack number rises with PR (p. 5, Fig. 9).
- Crack parameters (Table 3, p. 9): 12 mm group: w 79–198 µm, Nc 8–17 (text; table shows up to 23),
  Cd 0.11–0.24 mm⁻¹ (text); 18 mm group: w 88–130 µm, Nc 15–31, Cd 0.21–0.45 mm⁻¹.
- Fiber length at 0% PR: σ_tu of 12/18 mm = 1.7×/1.9× the 6 mm group; ε_cu 7.1×/9.5×; g_se 12.7×/18.2× (p. 9).
- PR in 12 mm group vs PR0: first-crack −13.5/−31.1/−21.9%; ε +17.9/+71.6/+31.6%; σ_tu −2.0/−2.2/−8.6%
  for 10/20/35 vol.% (p. 9).
- At 35 vol.% PR, ε_cu and σ_tu of 6 and 12 mm groups decreased; slight fiber agglomeration
  observed (p. 9). PE12-PR35 also shows lower PSH indices (p. 10, Fig. 15).
- Single-crack test: longer fibers markedly raise σ0; PR slightly raises σ0 (Table 5; Conclusion 3).
- Almost all mixes satisfy PSH_S > 1.2 and PSH_E > 3, including the 6 mm group, which nonetheless
  did not multiple-crack (p. 10).
- Optimum: 12 or 18 mm PE + 20 vol.% PR (Conclusion 5, p. 13).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- **f'c / E reduction:** air bubbles from non-polar (hydrophobic) rubber surface, discontinuous
  PR–matrix interface, low strength and modulus of PR (p. 5; refs 26, 46, 47 as cited).
- **Km reduction:** air bubbles + poor rubber–matrix bond; cracks propagate around the interface
  (SEM gap, Fig. 8) (p. 5). PR acts as artificial flaws, lowering cracking strength (p. 7).
- **Fiber length:** σ0 scales with embedment length (max = L/2). 6 mm fiber (3 mm max embedment) is
  below the minimum critical fiber length → all fibers pull out after debonding, unstable crack
  propagation (Maalej, Li & Hashida 1995, ref 48 as cited, p. 9). High-strength PE avoids rupture of
  longer fibers (p. 7).
- **Multiple cracking:** Kanda–Li condition — nth crack forms while its cracking stress < σ0 (p. 6).
- **Crack width control:** irregular, low-modulus rubber particles interlock and stretch, giving a
  rubber-bridging effect (Hou et al. 2022/2023, refs 27–28 as cited, p. 7).
- **σ0 slight rise with PR:** possibly rubber bridging or better fiber dispersion from increased
  mortar viscosity (Conclusion 3).
- **Excess PR (35 vol.%):** excessive plastic viscosity → poorer fiber dispersion / agglomeration
  (p. 7, 9; Li & Li 2013 and Lei et al. as cited).
- **PSH_E increase with PR:** J_tip falls because both Km and E fall (p. 10).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: PR mechanical properties not measured (p. 2); PE18-PR35 not produced (p. 3); PR > 40 vol.%
  unworkable (p. 3); f'c anomaly at 10 vs 20 vol.% attributed partly to experimental variation (p. 4);
  durability (freeze–thaw, chemical), fiber–matrix interaction, cost and construction practice are
  future work (Section 4, p. 12).
- Observed:
  - Specimen numbers per test not reported; large SDs in some mixes (e.g. PE18-PR10 Nc 23 ± 15, g_se 748 ± 221).
  - f'c values given only graphically; cylinder Ø50×100 mm (small size, size effect relative to other studies).
  - W/B not stated; rubber particle size not quantified beyond "similar to QP"; no fresh-state
    (flow/viscosity) measurement although viscosity is invoked as mechanism.
  - Strain definition ("ultimate tensile strain") and its unit misprint ("MPa") in Table 4.
  - Single fiber type (PE 20 µm); no single-fiber pullout, so τ0 not measured.
  - 6 mm group satisfies PSH criteria yet fails brittle → the indices as computed did not predict
    behavior; σss taken as composite first-crack strength rather than from matrix tests.
  - Water curing at "room temperature" — temperature not stated.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with:
  - Ranade et al. 2013 "Micromechanics of HSHDC" (ACI MJ 110(4); not a wiki source) / Ranade et al. 2013 "Composite properties of HSHDC" (ACI MJ 110(4); not a wiki source): a PE-fiber,
    2 vol.% high-strength matrix can strain-harden; high Km is the obstacle to ductility (as cited, p. 1–2).
  - PE12-PR0 crack width 198 µm is of the same order as the ≈180 µm of Ranade HSHDC
    (Ranade et al. 2013 "Composite properties of HSHDC" (ACI MJ 110(4); not a wiki source)).
- Contradicts: ⚔️
  - ⚔️ Terminology: the paper equates "High Ductile Concrete (HDC)" with ECC and builds its HSHDC on
    **PE** fiber, whereas [[terminology-hdc-ecc-shcc]] currently associates the Chinese "high ductile
    concrete" usage with PVA fiber. This Chinese group (GDUT) uses HDC/HSHDC with PE.
  - Km of PE-free HSHDC-type matrix here 0.787 MPa·m^1/2 vs 1.1 MPa√m in Ranade HSHDC
    (Ranade et al. 2013 "Micromechanics of HSHDC" (ACI MJ 110(4); not a wiki source)) — different matrices (GGBFS, QP 50–300 µm, no heat curing), not a direct conflict.
  - Fiber-length literature cited is itself conflicting (Feng et al. 2022: longer PVA fiber → lower
    ductility; Lee et al. 2023: 18 mm > 12 mm), as cited p. 2. This study's PE result follows Lee et al.
- Extends:
  - Shows HSHDC-class behavior without 90 °C heat curing: PE18-PR0 f'c ≈157 MPa (figure) with
    ε_cu 3.8%, using 28 d room-temperature water curing (addresses gap in [[heat-curing]]).
  - First study (per authors) of rubber modification of HSHDC rather than normal-strength ECC (p. 2).
  - Cites rubberised ECC work (Zhang et al. 2019; Adesina & Das 2021; Huang et al. 2013; Hou et al.
    2022/2023), not yet ingested.
