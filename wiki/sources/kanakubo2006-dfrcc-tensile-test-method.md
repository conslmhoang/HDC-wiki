# Tensile Characteristics Evaluation Method for DFRCC (Kanakubo, 2006)

## One-paragraph summary
Invited paper (Univ. of Tsukuba) that reviews tension test methods for concrete and cementitious
composites and reports the **JCI Technical Committee round-robin test** (2001–2004) on four DFRCCs
(PVA, PE, PE/steel hybrid HB, and the UFC Ductal™ "DCT") tested with **four uniaxial tension
methods** (T1 30 × 13 mm plate dogbone; T2 100 × 60 mm dogbone cast horizontally or vertically;
T3 Ø70 mm dogbone; T4 Ø100 mm cylinder with fixed ends), plus compression and 4-point bending
(100 × 100 × 400 mm, horizontal/vertical casting). **Results depend strongly on the test method and
casting direction**: tensile strength ranks T1 > T2-H > T2-V = T3 > T4 (except PE), and strain at
maximum stress is largest for T1 and T2-H (e.g. PVA 2.70% in T1 vs 0.07% in T3; Tables 6–9).
The 2-D fiber orientation of the thin T1 plate and flow-induced orientation are blamed. COV of
strain at peak reaches > 100%, so the author argues strain at maximum stress is **not suitable**
to express DFRCC deformation capacity (p. 10). Inverse analysis of bending moment–curvature data
(two stress-block cases) gives tensile strength and ultimate strain within the scatter of the T2
results (Tables 11–12). The JCI Standard Committee therefore standardised only a **4-point bending
moment–curvature test**, with the tensile evaluation as a non-mandatory appendix (§5).

## Objective / research question
Compare uniaxial tension results from different test methods and casting directions on several
DFRCCs; relate them to tensile properties back-calculated from bending; explain how the JCI standard
test method was chosen (abstract; p. 4).

## Test methods
- Specimen geometry (Table 3, Fig. 3, p. 7):
  - **T1**: plate dogbone, 30 × 13 mm section, horizontal casting, pin–fix (Kanda & Li 1999 type);
    drawing shows 330 mm total, 80 mm gauge, 60 mm ends (same as the JSCE dogbone).
  - **T2-H / T2-V**: 100 × 60 mm section dogbone, 400 mm long, made in a 100 × 100 × 400 mm mold with
    curved plates (R350), horizontal or vertical casting, pin–fix (Shimizu 2004).
  - **T3**: Ø70 mm circular dogbone from a cylinder mold, vertical casting, pin–fix (Furuta 2003).
  - **T4**: Ø100 × 200 mm cylinder, vertical casting, **fix–fix**, end plates (Sato 2001).
  - Compression C: Ø100 mm cylinder. Bending B-H / B-V: 100 × 100 × 400 mm, 4-point, spans 100 mm
    (300 mm span, 100 mm constant-moment zone), Fig. 6.
- Loading / rate: not reported.
- Measurement: LVDTs; strain = displacement / gauge length; curvature from two LVDTs (70 mm apart) in the
  pure-bending zone (Fig. 6). Specimens failing outside the LVDT gauge were excluded.
- Curing & test age: not reported ⚠️. Molds: steel (T1, T2-H, B-H), tin (T3, T4, C), wooden panel (T2-V, B-V).
- n = 3 per DFRCC and specimen type in principle; 5–6 for some tension specimens (p. 7–8).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Test method governs results**: σ_tu ranks T1 > T2-H > T2-V = T3 > T4, except PE; strain at max is largest
  in T1 and T2-H (Fig. 12, p. 9). No clear trend for first-crack strength or strain.
- **Casting direction**: T2-H > T2-V in strength and strain for PVA and HB; the reverse for PE (p. 8). B-H gives a
  slightly larger maximum moment and a much larger curvature at max than B-V (p. 11).
- **Scatter**: COV of σ_tu is 5–15% (as for compression, 2–6%); COV of strain at max is larger, up to 127–157%.
  "Impossible to evaluate" tensile characteristics with ≥ 100% COV; strain at maximum stress "not suitable" for
  DFRCC deformation capacity (p. 9–10).
- **T4 fix–fix**: first crack not identifiable because of large bending moment (p. 8).
- **Inverse analysis** (Fig. 14; Tables 11–12): Case 1 (bilinear: σ_cr from M_cr, σ_max from M_max) and Case 2
  (uniform tension block) give similar σ_tu and ultimate strain except for DCT; differences from T2 results lie
  within scatter (p. 11–12; Fig. 16 compares calculated vs T2 strength).
- **Strain-hardening vs softening** DFRCC cannot be distinguished from bending results alone (p. 14).
- **Shear (Fig. 1, p. 3–4)**: with equal reinforcement, UFC (Ductal) beam reached 1.7× the maximum shear of a
  PVA-ECC beam (UFC f'c ≈ 5× PVA-ECC); crack patterns were similar. PVA-ECC with 2.0% fiber has "almost 2%"
  tensile strain capacity (Shimizu 2005, as cited). Tensile deformation capacity is "not such an important
  factor" for shear performance.
- **JCI standard**: only the 4-point bending moment–curvature test was standardised; applicable only to
  composites that form several separated cracks in the pure-bending zone; the tensile strength / ultimate
  strain evaluation is a non-mandatory appendix (p. 14–15).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Thin plate (T1) → 2-D fiber orientation → higher strength and strain; casting flow direction aligns fibers (p. 9).
- Uniaxial tension is sensitive to end shape (stress concentration at section change or anchors), boundary
  condition (pin–pin reduces moment but secondary moment appears after one-sided cracking; fix–fix adds bending)
  and specimen size, defects and ratio of fiber length to specimen size (p. 4–5, Table 2).
- Splitting tests are unsuitable for FRCC because specimens do not separate after cracking (p. 5).
- Modulus of rupture is a structural not a material property; strain gradient makes MOR ≫ σ_t for FRC (p. 5).
- For high f'c/σ_t (≥ 10) and several % strain, max moment is set by tensile strain at max tensile stress (p. 11).
- Tensile strain is a smeared quantity (crack width × crack density), so σ–ε models cannot give crack width (p. 13).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: the relation between uniaxial and bending-derived properties varies by DFRCC; more data needed
  (p. 15); uniaxial tension needs special jigs, time and labour, unsuited to plant QC (p. 14).
- Observed:
  - Very small n (1–3) for many T2 cells; DCT T2 failed entirely by end-plate delamination.
  - No mix proportions, curing regime, test age or loading rate reported.
  - Several "strain at max" values (PE T2-H 0.018%, HB T2-V 0.017%) equal the first-crack strain, i.e. no
    hardening in that test; the source of brittleness (orientation vs defect) is not resolved.
  - W/B basis (mass) not stated.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with:
  - [[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]]: cylinders give lower σ and ε than dogbones (Kesner 2003, Mechtcherine 2011,
    as cited there). Here the Ø100 cylinder T4 gives the lowest σ_tu for PVA, HB, DCT.
  - [[kunieda2006-hpfrcc-japan-applications]]: same JCI round robin; fiber orientation depends on geometry.
  - [[zhang2022-hshdcc-review]]: deflection hardening does not prove tensile strain hardening.
- Contradicts: ⚔️
  - [[yu2018-ecc-direct-tensile-review]] / Rokugo 2007 (as cited): thickness 13/30/50 mm had a negligible effect on
    PVA-ECC. Here, PVA strain at max fell from 2.70% (13 mm plate) to 2.38% (60 mm, H) and 0.77% (V), 0.07% (Ø70)
    and 0.32% (Ø100 cylinder). Different shape and casting, not thickness alone.
- Extends: first quantitative round-robin data on test-method dependence of ε_tu in the wiki; UFC (Ductal) tension
  data; the JCI bending-based standard.
