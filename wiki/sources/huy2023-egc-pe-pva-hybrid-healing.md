# High-strength, ultra-ductile PE–PVA hybrid engineered geopolymer composites and their autogenous healing (Nguyễn et al., 2023)

> Naming note: the first author's family name is **Nguyễn** (cite as Nguyễn et al., 2023); the slug and raw file use the given
> name "Huy" by the human's choice, to tell this paper apart from another Nguyễn 2023 source.

## One-paragraph summary
**Experimental** study from Chonnam National University (Gwangju, Korea) with Stevens Institute of Technology. Four
cement-free "engineered geopolymer composites" (EGCs) were made from GGBS + fly ash + 5% silica fume, activated by
NaOH flakes and water glass, with silica sand and 1.5 vol.% **hybrid PE + PVA** fibre (Table 1). Two binder ratios
(GGBS:FA 0.55:0.40 "S" and 0.30:0.65 "F", by mass) were crossed with two fibre ratios (PE:PVA 1.0:0.5 and 0.5:1.0 vol.%).
After 2 d at room temperature, 24 h at 100 °C and air to 7 d, then 28 d in water, f'c was 84.3–86.5 MPa (S) and
50.4–51.9 MPa (F) (Fig. 3). **All four mixes strain-hardened with multiple cracking**: σ_tu 4.60–6.64 MPa and tensile strain
capacity **6.79–11.99%** (JSCE dumbbell, 80 mm gauge; Table 5), with 61–84 cracks and average crack width 83–136 µm
(Table 6). S-PE combined 84.3 MPa with 10.51%, giving a performance index f'c × ε of 8.8 MPa, close to PE-only HSUD-ECCs
(9.2–10.0) and well above earlier EGCs (1.2–2.6) (Fig. 6), at the lowest material cost per unit performance (91 $/(MPa·m³),
Fig. 7). Autogenous healing in water (28 d, after 3% tensile pre-strain at 7 d) was **limited**: crack healing rate 7.4–31.4%
(Fig. 10) and stiffness (resonant-frequency) recovery +15.6 / +24.2% for S mixes but **negative** (−9.6 / −6.6%) for F mixes
(Fig. 12). More PVA and more GGBS healed better. Re-loaded specimens still strain-hardened (ε 6.29–11.97% including the 3%
pre-strain; Table 8). SEM/EDS identified the healing product as **C-(N)-A-S-H gel** (Table 9, Fig. 15).

> Synthesis: the first primary evidence in the wiki that a **cement-free** matrix can reach the high-strength class
> (≈ 85 MPa) together with > 10% tensile strain capacity, and the first primary self-healing data on a strain-hardening
> alkali-activated composite. Healing behaves differently from cement-based ECC: the product is C-(N)-A-S-H, not CaCO₃,
> and the FA-rich mixes lost stiffness in water instead of recovering.

## Objective / research question
- Develop high-strength (HS, f'c > 55 MPa) and ultra-ductile (UD, ε > 6%) EGCs, using a GGBS/FA binder and PE–PVA fibre
  hybridisation to cut cost (PVA ≈ 1/5 of PE price) (§1, p. 2–3).
- Effects of GGBS:FA ratio and PE:PVA ratio on density, compressive strength, tensile behaviour, crack pattern, and on
  autogenous healing (crack-width reduction, resonant-frequency recovery, post-healing tension, healing chemistry) (§1, p. 3).

Units of binder quantities: mass ratio to total precursor (GGBS + FA + SF = 1) (Table 1, §2.1); fibre by volume.
- **W/B note:** Table 1 lists only "Water 0.1". WG contains 62.0% H₂O (§2.1), so water from WG = 0.45 × 0.620 = 0.279 and
  total water / precursor ≈ 0.38 by mass (**computed here**, activator solids not counted in the binder). The authors give no W/B.
- GGBS:FA by mass = 5.5:4 (S) and 3:6.5 (F); FA/(GGBS + FA) = 0.42 (S) and 0.68 (F) (computed). PE:PVA by volume = 2:1 and 1:2 (§2.1).
- GGBS (XRF, Table 2): CaO 56.8, SiO₂ 17.5, Al₂O₃ 8.5, Na₂O 7.8 wt%; specific gravity 2.91; Blaine 4030 cm²/g (§2.1).
- FA (Table 2): SiO₂ 74.6, Al₂O₃ 14.4, CaO 3.0 wt% (low-calcium; class not stated); specific gravity 2.22; Blaine 3870 cm²/g.
- SF: Elkem, mean particle 0.15 µm, specific gravity 2.25.
- Activators: NaOH flakes (99% purity, "molar concentration of 11.5 M"); water glass (Duksan) 28.9% SiO₂, 9.1% Na₂O, 62.0% H₂O (§2.1).
- Silica sand: average particle 120 µm, density 2.62 g/cm³. SP: polycarboxylate (Flowmix 3000S).
- **Fibres (Table 3):** PE d 12 µm, L 18 mm, L/d 1500, 3030 MPa, 88 GPa. PVA d 40 µm, L 12 mm, L/d 300, 1560 MPa, 41 GPa.
  Fibre density and surface oiling not reported.
- Mixing: 18 L planetary mixer; dry powders 3 min at 90 rpm; activator solution (WG + CS + water, mixed 3 min) + SP, 3 min at
  270 rpm; fibres added slowly at 270 rpm until dispersed (§2.2).
- Hardened density 2.01–2.10 g/cm³, within 1.5% of theoretical (Table 4).

## Test methods
- **Specimen geometry:** compression and density: 50 mm cubes, 3 per mix (ASTM C109/C109M). Tension: JSCE dumbbell, 330 mm
  long, 30 × 13 mm section in the 80 mm gauge zone (Fig. 1a), 3 per mix; healing: 4 per mix (3 for healing tests, 1 for
  SEM/EDS) (§2.2).
- **Loading / rate:** 20 kN universal testing machine following JSCE (2008) HPFRCC recommendations; loading rate **not reported**
  (§2.3). Compression on a 300 kN machine.
- **Measurement:** load cell; two LVDTs over 80 mm gauge. Crack count with a magnifier; average crack width = maximum
  deformation / number of cracks (deformation of uncracked matrix assumed negligible); crack spacing = 80 mm / crack count
  (§2.3). Checked: 10.51% × 80 mm / 84.3 cracks = 99.8 µm vs 100.3 µm in Table 6, so the strain and width are **at peak
  stress** (computed here).
- **Healing protocol:** tensile pre-loading to 3% strain at 7 d; then water at 23 ± 3 °C for 28 d. Crack widths by digital
  microscope (× 84) at 24 crack positions per mix (Fig. 9). Stiffness recovery via longitudinal resonant frequency (ASTM C215,
  accelerometer + steel-ball impact, FFT), every 7 d. Re-loading in tension after 28 healing days (35 d age). SEM (× 1000,
  × 20 000) and EDS (15 kV) on dry-cut healed crack samples, palladium-coated (§2.3).
- Indices: crack healing rate γ_r = (W_c,i − W_c,h)/W_c,i ("Eq. (3)" in text, printed as Eq. (4)); RF_r = RF₂₈ − RF₀ in
  normalised-% points ("Eq. (4)", printed as Eq. (5)); performance index p_index = f'c × ε_tu (Eq. 2, after Ranade 2014);
  cost effectiveness C_e = Σ m_i c_i / p_index (Eq. 3) (§2.3, §3.1.3, §3.2).
- **Curing & test age:** sealed under plastic 2 d at 23 ± 3 °C, demoulded; **100 °C for 24 h** (to enable geopolymerisation);
  air at 23 ± 3 °C to 7 d; then all specimens in water at 23 ± 3 °C for another 28 d before mechanical and healing tests (§2.2).
  So the virgin tension and compression tests were at ≈ 35 d (7 + 28; inferred, not stated as a number) ⚠️.

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **Binder ratio dominates strength**: S mixes had 67.3% (PE) and 66.7% (PVA) higher f'c than F mixes; fibre type hardly
  changed f'c or density (Fig. 3, §3.1.1, Conclusion 1). F mixes were ≈ 3.8% less dense (Table 4).
- **Tension**: S mixes have higher first-crack and tensile strength; F mixes have higher ε (Table 5). PE-rich mixes have higher
  σ_tu and ε than PVA-rich ones (Table 5, §3.1.2). Maximum ε 11.99% (F-PE); S-PE 10.51% at 84.3 MPa.
- SPI (σ_tu/σ_fc) was 6.7% (PE) and 3.2% (PVA) higher in F than in S mixes (§3.1.2, p. 7); from Table 5 the SPI values are
  1.79 / 1.57 / 1.92 / 1.62 for S-PE / S-PVA / F-PE / F-PVA (computed here). EPI not quantified; lower matrix toughness of F
  mixes is inferred from lower f'c, not measured.
- **Crack pattern**: S mixes have more cracks, narrower cracks (83–100 vs 128–136 µm) and smaller spacing, which the authors read
  as a stronger fibre–matrix bond (Table 6, §3.1.2).
- **Performance index** f'c × ε (Fig. 6): S-PE 8.8 MPa vs HSUD-ECCs 10.0 (Yu 2018) and 9.2 (Zhang 2021); HS-ECCs 1.4–6.1
  (M45 1.4); earlier EGCs 1.2–2.6. Text says the two HSUD-ECCs are 4.5% and 23.7% higher ⚠️ (the bars give ≈ 4.5% and 13.6%).
- **Cost effectiveness** (Fig. 7): S-PE 91 $/(MPa·m³) vs 124–133 (HSUD-ECCs), 181–274 (HS-ECCs incl. M45 264) and 185–423
  (EGCs). Unit costs (Table 7): PE 52.5 $/kg, PVA 10 $/kg, GGBS 0.04, FA 0.07, SF 0.40, WG 0.53, CS 0.25 $/kg.
- **Crack-width healing** (Fig. 9, §3.2.1): a few cracks < 50 µm healed completely; most cracks of 50–150 µm healed only partly
  or little. Crack healing rate 26.78 / 31.40 / 7.36 / 12.48% for S-PE / S-PVA / F-PE / F-PVA (Fig. 10); S-PVA was 1.2, 4.3
  and 2.5 times the others (p. 9).
- **Stiffness recovery** (Figs. 11–12): normalised RF dropped sharply at 3% pre-strain; at 28 d of water it was 61.5 / 76.9 /
  27.6 / 45.1% of uncracked RF (p. 9). RF_r = **+15.63 / +24.16 / −9.58 / −6.59** %-points (Fig. 12): S mixes recovered, F mixes
  lost stiffness further in water.
- **Post-healing tension** (Table 8, Fig. 13): first-crack 3.21–4.21 MPa, σ_tu 4.40–7.53 MPa, ε 6.29–11.97% (sum of 3% pre-strain
  and additional strain, excluding re-opening of old cracks), 52–66 cracks. On average σ_tu +3.7% and ε −6.1% vs virgin
  specimens (p. 11). The text says the F mixes cracked at lower stress after healing, but Table 8 gives higher re-loading
  first-crack values than Table 5 ⚠️ (see "To verify").
- **Healing chemistry** (Fig. 14, Table 9, Fig. 15): S-PVA healed visibly along crack mouths, F-PVA showed very few products;
  products were lamellar plus crystalline; EDS (atomic %) Ca 10.9–14.1 and Si 12.1–17.1 for S-PVA vs Ca 7.4–9.0 and Si
  25.1–30.5 for F-PVA; on the CaO–SiO₂–Al₂O₃ diagram both plot in the **C-(N)-A-S-H** gel field.

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- GGBS gives finer, more amorphous products and accelerates FA geopolymer reaction → higher f'c and density (§3.1.1).
- FA lowers matrix fracture toughness and raises complementary energy (wider cracks) → higher ε, following Wang & Li 2007 for
  HVFA-ECC (§2.1, §3.1.2). → [[fly-ash]], [[matrix-fracture-toughness]]
- PE's higher strength and aspect ratio → higher σ_tu and ε than PVA (§3.1.2).
- Healing: under water, Na⁺, Ca²⁺ and Al³⁺ leach from unreacted GGBS, FA, residual activators and matrix; further reaction of
  alkali-activated slag/geopolymer forms C-(N)-A-S-H that seals cracks from the inside out until the reactants are exhausted
  (Fig. 16, p. 11). Hydrophilic PVA attracts healing products (adhesion); hydrophobic PE does not (§1, §3.2.1).
- Sodium-silicate activators seemed unhelpful for healing, as in an earlier strain-hardening AAS study (ref. [62]) (p. 8).
- F-group RF loss: their microstructure is "chemically less stable" in water (p. 9); not tested further.
- Healed interface bonds better → σ_tu up, crack width and ε down at re-loading (p. 11).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: healing performance "relatively limited"; sodium-silicate activators not useful for healing (Abstract, p. 8).
- Observed: only 3 tension specimens per mix and 3 healing specimens; loading rate not reported.
- Observed: heat curing at 100 °C for 24 h is needed; ambient-cured performance unknown. → [[heat-curing]]
- Observed: virgin mechanical tests followed 28 d of water immersion (≈ 35 d), so they are not 28 d values; the F-group RF loss in
  water raises the question whether virgin F specimens were also weakened by immersion (not checked).
- Observed: the F group (≈ 50–52 MPa) is below the authors' own 55 MPa HS threshold but is still called HSUD-EGC.
- Observed: no W/B stated; matrix fracture toughness, fibre–matrix bond and EPI not measured, so the micromechanical explanation is
  qualitative. Only one healing regime (continuous water, 28 d) and one pre-strain (3%).
- Observed: cost comparison uses literature prices from different countries and years (Table 7); production energy/CO₂ not assessed.
- Observed: the abstract pairs "87 MPa" (S-PVA, 86.5 in Fig. 3) with "10.5%" (S-PE) for the 5.5:4 binder; no single mix has both.

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: [[choi2016-pe-alkali-activated-slag-composite]] (same Chonnam group): PE fibre gives > 7% strain in cementless
  alkali-activated matrices; here > 10% with only 1.0% PE + 0.5% PVA. Healing crack-width bands (< 50 µm full, 50–150 µm partial)
  match those of [[yang2009-ecc-self-healing-wet-dry]] (tension-softening mortar), now on a strain-hardening composite.
- Agrees with: FA raises ε and lowers f'c and crack count as in cement-based HVFA ECC ([[ozbay2013-ecc-self-healing-sustained-load]];
  Wang & Li 2007 as cited) — but here FA **reduced** healing, unlike cement-based ECC where high FA/C gave better healing.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ [[homma2009-frcc-self-healing]] found fine PE fibres promote CaCO₃ healing deposits (cement FRCC); here PE-rich
  mixes healed less than PVA-rich ones (alkali-activated, C-(N)-A-S-H). ⚔️ The wiki's healing mechanism (CaCO₃ main product,
  [[self-healing]]) does not hold for this cementless matrix. ⚔️ Fly ash and healing: higher FA/C healed better in PVA-ECC
  ([[ozbay2013-ecc-self-healing-sustained-load]]) vs FA-rich EGC healed worst, with negative RF recovery (Fig. 12).
- Extends: [[hshdc]] — a cementless route to the high-strength class with ε ≈ 10.5% (performance index 8.8 MPa vs 10.0 for
  Yu 2018 UHP-ECC as cited). [[hybrid-fiber]] — first polymer–polymer (PE + PVA) hybrid with high-strength matrix and > 6% strain.
  Uses the SPI/EPI criteria of [[kanda2006-psh-design-criteria]].
