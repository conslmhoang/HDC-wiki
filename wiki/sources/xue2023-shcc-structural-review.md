# SHCC in structural applications: a review (Xue et al., 2023)

> Review. Almost all data are the corresponding author's own earlier experiments (Zhang Y.X. et al.,
> refs. 24, 25, 27, 28), re-presented **as cited**. No new tests. Specimen counts, ages and curing
> are not given.
>
> Re-checked against the publisher PDF (12 pp.) on 2026-10-21: page numbers added, beam geometry and
> capacities read from the figures (marked ≈).

## One-paragraph summary
Nanjing Forestry University authors review how SHCC behaves in members and strengthening layers
compared with material-level dogbone tests. One PE-SHCC (W/B 0.22, cement + silica fume) is used
throughout (Section 2, p. 3, as cited from ref. 24): first crack 4.2 MPa, σ_tu 7–8 MPa at 1.5–2.2% strain.
Unreinforced SHCC beams crack multiply in flexure but with fewer cracks than in uniaxial tension
(Section 3.1, p. 4, Fig. 2). Longitudinally reinforced SHCC beams without stirrups fail in sudden diagonal
shear at 9.8 MPa (a/d = 2) and 7.0 MPa (a/d = 3) nominal shear stress, with an unusually smooth
fracture surface (Section 3.2, pp. 4–6, Figs. 3–5). An SHCC layer on the tension side of RC beams raised the
flexural capacity by 16.5%, 34.8% and 61.0% for 10, 30 and 50 mm layers (Section 4.1.1, p. 7, Fig. 6, as
cited from ref. 25). Thin layers crack only near the existing RC crack ("zero-span tension"), so
the material's ductility is partly lost; thick layers show bending-driven, evenly distributed cracks
(Section 4.1.2, pp. 7–9, Figs. 7–8). A 3 mm SHCC layer on both beam sides "significantly" increased the shear
capacity of an RC beam without stirrups — ≈52 → ≈66 kN peak load, read from Fig. 9 (Section 4.2, pp. 9–10, as
cited from ref. 28; no number in the text).

## Objective / research question
- Show how SHCC structural behaviour differs from SHCC material behaviour, focusing on flexure and
  shear of SHCC members and of SHCC strengthening layers on RC (Abstract, p. 1; Section 1, p. 3).

## Test methods
All as cited from the corresponding author's earlier papers:
- **Material tension** (ref. 24): dumbbell, 13 × 30 mm section, 100 mm gauge length (p. 3, Fig. 1); 3 curves
  shown. Rate not given.
- **Unreinforced SHCC beam, flexure:** 1800 mm long, 150 × 200 mm, no reinforcement, 4PB; span 1600 mm and
  load points 300 mm apart (read from Fig. 2 drawing, p. 4); load cell 300 kN (0.1 kN), transducers 25 mm range
  (0.002 mm) (Section 3.1, p. 4).
- **SHCC beam, shear** (ref. 24): 100 mm wide, 200 mm deep, 150 mm effective depth, one 25 mm deformed bar
  (E 200 GPa, f_y 1050 MPa), no stirrups; a/d = 2 (shear span 300 mm, case 2-100; beam 900 mm incl. 150 mm
  overhangs) and 3 (450 mm, case 3-100; beam 1200 mm); shear stress = V/(b·d) (Section 3.2.1, pp. 4–5, Fig. 3).
  The Fig. 3 drawings show a single central load (three-point loading). Fracture surface roughness by laser
  displacement meter over 123 mm of a 125 mm sample, 6 lines (Section 3.2.2, p. 6, Fig. 5).
- **RC + SHCC flexural strengthening** (ref. 25): 4PB; tension-side SHCC layer 10, 30, 50 mm
  (SHCC-10/30/50) vs control RC (Section 4.1, p. 7, Fig. 6). From the Fig. 6 drawing: beam 1200 mm,
  100 × 150 mm (effective depth 130 mm), 2D10 tension bars, D6 stirrups in the shear spans, span 1000 mm,
  shear spans 300 mm, constant-moment zone 400 mm.
- **RC + SHCC shear strengthening** (ref. 28): 1200 mm, 100 × 200 mm, 2 × 10 mm bars, no web
  reinforcement, a/d = 3; SHCC-3 = 3 mm layer on both sides, substrate roughened by retarder wash-out (Section 4.2,
  p. 9, Fig. 9). Fig. 9 drawing: d = 165 mm, shear span 500 mm, span 1000 mm, single central load; the text calls
  it a four-point bending setup.
- **Curing & test age:** not reported for any test (confirmed in PDF).

Main findings (with location):
- **Unreinforced SHCC beam in flexure:** multiple cracking then localisation; fewer cracks than in
  uniaxial tension (Section 3.1, p. 4, Fig. 2).
- **SHCC beam in shear:** flexural cracks first, then independent shear-span cracks that dominate;
  localisation then a **sudden** load drop; nearly linear response up to peak (Section 3.2.1, p. 5; Conclusion 1, p. 10).
- **Fracture surface:** smooth except a collapsed coarse zone near the load point, unlike rough normal
  concrete; serrated roughness profile, larger in the coarse area (Section 3.2.2, pp. 5–6, Figs. 4–5).
- **Flexural strengthening:** initial stiffness rises with layer thickness; after rebar yield the SHCC layer
  carries load by multiple cracking, then localises; ends with debonding (SHCC-10, SHCC-30) (Section 4.1, pp. 7–8).
- **Two crack modes in the layer** (Section 4.1.2, pp. 7–9, Figs. 7–8):
  - **zero-span tension:** cracks concentrated around the existing RC crack, spreading from the top
    (interface) of the layer downward; dominant in the 10 mm layer;
  - **uniaxial (bending) tension:** evenly distributed cracks spreading from the layer bottom upward;
    dominant in the 50 mm layer; both present at 30 mm.
- **Shear strengthening:** fewer diagonal cracks in the layer than in the shear-failed SHCC member → the
  zero-span effect also reduces ductility in shear strengthening (Section 4.2, p. 9; Conclusion 4, p. 11).
- The authors recommend the **zero-span tensile test** (a pair of steel plates forming a zero-length span)
  for thin SHCC strengthening layers (refs. 25–27, p. 9, Fig. 8b).

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Member behaviour differs from material behaviour because fibre distribution in large members differs
  from small specimens (refs. 22–23, Section 1, p. 3).
- Sudden shear failure "may be due to" the smooth crack surface (Section 3.2.1, p. 5).
- Shear-strengthening gain attributed to shear transfer on the layer's crack surface by contact stress
  plus fibre bridging (Section 4.2, p. 9).
- Stress concentration at the localized RC crack limits multiple cracking in thin layers (Section 4.1.2, pp. 7–8).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Stated: none explicitly.
- Observed:
  - Narrow scope: essentially a summary of one group's work (Zhang Y.X.), not a systematic review;
  - fibre length, diameter, vol.%, f'c, curing, ages, loading rates and specimen counts are absent;
  - shear-strengthening gain and unreinforced member capacity are given only in figures;
  - text calls the Fig. 9 test four-point bending, but the drawing (and Fig. 3) shows a single central load;
  - text strain/stress at B2 (2.2%, 8 MPa) does not match the B2 marker in Fig. 1 (≈2.6%, ≈7.7 MPa);
  - Table 1 reference numbering (pp. 2–3) is inconsistent with the reference list: "Li et al. (2018) [12]"
    (super-ductile FRCC repair, USA) vs ref. 12 = Bartosz et al. 2018 (curaua SHCC); "Bartosz et al. (2011) [13]"
    vs ref. 13 = Li, Horii, Kabele & Lim, EFM vol. 65 (listed as 2011) — refs. 12 and 13 appear swapped;
    ref. 23 gives J. Eng. Mech. volume "188" for Li & Leung 1992 (should be 118 per DOI).
  - Strain capacity 1.5–2.2% is below the usual "> 3%" ECC convention.
