# Steel fiber and residual properties of HS PVA-ECC after 200–800 °C (Zhou et al., 2023)

## One-paragraph summary
A Guangzhou University group (with U. Queensland) heated a fly-ash "high strength ECC" (W/B 0.20,
FA/C 1.2) with 2.0 vol.% fiber in three combinations: 2.0% PVA (PO), 1.5% PVA + 0.5% steel (PS-I) and
1.0% PVA + 1.0% steel (PS-II) (Table 3). Specimens were heated in a muffle furnace at 20 °C/min to
200, 400, 600 or 800 °C, held 1 h, cooled to room temperature and tested at 28 d (Section 2.3, Fig. 5).
Reported outcomes: colour change, spalling, surface crack width (video microscope × 200), mass loss,
DTG, residual cube compressive strength, prism flexural strength and peak deflection, SEM and XRD
(Table 5). TG of the bare fibers shows **PVA starts to melt at 230 °C**, with thermal instability between
220 and 420 °C; steel fiber mass is almost unaffected (Fig. 4, p. 750). PVA alone prevented spalling at
every temperature; steel fiber **caused slight spalling at 800 °C** and wider surface cracks (up to
271.1 µm), but it raised residual compressive and flexural strength, especially at 1.0 vol.% steel
above 400 °C (Table 5). The PO flexural strength fell from 15.8 MPa (30 °C) to 5.9 MPa (400 °C) and
2.1 MPa (800 °C) as the PVA melted.

> Synthesis: this is the wiki's **first primary fire study on PVA-ECC**. It confirms the second-hand
> PVA melting range (about 230 °C) and shows PVA-only ECC flexural capacity collapses between 200 and
> 400 °C. But the paper has no tension test and the flexural curves are softening at 30 °C, so the
> results describe a fiber-reinforced high-strength mortar labelled "ECC", not proven strain-hardening
> behaviour.

## Objective / research question
- Can partial replacement of PVA by steel fiber (25% and 50% by volume) retain the residual mechanical
  properties and ductility of HSECC after elevated temperature (Section 1, p. 749)?
- How does steel fiber content change spalling, surface cracking, mass loss and fiber bridging
  (SEM) at 200–800 °C?
- The authors state that research on the mass change of HSECC is scarce and that the micro-mechanism of
  hybrid-fiber HSECC under elevated temperature is unclear (p. 749).

## Materials & mix design
Constituents (Section 2.1, Tables 1–2):
- **Cement:** P.O. 42.5 (stated to comply with ASTM C150). CaO 57.40%, SiO₂ 21.91%.
- **Fly ash:** Class F, Grade I, apparent density 2.42 g/cm³; SiO₂ 54.21%, Al₂O₃ 31.55%.
- **Sand:** quartz sand, fineness modulus 1.7, apparent density 2.645 g/cm³; maximum size not stated
  (PSD in Fig. 2 reaches about 1 mm ⚠️ read from figure).
- **SP:** polycarboxylate, solid content 39.51%, water-reduction 46%, density 1.074 g/cm³.
- **PVA fiber (Kuraray):** ρ 1300 kg/m³, L 12 mm, d **0.015 mm (15 µm)** ⚠️ as printed, σ_f 1780 MPa,
  E 39 GPa, elongation 7.0% (Table 2). Oil coating not stated.
- **Steel fiber (Zhitai, copper-coated):** ρ 7800 kg/m³, L 13 mm, d 220 µm, σ_f 2800 MPa, E 200 GPa,
  elongation 20.0% (Table 2).

## Test methods
- **Specimen geometry:** 100 mm cubes (compression); 40 × 40 × 160 mm prisms (flexure); n = 3 per group
  and temperature (Section 2.2). Flexural span and loading arrangement not stated.
- **Heating regime:** muffle furnace; 20 °C/min to 200 / 400 / 600 / 800 °C; 1 h hold; cooling to room
  temperature (cooling method and rate not stated); unheated control at "30 °C" (Section 2.3, Fig. 5).
  This is a **residual (post-cooling) test**, not an in-situ hot test.
- **Measurement:** RH-2000 3D video microscope (up to × 400; crack images at × 200); TGA4000 on core
  powder; JSM-7001F FE-SEM with EDS; XRD 2θ 5–80° (Section 2.4). Mass loss measured three times.
- **Curing & test age:** tests "at the age of 28 days" after heating and cooling (Section 2.3). The age at
  heating is not stated ⚠️.
- **No uniaxial tension test.** Ductility is judged from f_f/f_c and peak-load deflection.

Main findings (with location):
- **Fiber thermal behaviour** (Figs. 3–4, p. 750): PVA turns black between 200 and 400 °C and is gone at
  600 °C; TG shows PVA melting from 230 °C, instability 220–420 °C. Elsewhere the paper cites PVA melting
  at "about 235 °C" (Liu & Tan 2018, p. 752) and "about 250 °C" (Deshpande 2019, p. 749).
- **SEM** (Section 3.6, Fig. 11): PVA diameter shrinks at 200 °C (softening); at 400 °C PVA has melted
  completely, leaving channels up to 21.5 µm wide.
- **Spalling** (Section 3.2, Table 5): none in PO at any temperature; slight spalling at 800 °C in both
  PS mixes; authors attribute it to fewer PVA melt channels for vapour release.
- **Residual compressive strength** (Section 3.4, Fig. 9): f_c,T/f_ck for PO 87.5 / 101.3 / 76.7 / 50.3%
  at 200 / 400 / 600 / 800 °C; PS-I 115.6 / 98.3 / 77.6 / 43.5%; PS-II 91.3 / 105.9 / 72.9 / "0.36%" as
  printed (41.6% computed here from Table 5 ⚠️). All mixes drop sharply above 600 °C.
- **Flexure** (Section 3.5, Fig. 10, Table 4): f_f/f_c for PO 0.19 / 0.18 / 0.07 / 0.05 / 0.05 at
  30–800 °C; PS-I 0.19 / 0.18 / 0.16 / 0.11 / 0.12; PS-II 0.17 / 0.18 / 0.14 / 0.16 / 0.17. PO loses most
  flexural capacity between 200 and 400 °C (15.8 → 13.1 → 5.9 MPa). PS-II at 400 °C has about twice the
  flexural strength of PS-I.
- **Load–deflection shape** (Fig. 10, read from figure ⚠️): all curves peak at about 0.3–0.7 mm and then
  soften; no deflection hardening is visible at any temperature, including 30 °C.
- **Mass loss** (Section 3.3): PO < PS at every temperature; among PS mixes, more steel gives less mass loss.

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Below 200 °C mass loss is water evaporation; C-S-H decomposes 200–600 °C; Ca(OH)₂ dehydroxylation
  450–550 °C; CaCO₃ decarbonation about 700 °C (DTG, Fig. 8, Section 3.3).
- Strength recovery at 400 °C (PO, PS-II) is attributed to further hydration of unhydrated cement and
  pozzolanic FA reaction at moderate heat (p. 754).
- PVA melt channels relieve vapour pressure ("decompression effect"); replacing PVA with steel reduces the
  channels and raises spalling (p. 752).
- Steel fiber–matrix ITZ is weak at 30–200 °C (smooth copper-coated surface); at 400 °C hydration and
  oxidation products attach to the steel and strengthen the ITZ; at about 750–800 °C an oxide film makes
  the steel surface smooth, so it pulls out and bridging is almost lost (Section 3.6, Fig. 12).
- Above 600 °C, Ca(OH)₂ decomposition coarsens pores, and steel oxidation accelerates deterioration (p. 755).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- **Observed:** no uniaxial tension or crack-pattern test under load; "ECC" status and strain-hardening
  are not demonstrated, and Fig. 10 curves are softening at 30 °C. ([[strain-hardening]] is not shown.)
- **Observed:** n = 3; no scatter or standard deviation reported in Table 5.
- **Observed:** flexural span, loading arrangement and age at heating not stated; cooling regime not stated.
- **Observed:** PVA diameter 15 µm is unusual for a 12 mm Kuraray fiber (typical ECC grade 39–40 µm) ⚠️.
- **Observed:** inconsistencies in text — PS-II 800 °C ratio "0.36%"; a sentence that compares "PS-I with
  PS-I" (p. 752, probably PS-II); PS-I crack width at 600 °C ("around 90") is smaller than at 400 °C (213).
- **Observed:** "ductility" is inferred from f_f/f_c and peak deflection (0.3–0.7 mm), not from energy or
  post-peak behaviour.
- Single heating rate (20 °C/min) and 1 h hold; no in-situ hot testing.

## Relation to other work
- **Agrees with:** PVA melting near 230 °C and loss of PVA-ECC capacity between 200 and 400 °C, as cited
  second-hand in [[high-temperature-behaviour]] (Bhat 2014: brittle at 300 °C) and [[ma2021-ecc-durability-review]].
  PVA fibers prevent explosive spalling (Liu & Tan 2018, as cited p. 752), in line with Sahmaran 2011 (as cited in the wiki).
- **Contradicts:** ⚔️ none directly. The PVA melting "about 230–273 °C" range in the wiki is narrowed:
  this primary TG shows melting onset at 230 °C (Fig. 4).
- **Extends:** first primary residual data at 200–800 °C for PVA-ECC and PVA/steel hybrids in the wiki;
  steel fiber raises spalling risk but keeps residual strength. Same group's earlier work on unoiled PVA
  (Wang et al. 2020, ref.) — oiling of the PVA here is not stated.
