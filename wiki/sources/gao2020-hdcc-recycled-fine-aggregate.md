# Recycled fine aggregate in PVA-HDCC (Gao et al., 2020)

> Re-checked against the publisher PDF (18 pp.) on 2026-10-21: page numbers added; compressive and
> 3-pt flexural strengths, dogbone geometry, particle-size distribution and Eq. 5–6 data points read
> from the figures; every tabulated number confirmed.

## One-paragraph summary
Zhengzhou University (Gao Danying group) (p. 1) replaced river sand **fully (100%) with recycled fine aggregate (RFA)**
from crushed waste concrete, sieved with a single 1.18 mm screen (no Fuller-curve regrading), in a PVA-fiber
HDCC with 0–2 vol.% fiber (§1, p. 2; §2.1, p. 3). RFA is finer, rougher and more absorptive than river sand (water absorption
6.72% vs 1.18%; 4.86× more crush dust < 0.075 mm) (Table 4, §3.1, p. 7). The authors find a **denser matrix and a
stronger fiber/matrix bond** with RFA, giving higher compressive, flexural, bending and tensile strengths, and —
counter to the usual ECC rule that lower bond favours ductility — **more multiple cracking and higher ductility**:
at 2% fiber, tensile peak strain 4.76% vs 1.68% (+183%) and four-point bending peak deflection +183% at 1% fiber
(Table 5, p. 11; Table 6, p. 13). Only R-HDCC with 1.5–2.0% fiber showed evident tensile strain hardening. Linear models relate
σ_bp/f_c and σ_tp/f_c to Vf (Eqs. 5–7, p. 15).

> Synthesis: a **third Chinese HDCC lineage** in the wiki (Zhengzhou), distinct from Southeast Univ. / Taiyuan
> ([[chai2023-hdc-ambient-temperature]], [[chai2019-hdcc-freeze-thaw-carbonation-flexure]]) and the Xi'an groups.
> Note that the tensile series uses **W/B 0.45 without superplasticizer** and reaches only σ_tp 1.1–2.8 MPa — far
> weaker than typical PVA-ECC (≈ 4–5 MPa). The bending/compression series uses W/B 0.25 (f_c ≈ 71–85 MPa).
> Tension and bending results therefore come from **different matrices** and should not be combined.

## Objective / research question
- Can RFA with its original grading (single 1.18 mm screen) fully replace river sand in HDCC while keeping good
  bending and tensile behaviour (§1, p. 2)?
- How do RFA and NFA differ physically, mineralogically and in their ITZ, and how does Vf (0–2%) interact (§1, §3)?
- Stated gap: bending and tension of HDCC with **100% RFA** "has not been studied in detail" (§1, p. 2).

## Materials & mix design
Constituents (§2.1, p. 3; Tables 1–2, p. 3; Table 4, p. 7):
- **Cement:** ordinary Portland cement 42.5R (CaO 63.42%, SiO₂ 18.77%).
- **Fly ash:** Grade I (Hejin power plant, Shanxi); CaO 4.2%, SiO₂ 54.45%, Al₂O₃ 28.37% (Table 1).
- **RFA:** waste concrete → jaw crusher → hammer crusher → sieve ≤ 1.18 mm. Apparent density 2536 kg/m³, bulk
  1309 kg/m³, water absorption 6.72%, crushing index 18.3% (Table 4). Contains SiO₂, CaCO₃, dolomite (XRD, Fig. 8, p. 8);
  heterogeneous (old paste + natural sand + coarse-aggregate fragments, CT Fig. 7, p. 8).
- **NFA:** river sand ≤ 1.18 mm; apparent 2593 kg/m³, bulk 1460 kg/m³, absorption 1.18%, crushing index 12.8% (Table 4).
- **Particle-size distribution** (Fig. 6, p. 7, bar labels), % by mass in 0–0.075 / 0.075–0.15 / 0.15–0.3 / 0.3–0.6 / 0.6–1.18 mm:
  RFA 1.8 / 20.94 / 23.31 / 20.2 / 33.75; NFA 0.37 / 4.43 / 13.75 / 29.62 / 51.83.
- **SP:** polycarboxylate water reducer (Subote).
- **Fiber:** PVA, L 12 mm, d 40 µm, σ_f ≥ 1250 MPa, E > 30 GPa, elongation ≥ 6%, density "1.3 g/m³" (typo for
  g/cm³; 26 kg/m³ ↔ 2.0 vol.% is consistent) (Table 2, p. 3). Manufacturer and oil coating: not reported.

Units of binder quantities: kg/m³ (Table 3, p. 4). Water 297 (W/B 0.25) and 403 (W/B 0.45) kg/m³.
Ratios (Table 3, checked here by mass): aggregate/binder R/B = 0.4; FA = 30% of binder → FA/C = 0.43 (computed).
The 25-series is used for compression, 3-pt flexure and 4-pt bending; the 45-series **only** for tension (§2.2, p. 3).

- Mixing (§2.2, p. 3): dry solids ≈ 2 min → water + SP slowly → fibers 2 min → high-speed mixing → 30 s vibration.
- Curing: 24 h in the mould at 20 °C, RH ≥ 98%, then water at 20 °C to 28 d (§2.2, p. 3).

## Test methods
- **Compression / 3-pt flexure:** 40 × 40 × 160 mm prisms, 100 mm span, 50 N/s; compression on the halves
  (40 × 40 mm platen), 2.4 kN/s; GB/T 17671-1999; 300 kN machine; n = 3 (§2.3.1, p. 4). Results only in Fig. 9 (p. 9, bar labels; no table).
- **4-pt bending:** thin plates 400 × 100 × 15 mm, span 300 mm, third-point loading (loads 100 mm apart, 50 mm overhangs,
  Fig. 3a, p. 5), 0.1 mm/min, two mid-span LVDTs; 50 kN machine;
  σ = PL/(bd²) per ASTM C1018 / C78; n = 3 (§2.3.2, p. 5). σ_bc = onset of non-linearity, σ_bp = peak, δ_bu = maximum
  deflection, G_b = area under the **whole** load–deflection curve (unit not stated ⚠️).
- **Uniaxial tension:** dogbones (Fig. 1, p. 4): total length 160 mm, end width 80 mm, thickness 40 mm; central prismatic
  section 40 × 40 mm over **50 mm** length, with 40 mm curved transitions and 15 mm grip ends. MTS,
  0.001 mm/s; four clip extensometers, longitudinal gauge 50 mm, transverse 25 mm (§2.3.3, p. 6; Eqs. 2–4). σ_tc = first stress drop,
  σ_tp / ε_tp = peak, **ε_tu = maximum strain (post-peak, not at peak)**, G_t = area under the whole curve (unit not stated ⚠️),
  ν_t = Poisson's ratio. Only specimens failing in the gauge length without eccentricity kept; ≥ 2 valid, deviation < 30% (p. 6).
- **Microstructure:** XRD (PANalytical X'Pert3, aggregates), X-ray CT (Zeiss Xradia 410 Versa; aggregates, post-test dogbones),
  SEM (Zeiss EVO HD15; ITZ and pulled-out fibers) (§2.3.4, pp. 6–7).
- **Curing & test age:** 28 d (water curing).

Main findings (with Table/Fig. location):
- **RFA properties:** 0.075–0.15 and 0.15–0.3 mm fractions 20.94% and 23.31% (372.69% and 69.53% above NFA);
  4.86× crush dust; bulk density −10.34%; absorption 5.69× and crushing index 1.43× those of NFA (§3.1, Fig. 6, Table 4, p. 7).
- **Compression (40 mm cube halves of prisms, W/B 0.25):** R-HDCC 83.2–84.9 MPa vs N-HDCC 70.9–78.0 MPa, i.e. 6.67–18.45% higher
  across Vf; Vf has little effect on f_c (Fig. 9a, p. 9; §3.2, p. 8).
- **3-pt flexure:** R-HDCC 9.4 → 34.3 MPa and N-HDCC 9.2 → 28.0 MPa from 0 to 2% fiber; R-HDCC 2.17–22.5% higher;
  gains of 264.89% (R) vs 204.35% (N) (§3.2, p. 9; Fig. 9b). These 3-pt values on 40 mm prisms are ≈ 3× the 4-pt σ_bp of the
  15 mm plates (Table 5) — size/test-set-up effect (observed here).
- **4-pt bending:** plain mortar R vs N: σ_bc +27.1%, deflection −10% (p. 9). At 0.5% fiber, no deflection hardening (fibers ruptured; bridging < cracking strength), and R-HDCC
  is *less* deformable than N-HDCC (δ_bu 1.76 vs 5.30 mm). At ≥ 1% fiber, R-HDCC shows multiple cracking and clear
  deflection hardening; N-HDCC less so (§3.3, pp. 9–11; Table 5; Figs. 10–11, p. 10).
- **Tension:** at 0.5–1.0% both mixes soften after a few fluctuations; at 1.5–2.0% only R-HDCC strain-hardens with
  multiple cracking (Figs. 12–14, pp. 12–13). From 0.5 to 2.0% fiber, R-HDCC σ_tp +88.08%, ε_tp +260.60%, ε_tu +130.91%,
  E_t +58.74%, G_t +824.84% (§3.4, p. 11). ν_t 0.1–0.3 with no trend (p. 12).
- **CT:** R-HDCC voids smaller and narrower in size distribution (Fig. 14, p. 13).
- **SEM:** tight RFA/paste ITZ vs a visible gap at NFA; fibers pulled from R-HDCC are rough, coated with hydrates and
  stripped of surface filaments; those from N-HDCC smooth (Fig. 15, p. 13; Fig. 16, p. 14).
- **Models** (R-HDCC; Vf in %, Figs. 18–19 axis "V_f (%)"): σ_bp/f_c = 0.036 Vf + 0.069 (R² 0.948); σ_tp/f_c = 0.025 Vf + 0.026
  (R² 0.967); σ_tp/f_c = 0.668 σ_bp/f_c − 0.02 (R² 0.966) (Eqs. 5–7, p. 15; Figs. 18–20, pp. 15–16).
  - Fig. 18 data points (≈ 0.074, 0.078, 0.110, 0.124, 0.142; read from Fig. 18) equal σ_bc or σ_bp (Table 5) divided by the
    **W/B 0.25** f_c of Fig. 9a (computed here; at Vf = 0 σ_bc is used).
  - Fig. 19 data points (≈ 0.027, 0.035, 0.052, 0.067, 0.073; read from Fig. 19) imply f_c ≈ 39–43 MPa when divided into
    σ_tp (Table 6) (computed here). This is **not** the 71–85 MPa of Fig. 9a, so Eq. 6 must use an unreported compressive
    strength of the W/B 0.45 tension series ⚠️.

## Mechanisms / explanations offered by authors
- Denser matrix with RFA from: reactive old-paste crumbs and crush dust; better particle packing/filling of the ITZ;
  finer, rougher particles; absorption of surface water by RFA → thinner water film and tighter ITZ (§3.2, p. 8).
- Higher fiber/matrix bond → higher bridging stress and more multiple cracking under SH conditions; lower elastic
  modulus of RFA adds deformation; finer RFA means less crack detour, promoting multiple cracking via "crack trapping"
  (§3.3, p. 11, citing ref. 23 ⚠️).
- At 0.5% fiber the higher bond makes fibers rupture more easily, so RFA lowers ductility at low Vf (§3.3, p. 9).

## Limitations (stated + observed)
- Observed: tension (W/B 0.45, no SP) and bending/compression (W/B 0.25) use **different matrices**; f_c of the
  tension series is not reported, and Eq. 6 back-calculates to f_c ≈ 39–43 MPa (Fig. 19, computed here) ⚠️.
- Observed: **ε_tu here is the maximum (post-peak) strain** over a 50 mm gauge (up to 11.73%), which includes
  localisation; the wiki's tensile strain capacity is ε_tp (≤ 4.76%).
- Observed: very low tensile strengths (σ_tc 0.98–2.38 MPa, σ_tp ≤ 2.84 MPa) compared with PVA-ECC.
- Observed: crack width and crack number not measured; strain hardening judged from curves and CT only.
- Observed: no interface test (pull-out) — "higher bond" is inferred from SEM and strength.
- Observed: three percentage statements do not match the tables: σ_tp 45R15 vs 45N15 "34.82% higher" (p. 12; Table 6 gives
  31.2%); G_b 25R20 vs 25N20 "95.22% higher" (p. 11; Table 5 gives 86.8%); E_t 45R00 vs 45N00 "80.12% higher" (p. 11;
  Table 6 gives 77.0%) ⚠️. All other quoted percentages check out (computed here).
- Observed: citation numbering slips (Lederer cited as [23] in §3.2, p. 8, but listed as ref. 19; ref. 23 is Li 1992 on
  compressive strength, also cited for "crack trapping"; the ref. 19 author list is a garbled nuclear-physics author list, p. 17) ⚠️.
- Observed: G_b and G_t units missing; durability not tested.
- Stated: Fuller-curve treatment (Li & Yang 2017, as cited) needs multiple screens and more crushing; this study avoids it (§1, p. 2).

## Relation to other work
- Agrees with: Li & Yang 2017 (as cited, §1, p. 2): ECC with recycled concrete fines reaches "several percent" tensile strain.
- Contradicts: ⚔️ The ECC design rule that **lower** bond raises ductility ([[li2002-pva-ecc-interface-tailoring]]:
  oil coating cuts τ0/Gd, ε 0.99 → 4.88%; Li & Leung 1992 as invoked in [[chai2019-hdcc-freeze-thaw-carbonation-flexure]],
  p. 127). Here a **higher** bond (inferred) coincides with **more** ductility at Vf ≥ 1%, while at 0.5% the same
  higher bond reduces ductility. Consistent with an optimum-bond view only if N-HDCC's bond is below the optimum
  (not measured). See [[fiber-matrix-interface]].
- Extends: [[fiber-geometry]] (Vf 0–2% series; strain hardening needs ≥ 1.5% here).
- Contrast: [[chai2023-hdc-ambient-temperature]] and [[chai2019-hdcc-freeze-thaw-carbonation-flexure]] use the same
  class of domestic 12 mm / ≈ 40 µm / ≥ 1250 MPa PVA at 26 kg/m³ (2 vol.%) with river sand ≤ 1.18 mm. Chai 2023 at
  W/B 0.30 and FA/C 0.88 reached ε_tu 1.15% at 4.23 MPa; Gao's N-HDCC at W/B 0.45 reached ε_tp 1.68% at 2.75 MPa.
